Wave 70 Hits 5 Bases: Qeshm Fortress Blocks Hormuz

July 29, 2026 · 8 min read
Khalid Naami
Founder, Owner, and CEO at Dashboard Options

Iran's Wave 70 escalation struck five U.S. military bases simultaneously across the Gulf using Qiam and Emad ballistic missiles alongside Shahed attack drones. A Tasnim-sourced IRGC statement warned that any attempt to seize Iran's islands would result in the destruction of Dubai and Abu Dhabi by name, while Iran mobilized one million troops and declared readiness for a full ground war. As the Wall Street Journal reported that U.S. Marines are studying a seizure of Kharg Island, the 1,445-square-kilometer fortress of Qeshm, with its underground missile cities and tunnel networks, stands as the unsinkable barrier that could turn any American amphibious operation into a catastrophe.

Wave 70: Five U.S. Bases Under Simultaneous Fire​

Iran's seventieth wave of retaliatory strikes targeted five American military installations across the region simultaneously:

  1. Prince Sultan Air Base (Al Kharj), Saudi Arabia
  2. Al Dhafra Air Base, UAE
  3. Ali Al Salem Air Base, Kuwait
  4. Erbil Air Base, Iraqi Kurdistan
  5. U.S. Fifth Fleet Headquarters, Bahrain

The strikes employed a combination of Qiam short-range and Emad medium-range ballistic missiles, supplemented by Shahed-series attack drones on offensive trajectories. The IRGC declared in a joint statement with the regular armed forces that its strategy is now fully offensive, and that any source of aggression against Iranian territory and sovereignty will be met with greater force.

The Tasnim Warning: Dubai and Abu Dhabi Named as Targets​

The most explosive development came from a Tasnim News Agency report quoting a senior IRGC source who, following the assassination of the IRGC spokesperson, issued a direct threat to the UAE:

  • Named cities: Dubai and Abu Dhabi were explicitly identified as primary targets if the U.S. attempts to seize Iranian islands.
  • Regional war declared: The source stated that a regional war has already erupted and that all energy infrastructure in the region has been disrupted.
  • Verbatim warning: "If Trump commits any foolishness in this regard, we will create a surprise that will leave him unable to even extract his soldiers' coffins from our land."

The threat is directly linked to the islands dispute. The Greater Tunb, Lesser Tunb, and Abu Musa islands, controlled by Iran but claimed by the UAE, sit at the entrance to the Strait of Hormuz. Any American operation to seize Kharg Island would inevitably extend to these islands, triggering Iran's declared response: the destruction of the UAE's two largest cities and economic centers.

One Million Troops: Iran Mobilizes for Ground War​

Iran's armed forces have mobilized one million personnel, a historic figure that places the country on a full war footing. The mobilization encompasses both the regular army (Artesh) and the IRGC ground forces, unified under the war coordination of Hossein Dehghan.

Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei warned that any further American attack would lead to a comprehensive ground invasion and direct battles inside "other territories," a veiled reference to offensive operations beyond Iran's borders. The statement signals that Iran views the conflict as existential and is prepared to absorb a ground campaign while launching counter-invasions.

Iran's Geographic Fortress: A Thousand Doors In, No Door Out​

Iran's terrain constitutes a natural defensive fortress unmatched by any potential U.S. adversary:

  • Average elevation: 1,200 meters above sea level
  • Mountain coverage: Two major mountain chains (Zagros and Alborz) covering approximately 55% of the country's landmass
  • Mount Damavand: At 5,671 meters, the highest peak in Western Asia, anchoring the northern defensive wall
  • Central deserts: The Dasht-e Kavir (salt desert) and Dasht-e Lut (sand desert), among the hottest and most arid regions on Earth, create natural barriers against mechanized advance

These natural walls surround the central plateaus, making any blitzkrieg-style ground operation impossible. The terrain channels invading forces into predictable corridors where pre-positioned defenses can concentrate fire. As Iranian strategists state: "Iran's land has a thousand doors for entry and no door for exit."

Qeshm Island: The Unsinkable Aircraft Carrier​

Qeshm Island, covering 1,445 square kilometers in the Strait of Hormuz, functions as Iran's unsinkable stationary aircraft carrier and the cornerstone of its coastal defense:

  • Underground missile cities: Massive tunnel networks house anti-ship missile batteries, ballistic launchers, and command centers beneath the island's geological formations.
  • Naval suicide drones: Explosive-laden unmanned surface vessels are staged for mass swarm attacks against large warships.
  • Mine warfare: Dense minefields protect the approaches to the island and the strait.
  • Anti-ship missiles: Multiple types of coastal defense cruise missiles are positioned to engage any vessel transiting within range.
  • Natural concealment: The island's salt dome formations, branching valleys, and geological ridges provide natural camouflage that degrades the effectiveness of conventional air strikes and complicates amphibious landing operations.

The U.S. struck Qeshm's freshwater desalination plant, cutting water supplies to 30 villages, an act Iran described as a "flagrant and desperate crime." This strike, however, did not degrade Qeshm's military function; it intensified Iran's resolve to defend the island as both a military bastion and a symbol of sovereign resistance.

The WSJ Report: Marines Study Kharg Island Seizure​

The Wall Street Journal, citing Barak Ravid and Mark Caputo of Axios, reported that U.S. Marine Corps planners are studying a seizure of Kharg Island, located 15 miles off the Iranian coast, through which 90% of Iran's crude oil exports are processed.

The operation would place American forces directly on the Iranian firing line. Kharg is a geological citadel that was once an open museum before becoming an oil terminal. Any landing force would face:

  • Pre-positioned coastal defense missiles from Qeshm, Larak, and the Iranian mainland
  • Submarine and mine threats in the shallow approaches
  • Counter-battery fire from IRGC rocket artillery positioned in the Zagros foothills
  • Swarm attacks from fast-attack craft and naval drones

The militarization of the Strait of Hormuz through an American occupation of Gulf islands would create a permanent conflict zone, far more dangerous than Trump's advisors anticipate.

Israel's Claimed Destruction: The Numbers Don't Add​

The Israeli military claims to have destroyed 85% of Iran's surface-to-air missile inventory and 75% of its missile launch platforms. These figures are contradicted by operational reality:

  • Iran is launching 20 missiles per day against Israel, double the previous average.
  • 70% of warheads are now cluster munitions with larger individual submunitions.
  • 400 launch operations have been conducted since the war began.
  • The IRGC and armed forces issued a joint statement, an unprecedented move signaling unified command readiness.

If 85% of air defenses and 75% of launch platforms were truly destroyed, Iran could not sustain this launch tempo. The discrepancy suggests that either the Israeli estimates are inflated, or that Iran's redundant systems and underground production facilities have compensated for losses faster than anticipated.

China and Russia Declare Military Support​

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi confirmed that Iran has active military cooperation with both Russia and China, stating: "We have had close cooperation for a long time, and it continues, including military cooperation."

This confirmation transforms the conflict from a bilateral U.S.-Iran confrontation into a multipolar crisis:

  • Russia: Has declared that Caspian strikes damage its interests directly and is openly discussing the transfer of carrier-killing weapons to Iran.
  • China: Has signaled strategic support, particularly as the conflict validates Beijing's thesis that U.S. stealth technology and carrier-based power projection have critical vulnerabilities.
  • Iran's new weapons: Tehran announced that new weapons systems will be used for the first time, without specifying their nature, echoing Putin's own ambiguous weapons declarations.

The UAE's Existential Dilemma​

The UAE faces a unique vulnerability in this conflict. Unlike Saudi Arabia or Kuwait, the UAE has a direct territorial dispute with Iran over the three islands. Any U.S. operation targeting Abu Musa or the Tunbs would automatically implicate the UAE, giving Iran legal and strategic justification to strike Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

The IRGC source specified that the Maktoum and bin Zayed ruling families are the direct targets, and that the remaining emirates would be spared. This precision in the threat is designed to split the UAE federation internally, creating pressure from Sharjah, Ajman, and the northern emirates against Abu Dhabi's war posture.

Dubai's glass-tower economy and Abu Dhabi's concentrated wealth infrastructure are catastrophically vulnerable to ballistic missile strikes. A single wave of Emad missiles targeting the Burj Khalifa district, Jebel Ali port, or ADNOC headquarters would produce economic damage measured in hundreds of billions of dollars.

Conclusion: The Thousand Doors and No Exit​

Iran's geographic fortress, its mobilization of one million troops, the underground missile cities of Qeshm, and the explicit threat to destroy Dubai and Abu Dhabi create a deterrent architecture that no amphibious operation can cheaply overcome. The Wall Street Journal's report on Marine Corps planning for Kharg Island confirms that Washington is considering the maximum escalation scenario, but every military analyst who has studied Iran's coastal defenses reaches the same conclusion: entry is possible, but exit is not.

The war has reached the point where the cost of continuation is measured not in missiles and sorties, but in the potential destruction of entire cities and the collapse of the Gulf economic model. Iran's message is clear: any attempt to seize its islands will turn the Persian Gulf into a graveyard for both navies and economies.
