# Yahya Saree: Houthi Strike on Yanbu

Yemeni military spokesman Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree has issued an official statement claiming responsibility for drone strikes targeting Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline. The statement follows Saudi claims that the drones originated from Iraq, which observers characterize as an attempt to drag Iran and its Iraqi allies into a direct confrontation. Yahya Saree confirmed the strikes targeted sensitive nodes along the transit route to Yanbu.

## Saree's Declaration: Claiming the Strike on the East-West Pipeline

The Yemeni military command rejected Saudi claims regarding the origin of the strike:
*   **The Claim**: Saree confirmed Yemeni drones executed the operation directly.
*   **The Target**: Critical transit nodes along the pipeline from eastern provinces to Yanbu.
*   **The Warning**: The command stated that operations would continue until the blockade is lifted.

---

## Saudi Accusations: Blaming Iraq to Drag in External Actors

The Saudi statement sought to shift the source of the strikes to Iraqi territory:
*   **The Claim**: Saudi officials asserted that drones were launched from southern Iraq.
*   **Political Goal**: Riyadh aimed to draw U.S. and Israeli forces into direct action against Iran.
*   **Target Profile**: Blaming Iraq matches efforts to limit the influence of local mobilizations.

---

## Houthi Sovereignty: Keeping the Conflict Bilateral and Focused

Yemeni planners insisted on taking responsibility to focus on their demands:
*   **Bilateral Focus**: Houthi officials framed the conflict as a direct Saudi-Yemeni dispute.
*   **Blockade Link**: The strikes are directly linked to the closure of Sana'a and Al-Hudaydah.
*   **Sovereign Right**: The statement emphasized the right to respond to airspace violations.

---

## Intelligence Failure: Saudi Assumptions Challenged in the Field

The discrepancy in identifying the launch origin shows gaps in Saudi intelligence:
*   **Gaps Identified**: Air defense networks failed to track the flight paths of the drones.
*   **Tracking Deficit**: Radar arrays could not distinguish the launch coordinates in real time.
*   **Attribution Error**: Blaming Iraq highlights the fragility of Riyadh's surveillance systems.

---

## Flight Suspensions: Jeddah and Riyadh Airports Divert Traffic

The drone arrivals forced the suspension of civilian flights in major cities:
*   **Airport Freeze**: Airfields in Jeddah and Riyadh suspended departures for several hours.
*   **Diverted Routes**: Inbound flights were diverted to Bahrain and Oman.
*   **Public Impact**: The suspensions confirmed the reach of Houthi strike systems.

---

## Red Sea Loading Drop: Yanbu Shipments Decline Rapidly

The strikes have intensified the drop in Saudi Arabia's western oil exports:
*   **Export Drop**: Crude loadings at Yanbu declined by 40 percent since early July.
*   **Data Source**: Windward intelligence reports confirmed the export decline.
*   **Blockade Impact**: The decline restricts Saudi Arabia's ability to bypass the Strait of Hormuz.

---

## Economic Pressure: Houthi Blockade Costs Saudi Millions

Houthi maritime operations have caused financial losses for Saudi energy systems:
*   **Financial Impact**: Maritime analysts estimate losses at $504 million.
*   **Revenue Loss**: The drop represents a major loss of Red Sea oil revenues.
*   **Vessel Refusal**: International shipping lines refuse to load at western terminals.

## CENTCOM Stance: The Attempt to Trigger "Midnight Hammer"

U.S. commanders had proposed using the strikes to expand regional operations:
*   **U.S. Strategy**: CENTCOM commander Cooper proposed strikes to destroy Iranian assets.
*   **The Target**: U.S. planners aimed to destroy 20 percent of Iran's conventional forces.
*   **Airstrike Freeze**: The plan was frozen due to interceptor shortages and Iranian threats.

## Conclusion: The Strategic Implications of Houthi Claims

The official statement by Yahya Saree and the drone strikes on Saudi pipelines show the strategic implications of Houthi claims.

By claiming direct responsibility for targeting the East-West pipeline, Yemeni forces have rejected Saudi efforts to globalize the conflict. The suspension of flights in major cities and the drop in Yanbu oil exports confirm that Saudi transit networks remain vulnerable. As regional commands evaluate their air defense options, these developments show that maritime and energy security requires direct negotiations rather than proxy accusations.
