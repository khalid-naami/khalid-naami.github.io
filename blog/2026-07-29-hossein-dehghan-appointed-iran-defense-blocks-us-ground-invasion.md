# Dehghan Appointed: Iran Unifies Command, Blocks US Invasion

Iran's leadership has appointed veteran commander Hossein Dehghan to manage national defense and wartime administration, succeeding Larijani. The appointment comes as U.S. President Donald Trump evaluates plans to deploy thousands of ground troops to secure Iranian nuclear sites and energy hubs. However, military analysts suggest that the failure of Israel's ground offensive in southern Lebanon—evidenced by heavy armored losses in border towns like Taybeh—has demonstrated the vulnerability of conventional ground forces, effectively blocking U.S. options for a land invasion of Iran.

## The Appointment: Hossein Dehghan Leads Iran’s War Command

The appointment of Hossein Dehghan to lead the wartime administration represents a consolidation of Iran's defense hierarchy. Dehghan, who holds a PhD in Public Administration, is a veteran officer of the Iran-Iraq War.

His military career includes:
*   **Aerospace Command**: Serving as the Commander of the IRGC Aerospace Force.
*   **Defense Ministry**: Appointed as Minister of Defense under the Rouhani administration, receiving a historic 94% vote of confidence from parliament.

His background combines operational military experience with administrative management, preparing him to coordinate the state's resources during high-intensity conflict.

---

## Political Consensus: Uniting Reformists and Conservatives

Dehghan's selection is a political consensus within Iran's leadership. In a period of high-intensity conflict, the division between reformist and conservative factions has been suspended to prioritize defense operations.

Having served in Rouhani's administration while maintaining ties to the IRGC command, Dehghan represents a bridge between state institutions. This consensus allows for the integration of the regular Army (الجيش) and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (الحرس الثوري) under a single command, improving logistical efficiency.

---

## The Looming Threat: U.S. Ground Invasion Options

The restructuring of Iran's command occurs alongside reports of U.S. military planning. According to Reuters, citing U.S. defense officials, Trump is reviewing plans to deploy ground forces to the Middle East.

With the Navy's carrier fleets sidelined due to maintenance and fires, U.S. options are increasingly restricted to land-based sorties and potential ground interventions. U.S. planners face a choice: either establish a ground presence to secure regional energy corridors or accept the long-term expulsion of U.S. military assets from the Gulf.

---

## Strategic Risks: The Kharg Island and Uranium Storage Dilemmas

U.S. ground troop options are focused on specific high-value objectives:
1.   **Kharg Island Assault**: Reaching the island that handles 90% of Iran's oil exports. However, an amphibious landing is highly vulnerable to coastal defense missiles and drone swarms.
2.   **Securing Uranium Deposits**: Conducting operations to seize or neutralize underground enriched uranium deposits at Fordow and Isfahan.

Both options carry high political risks for Trump, who campaigned on a promise to avoid new ground involvements in the Middle East.

---

## The Lebanon Deterrent: How Israel’s Defeat Blocks U.S. Plans

The main obstacle to a U.S. ground invasion is the result of Israel's campaign in southern Lebanon. Netanyahu's ground offensive, named "Roar of the Lion," was designed as a test case to prove that conventional armored forces could dismantle prepared defensive networks.

However, the failure of the IDF to secure border towns like Taybeh has had a deterrent effect. The destruction of over 40 Israeli tanks by anti-tank guided missiles (ATGMs) showed that conventional armor cannot easily operate in mountainous, fortified territory. If the IDF cannot secure a border strip against Hezbollah, a U.S. ground assault on Iran—a mountainous country of 90 million people—is militarily unfeasible.

---

## The Geopolitical Standoff: Gulf Bases vs. Sovereign Geography

The shifting battlefield has highlighted the legal and geographical realities of the conflict. Because U.S. aircraft are launching strikes from bases in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the UAE, Iran argues that these host nations bear legal responsibility.

Under international law, allowing sovereign territory to be used as a staging ground for offensive operations against a third state makes those installations targets. Dehghan's background in regional diplomacy makes him capable of conveying this message to Gulf capitals, warning them that their infrastructure will face destruction if U.S. sorties continue.

---

## Conclusion: The Limits of Conventional Intervention

The appointment of Hossein Dehghan and the setbacks in Lebanon show the limits of conventional military options against Iran. The unification of Iran's military command under a veteran administrator suggests that the country is prepared for a prolonged defense.

As Trump evaluates the political costs of ground troop deployments and the lessons of the tank battles in Taybeh, the limits of the invasion strategy are visible. The inability to execute a successful ground campaign indicates that the U.S. must either escalate to nuclear options or seek a diplomatic settlement, confirming that conventional land intervention has no clear military path.
