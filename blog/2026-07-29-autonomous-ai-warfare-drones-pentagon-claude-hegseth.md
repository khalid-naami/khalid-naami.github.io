# War by Algorithm: AI Autonomous Drones in Iran

The military confrontation in the Middle East has entered a new phase characterized by the deployment of fully autonomous, artificial intelligence-driven combat systems. As electronic warfare networks disrupt traditional satellite signals and remote control links, both the U.S. and Israel have transferred operational control to autonomous algorithms. This shift removes human decision-making from the immediate loop, creating a battlefield governed by speed and calculation.

## The Shift to Full Autonomy: Israel’s Countermeasure to Drone Interception

For years, remote pilot intervention and GPS guidance were the standard for unmanned aerial systems. However, after Iranian air defense units successfully intercepted and forced down several Israeli Hermes and other reconnaissance drones, extracting their communication codes, the Israeli military was forced to redesign its operations.

To prevent future electronic captures, Israeli drones entering Iranian airspace are programmed for complete communication silence. Once they cross the border, all links between the aircraft, its operator, and the wider network are severed. The drone becomes entirely autonomous, utilizing onboard AI to navigate, complete its reconnaissance or strike mission, and return to base without transmitting or receiving external signals.

---

## Tactical AI: Dynamic Targeting and Double-Strike Assessments

Inside the target zone, the autonomous AI does more than just follow a pre-programmed flight path. Onboard machine learning models process live optical and radar imagery to identify targets.

To conserve ammunition, the AI evaluates targets in real time, determining whether a strike has succeeded or if a secondary strike is required. Furthermore, if the system detects an unprogrammed target during flight—such as a mobile missile launcher or an active radar battery—it can autonomously prioritize this new target, adjust its mission parameters, and execute a strike without waiting for human approval.

## The Claude Conflict: Anthropic's Role in the Pentagon's Campaign

The deployment of military AI has sparked significant political controversy within the United States. President Donald Trump previously expressed concerns regarding the ideological leaning of tech companies, declaring that he would not allow politically biased corporations to determine how the military fights.

Despite Trump's public stance, reports indicate that the Pentagon continues to utilize Anthropic's Claude models for operations in the Middle East. The military relies on these models to analyze massive streams of satellite imagery, compile live high-definition battlefield maps, and automate targeting lists. This reliance highlights the tension between political directives and the military's demand for advanced software.

---

## The Hegemony of Algorithms Over Ethics

As decision-making loops accelerate, the traditional ethical framework of warfare is increasingly bypassed. The speed of AI processing has surpassed human capability, meaning that target selection, airspace coordination, and damage assessment are now managed by computer networks.

In joint U.S.-Israeli operations, such as strikes targeting strategic fuel reserves, the process is heavily automated. Decisions are executed rapidly based on algorithmic models. Ethical considerations regarding collateral damage are replaced by statistical risk assessments, with military operations increasingly justified by the logic of the machine.

---

## Hegseth's Campaign: Restructuring 'Epic Wrath' via Machine Learning

The transition to algorithmic warfare is supported by U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Described as one of the most hawkish members of the administration, Hegseth has prioritized the integration of AI into combat operations.

Under Hegseth's direction, the U.S. military campaign, code-named "Epic Wrath" (الغضب الملحمي), has been restructured. The AI systems have spent the first week of operations analyzing strike results, logistics data, and defensive responses. The software has reconstructed the targeting list, directing the next phase of the campaign based on its own predictive models rather than traditional staff planning.

---

## The AI’s 3-Day War Projection and Phase 2 Execution

The Pentagon's strategic planning models suggest that any attempt to assassinate Iran's top leadership could trigger a chaotic, multi-front regional war. The AI models project a conflict spanning 12 distinct fronts, drawing in regional and international powers.

To manage this risk, the AI has divided the campaign into distinct operational phases. The system is designed to execute a critical Phase 2 operation, monitoring regional responses in real time. Following this phase, the AI will assess the tactical situation and autonomously determine whether to transition to Phase 3 within a three-day window, demonstrating the extent of algorithmic control.

---

## Conclusion: The Era of Dehumanized Warfare

The integration of autonomous AI into the Middle East conflict represents a fundamental shift in military history. By delegating navigation, targeting, and strategic planning to software, the U.S. and Israel have accelerated the pace of combat.

While this technology bypasses traditional defenses like GPS jamming and remote signal interception, it removes human judgment from critical decisions. As algorithms assume control over target selection and mission execution, the line between technology and strategy continues to blur, creating a highly unpredictable security environment.
