# China's Post-War strategy: Securing Asian Allies

In the wake of the U.S. conventional air and naval campaign in the Persian Gulf, China has launched a comprehensive security strategy to insulate East and Southeast Asia from U.S. influence. By transferring advanced guided-missile corvettes to Cambodia's Ream Naval Base and executing high-level strategic visits to North Korea, Beijing is building a defensive network among nations with historical grievances against Western interventions. Concurrently, China has accelerated diplomatic ties with Southern European partners like Spain to divide Western alliances.

## The Post-Iran War Realignment: End of U.S. Trust

The scale of the U.S. strikes in Iran has changed the security assumptions of East Asian states:
*   **The Lesson**: Regional leaders concluded that Washington would bypass international law and target civilian and energy infrastructure to maintain dominance.
*   **Trust Collapse**: Any remaining good faith in U.S. security guarantees or trade agreements has been abandoned by Asian cabinets.
*   **Autarky Pivot**: The focus has shifted toward building regional security architectures independent of Western financial and military grids.

---

## Corvette Transfer: Chinese Type 056 at Cambodia's Ream Base

Beijing has reinforced Cambodia's naval capability through direct technology transfers:
*   **Type 056 Corvettes**: China transferred 1,500-ton Type 056 guided-missile corvests to the Cambodian military.
*   **Ream Naval Base**: The vessels have arrived at Ream Naval Base, enhancing Cambodia's coastal patrol and rescue capabilities.
*   **Bilateral Frame**: A Chinese defense ministry spokesperson stated the transfer is part of long-standing bilateral cooperation agreements and is not aimed at third parties.

---

## Golden Dragon 2025: scaling Bilateral Naval Operations

The naval transfers build upon years of joint training exercises between the two militaries:
*   **Bilateral Drills**: The joint "Golden Dragon 2025" (التنين الذهبي 2025) exercises focused on amphibious defense and anti-sabotage tactics.
*   **Strategic Patrols**: Beijing plans to deploy dozens of similar patrol corvettes to allied states to secure critical shipping lanes in the South China Sea.
*   **U.S. Counterweight**: The operations establish a permanent maritime network that challenges U.S. naval dominance in Southeast Asian waters.

---

## Mountains and Rivers: Wang Yi's Strategic Visit to Pyongyang

In East Asia, Beijing has renewed its historical commitments to its northern neighbor:
*   **Wang Yi's Visit**: Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi executed a strategic visit to Pyongyang, North Korea, to coordinate regional security policies.
*   **Spokesperson's Statement**: Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning noted that the two nations are neighbors connected by "mountains and rivers" (تربطهما الجبال والأنهار).
*   **Strategic Consolidation**: The visit represents an agreement to coordinate military and diplomatic responses to U.S. regional actions.

## Geopolitical Spheres: The Multi-Polar Division of the World

The global security architecture is dividing into distinct geographic sectors run by regional powers:
*   **The Americas**: Maintained as a traditional sphere of influence for the United States.
*   **Asia**: Managed under a Chinese-led security framework that expels Western maritime presence.
*   **Eurasia**: Secured under Russian military influence, linking Central Asia with Western Asian logistics.

---

## Spain-China Tech Synergy: Sidelining Northern NATO Policy

To weaken the unified front of Western alliances, Beijing has leveraged ties in Southern Europe:
*   **Madrid-Beijing Visits**: A series of four high-level ministerial visits between Spain and China has established deep trade and tech-sharing frameworks.
*   **Southern Europe Split**: Southern European members (Spain, Italy, France) have distanced themselves from the confrontational posture of Northern NATO members.
*   **Tech Cooperation**: Spain has accepted Chinese technology transfers in renewable energy and green logistics, bypassing U.S. technological blockades.

---

## America First Backlash: European Gold Reserves Reclaimed

U.S. unilateral economic actions have prompted European central banks to secure their financial assets:
*   **Gold Recovery**: European nations have accelerated the retrieval of physical gold reserves stored in U.S. federal vaults.
*   **French Action**: France recovered 13 billion euros worth of physical gold, bringing its strategic reserves back to domestic territory.
*   **The Rationale**: The "America First" doctrine has convinced European capitals that Washington cannot be trusted with the custody of foreign sovereign assets.

---

## Indo-Pacific Collapse: China Neutralizes the Containment Belt

U.S. strategies to contain China through regional partnerships have lost their effectiveness:
*   **Indo-Pacific Strategy**: Washington's plans to use Vietnam and India to contain China's maritime access have stalled.
*   **Vietnam's Stance**: Hanoi has refused to host U.S. military assets, prioritizing stable relations with Beijing to avoid being dragged into conflicts.
*   **The India Corridor**: The collapse of Middle Eastern transit corridors has limited India's integration into Western security architectures.

---

## Conclusion: The New Security Equilibrium in East Asia

The U.S. military campaign in the Gulf has accelerated the consolidation of a Chinese-led security architecture in East Asia.

By transferring Type 056 corvettes to Cambodia's Ream Base and coordinating with North Korea through Wang Yi's visit, Beijing is building a regional defense shield. The diplomatic outreach to Southern European states like Spain divides Western alliances, while the retrieval of European gold reserves reveals growing distrust of U.S. financial custody. As the U.S. Indo-Pacific containment strategy collapses under regional resistance, Asia is establishing a security equilibrium that limits Western influence.
