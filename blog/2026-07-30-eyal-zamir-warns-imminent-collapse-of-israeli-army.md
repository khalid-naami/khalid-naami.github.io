# Zamir Warns: Imminent Collapse of Israeli Army

In a closed-door meeting of the Political-Security Cabinet, the Director General of the Ministry of Defense, Eyal Zamir, issued a shocking warning regarding the structural limits of the Israeli military. According to leaked details broadcast by Channel 13's political correspondent Moria Asraf, Zamir outlined ten critical indicators pointing to the imminent collapse of the IDF's reserve and active service formations. The warning has triggered a domestic political crisis, with Opposition Leader Yair Lapid and former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett accusing the government of ignoring the military's manpower shortages.

## The Closed-Door Warning: Eyal Zamir's Cabinet Presentation

The presentation delivered by Eyal Zamir to the security cabinet marks the first time a top defense official has publicly questioned the military's ability to sustain the multi-front war. Zamir's presentation bypassed political messaging, focusing on raw logistical and demographic data.

Zamir warned that the IDF has reached its maximum mobilization limit, and that the current rate of operations is mathematically unsustainable. The warning was not a request for strategic adjustments, but a direct notification that the military's operational capacity is fracturing under the weight of the campaign.

---

## The 10 Indicators of Military Collapse

The core of Zamir's presentation was a list of ten structural vulnerabilities that threaten the integrity of the IDF:
1.  **Reserve exhaustion**: Reserve personnel are serving consecutive tours without adequate rotation.
2.  **Manpower deficit**: Critical shortages in infantry and engineering units.
3.  **Mandatory extension**: The immediate requirement to extend mandatory service to 36 months.
4.  **Reserve duty age**: The necessity of raising the retirement age for reserve officers.
5.  **Routine mission failure**: The inability to sustain border patrols and policing operations.
6.  **Training disruption**: Tactical training cycles have been canceled to deploy instructors.
7.  **Equipment wear**: Heavy vehicle fleets (tanks, APCs) are experiencing high maintenance failure rates.
8.  **Officer retention**: A decline in the rate of junior officers signing long-term contracts.
9.  **Mental exhaustion**: High rates of stress and combat fatigue among active-duty personnel.
10. **Budget deficit**: The depletion of discretionary defense funds.

## The Conscription Crisis: Mobilizing the Haredim

Zamir's warnings have brought the issue of ultra-Orthodox (Haredi) conscription back to the center of Israeli politics. With the military requiring thousands of new soldiers, the long-standing draft exemptions for yeshiva students have become a point of domestic friction.

The defense establishment argues that the burden of service must be distributed equally to prevent the collapse of the reserve system. However, the coalition government's reliance on ultra-Orthodox parties has prevented the implementation of mandatory conscription, creating a legislative deadlock while the military operates at a deficit.

---

## Opposition Backlash: Lapid and Bennett Confront the Cabinet

The leaks from the cabinet meeting have provided the political opposition with ammunition to challenge the Netanyahu administration:
*   **Yair Lapid**: The Opposition Leader warned that the government cannot plead ignorance in the event of a military disaster. He pledged that a future government would cut the 60 billion shekels allocated to draft-evading institutions and enforce conscription on all citizens.
*   **Naftali Bennett**: The former Prime Minister published a video confirming Zamir's warnings, stating that a government dependent on ultra-Orthodox political backing (specifically Aryeh Deri and Yitzhak Goldknopf) is structurally incapable of securing the state.

## The Financial Drain: $17 Billion in the First Week

The manpower crisis is accompanied by a severe financial drain. The first week of U.S. and Israeli operations generated massive expenditures:
*   **The U.S. share**: CENTCOM operations and logistics cost $11 billion.
*   **The Israeli share**: The IDF's operational costs reached $6.5 billion.
*   **The total cost**: Excluding physical damage to infrastructure, the first week of the joint campaign consumed $17 billion.

While Gulf monarchies have provided off-budget funding to subsidize the operations, this financial support has a limit, and it cannot resolve the domestic economic strain caused by the mobilization of hundreds of thousands of reservists.

---

## The Interceptor Economics: Depleting the Air Shield

The financial sustainability of the campaign is further challenged by the economics of air defense. The IDF's air shield relies on high-cost interceptor missiles:
*   **The Arrow and David's Sling**: Interceptors cost between $2 million and $3 million per launch.
*   **The Iron Dome**: Tamir interceptors cost approximately $50,000 to $100,000 per launch.
*   **The Asymmetry**: Hezbollah and Iran are deploying low-cost drones (such as the Shahed series) and unguided rockets that cost a fraction of the interceptors' price.

This imbalance means that a prolonged war of attrition will exhaust Israel's interceptor stocks and deplete its financial reserves, leaving the skies undefended.

---

## The SSM Doctrine Shift: Transitioning from Air Superiority

Faced with the high cost of air defense and the vulnerability of the IAF to overstretch, Israeli military planners (including Abi Ashkenazi in *Maariv*) are proposing a shift in military doctrine.

The proposal suggests transitioning from an Air Force-centric model to a ground-to-ground missile (SSM) model, similar to the Iranian doctrine. Proponents argue that surface-to-surface precision missiles are cheaper to operate, do not require pilots, and are less vulnerable to air defense grids than manned aircraft. This debate represents an admission that the traditional Western model of air superiority is no longer viable in a high-intensity war of attrition.

---

## The Multi-Front Overstretch: Lebanon, Gaza, and the West Bank

The structural decline of the IDF is exacerbated by the requirement to operate across multiple active fronts simultaneously:
*   **Southern Lebanon**: The primary theater, consuming elite divisions and experiencing high armor losses.
*   **The Gaza Strip**: Requiring continuous security and clearing operations.
*   **The West Bank**: Experiencing a rise in armed resistance, requiring the deployment of multiple border police and infantry battalions.
*   **Regional theaters**: Managing defense against long-range strikes from Yemen and Iraq.

This multi-front overstretch prevents the military from concentrating its forces, leaving each sector vulnerable to localized breakthroughs.

---

## Conclusion: The Limits of the Attrition Threshold

Eyal Zamir's warning to the cabinet confirms that the Israeli military has reached the limits of its strategic endurance. The combination of manpower shortages, reserve exhaustion, and high financial costs has undermined the viability of a long-term campaign.

As Netanyahu attempts to maintain a policy of military pressure, his own Ministry of Defense has signaled that the army is on the verge of collapse. The government can continue to reject ceasefires and issue ultimatums, but it cannot print the soldiers required to hold the borders. The attrition threshold has been reached, and if the political leadership does not seek a diplomatic exit, the structural collapse of the military may soon decide the outcome of the war.
