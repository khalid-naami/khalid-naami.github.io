# South Pars Struck: US Strips European Air Defenses

U.S. and Israeli air strikes have targeted Iran's South Pars gas field in Asaluyeh, halting production in two refineries and disrupting regional energy infrastructure. The escalation comes as U.S. President Donald Trump orders the removal of NATO's air defense systems and ammunition stockpiles from Europe to the Middle East, penalizing European nations for remaining neutral in the conflict. Fearing the vulnerability of their own energy networks, Gulf capitals are accelerating diplomatic options, pivoting toward Chinese models for long-term security.

## South Pars Struck: Disruption in Asaluyeh Refineries

The air strikes directly targeted Phases 5, 10, and 14 of the South Pars gas field in Asaluyeh. According to energy analyst Olga Sidorova, the strikes halted production at two major refineries, cutting off 100 million cubic meters of daily gas output.

Iranian military officials labeled the attack a "war crime" targeting the economic infrastructure of the Iranian population, pledging that the action would face retaliatory operations. The strikes aim to degrade Iran's domestic energy capacity, but the fallout is expected to affect regional gas distribution networks.

---

## Gulf Complicity: Basing Sorties and the Qatari Dilemma

The strikes on Asaluyeh were executed by U.S. and Israeli aircraft operating out of bases in the Persian Gulf, including Prince Sultan Airbase in Saudi Arabia. This involvement has created a diplomatic dilemma for regional states like Qatar.

The South Pars field is shared between Iran and Qatar, where it is known as the North Dome. Qatari authorities issued diplomatic statements that avoided direct criticism of the U.S. while expressing concern over the targeting of shared assets. Qatar remains dependent on the U.S. defense umbrella, leaving the country unable to block U.S. forces from utilizing local infrastructure for strike missions.

---

## The Retaliation Risk: Threats to Chevron and Gulf Joint Fields

Tehran has warned that because Gulf nations are hosting U.S. strike aircraft, they will bear the security consequences. The IRGC announced that any strikes on Iranian infrastructure will lead to direct retaliatory operations against U.S. corporate assets in the Gulf.

The primary targets for these counter-strikes include joint offshore fields and U.S. corporate operations, such as Chevron facilities in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar. This threat has alarmed Western energy companies, which do not want to see their multi-billion-dollar investments destroyed in a conflict over regional hegemony.

---

## Trump’s European Retaliation: Stripping NATO Air Defenses

Trump's campaign in the Middle East has also impacted European security. Highly frustrated by the refusal of European NATO allies to deploy minesweepers and warships to help break the Hormuz blockade, Trump ordered the reallocation of NATO defense assets.

The commander of the U.S. European Command confirmed that Patriot batteries, THAAD systems, and advanced interceptor stockpiles have been removed from Europe and shipped to the Middle East. This reallocation aims to protect U.S. bases in the Gulf and support Israeli defense networks, leaving Europe's eastern flank exposed.

---

## European Security Fallout: Delays in Patriot and Ammunition Delivery

The relocation of air defense assets has caused delays in the delivery of military equipment to European capitals:
*   **Ammunition Depletion**: Stockpiles of Patriot missiles and artillery munitions designated for Eastern Europe have been redirected to the Gulf.
*   **Contract Delays**: Delivery schedules for European defense contracts have been suspended to prioritize Middle East operations.

European leaders have criticized this policy shift, arguing that Trump is compromising European defense stability to sustain his campaign against Iran.

---

## Shifting Autonomy: French and German Strategic Defense Pivots

In response to U.S. volatility, France and Germany are accelerating their strategic autonomy initiatives:
*   **FCAS Project**: Joint development of the Future Combat Air System (FCAS) has received increased funding to reduce reliance on U.S. military hardware.
*   **Independent Deployments**: France deployed its own aircraft carrier to the region to protect French maritime interests in the Gulf, maintaining a policy independent of U.S. command.

While Germany expressed understanding of the security goals against Iran, both nations are adjusting to a security landscape where U.S. commitments are no longer reliable.

---

## Conclusion: The Gulf’s Pivot Toward Chinese Development Models

The strikes on South Pars and the hollowing out of European air defenses show the strategic costs of the U.S. campaign in the Gulf. Despite spending trillions on Western defense contracts over the decades, Gulf nations realize that their energy infrastructure remains vulnerable to both Iranian strikes and U.S. escalation.

Fearing that Trump is willing to compromise regional stability to secure his political legacy, Gulf capitals are looking to China. They conclude that long-term stability must be built on economic development and diplomatic mediation—represented by the Chinese model—rather than the military alliances of the West, which treat the region as a funding vehicle for proxy wars.
