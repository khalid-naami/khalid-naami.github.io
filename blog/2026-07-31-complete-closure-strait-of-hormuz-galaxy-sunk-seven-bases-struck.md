# Hormuz Blockade: 7 US Bases Struck

Following an exchange of over 300 strikes, Iran has announced the complete closure of the Strait of Hormuz. The blockade is supported by coordinated strikes on seven regional bases, hitting installations in Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, the UAE, and Jordan. The operations resulted in the sinking of the Cyprus-flagged commercial vessel *Galaxy*, the destruction of two U.S. command centers, and the wounding of 14 U.S. soldiers.

## Seven Bases Struck: Expanding the Regional Target Front

The Iranian retaliatory campaign expanded to hit seven distinct military installations in the region:
*   **Gulf Bases**: Strikes hit bases in Qatar (Al Udeid), Kuwait (Salem), Bahrain, and the UAE.
*   **Levant Bases**: Two installations in Jordan (Muwaffaq Salti and Prince Hassan) were targeted.
*   **Coordinated Waves**: Drone and missile units launched synchronized waves to saturate defense shields.

---

## Strait of Hormuz: The Complete Closure of the Energy Corridor

The IRGC has declared the complete closure of the Strait of Hormuz to commercial traffic:
*   **Total Blockade**: Navigation through the strait has been halted for vessels lacking Iranian clearance.
*   **Enforcement**: Patrol wings and coastal defense batteries have been authorized to fire on violators.
*   **Strategic Impact**: The closure halts a significant portion of global energy shipments.

---

## Sinking of the Galaxy: A Warning to Shipping in the Gulf

Iranian forces demonstrated their enforcement capabilities by targeting a commercial vessel:
*   **The Galaxy**: The Cyprus-flagged commercial vessel was targeted after entering the blockade zone.
*   **Direct Hits**: The vessel was struck by precision anti-ship missiles, catching fire.
*   **Sunk**: The ship sank to the bottom of the Gulf, showing the risks to merchant shipping.

---

## Command Infrastructure: Destroying Centers in Qatar and Jordan

The strikes focused on neutralizing the command and control nodes managing U.S. air operations:
*   **Al Udeid Center**: The main coordination hub in Qatar suffered direct hits.
*   **Prince Hassan Base**: The command and control center in Jordan was completely destroyed.
*   **Communications Loss**: The hits disrupted coordinates processing for regional combat operations.

---

## U.S. Casualties: Fourteen Wounded as Bases Face Sustained Strikes

The expansion of the target list has resulted in additional U.S. military casualties:
*   **Casualties**: The number of wounded U.S. personnel rose to 14 during the latest waves.
*   **Base Hits**: Casualties were recorded at installations in Kuwait and Bahrain.
*   **Medical Flights**: Support wings coordinated transport for injured personnel to medical facilities.

---

## Air Losses: The Downing of U.S. Pegasus and MQ-9 Reapers

The U.S. military suffered significant losses in its regional surveillance and support aircraft:
*   **Pegasus Lost**: An advanced Pegasus electronic support aircraft was downed.
*   **MQ-9 Reapers**: Four MQ-9 Reaper drones were destroyed on the ground at H-5 base.
*   **Surveillance Blinding**: The losses reduce the U.S. capacity to monitor coastal launch activities.

---

## Carrier Isolation: Lincoln Stranded Without Duqm Logistics

The strike on Oman's Port of Duqm has affected the deployment of the U.S. carrier strike group:
*   **Logistical Isolation**: The USS Abraham Lincoln carrier group lost access to its primary fuel depot.
*   **Refueling Limits**: The destruction of the Duqm fuel docks restricts carrier-based operations.
*   **Transit Shift**: The carrier group was forced to shift positions to avoid eastern waters.

---

## GCC Bases Dilemma: Gulf States Restrict Offensive Flight Paths

The U.S. air campaign has faced restrictions from partner states seeking to avoid retaliation:
*   **Flight Bans**: GCC states restricted the launch of offensive combat sorties from their soil.
*   **Intel Exposure**: The bans followed Iran publishing tracking data of regional flights.
*   **Navy Reliance**: The restrictions forced U.S. command to rely on carrier-based aircraft.

---

## Conclusion: The New Strategic Balance of Gulf Access and Power

The complete closure of the Strait of Hormuz, the sinking of the *Galaxy*, and the strikes on seven bases show the new strategic balance in the region.

By disabling U.S. command centers in Qatar and Jordan and isolating carrier groups through Duqm logistics, Iran has challenged the U.S. containment network. The downing of U.S. Pegasus and MQ-9 air assets confirms that U.S. air superiority face local challenges. As Gulf states restrict flight paths to avoid escalation, these developments show that regional stability will rely on negotiated access rather than unilateral naval corridors.
