# Runway Blocked: Iran Prepares for US Uranium Raid

Satellite imagery has revealed tactical preparations by the IRGC to obstruct key runways at Mehrabad International Airport in Tehran. Fearing a U.S. airborne commando operation to seize the country's enriched uranium stockpiles, Iranian forces have positioned explosive-laden vehicle blockades across the airport's primary runways. The move comes as U.S. forces build up airborne and marine units in the region to target nuclear sites in Fordow and Isfahan.

## Mehrabad Runway Blockade: Preventing Airborne Commando Landings

The satellite images confirm that the IRGC has parked at least 30 vehicles directly across the runways at Mehrabad International Airport. The airport features two major runways, each exceeding four kilometers in length, which would be essential for landing U.S. heavy transport aircraft during a surprise commando raid.

Intelligence sources indicate that these vehicles have been loaded with volatile fuel and explosives. This transformation turns them into defensive blockades designed to detonate upon contact, preventing aircraft like the C-130 Hercules or C-17 Globemaster from executing tactical landings.

---

## Air Defense Coverage and the Helicopter Assault Risk

By blocking the runways to heavy fixed-wing aircraft, the IRGC has forced U.S. planners to rely exclusively on helicopters for any airborne assault on Tehran. However, this shift increases the risk of interception.

Mehrabad is protected by mobile anti-aircraft guns and shoulder-fired MANPADS (Man-Portable Air-Defense Systems). Low-flying helicopters operating near the ground would be highly vulnerable to these systems. The same air defense units previously demonstrated their effectiveness by shooting down MQ-9 Reaper drones over Iraq and Yemen, suggesting that a helicopter landing would face significant attrition.

---

## U.S. Airborne Combat Build-Up in Europe and the Gulf

The Iranian preparations are in response to a visible build-up of U.S. special operations forces. The Pentagon has mobilized the 82nd Airborne Division and positioned 9 MC-130J Commando II special operations transport aircraft at RAF Mildenhall in the United Kingdom.

Additionally, the USS *Tripoli* (LHA-7) amphibious assault ship has arrived in the region, carrying 2,500 U.S. Marines, with an initial vanguard of 520 troops already deployed to forward staging areas in the Gulf. This build-up followed U.S. heavy bomber strikes that targeted Iran's primary oil export terminal on Kharg Island, indicating preparation for a potential ground phase.

---

## The Uranium Seizure Plan: Targeting Isfahan and Fordow

According to reports published by CNN, the Trump administration is studying a specialized military operation to destroy Iran's nuclear facilities and retrieve its enriched uranium. The target of the operation is 450 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60%.

The material is stored as Uranium Hexafluoride (UF6) gas in metal canisters at the conversion facility in Isfahan and the deep underground site at Fordow. While 60% enrichment is not weapons-grade (which requires 90%), it serves as a valuable feedstock. If moved to a small, hidden centrifuge facility, it could be enriched to 90% very quickly, making its recovery a priority for Western intelligence.

---

## The Fordow Airflow Crisis: GBU-57 MOP Bombing Failures

Executing a physical seizure at Fordow is complicated by the results of previous air strikes. U.S. GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrator (MOP) bombs hit the Fordow complex, destroying its primary ventilation shafts (فتحات التهوية).

While the strikes damaged the surface infrastructure, they failed to breach the deep underground containment chambers. However, the destruction of the ventilation shafts has created suffocating conditions inside. Entering the unventilated facility would be hazardous for commando units, and the U.S. does not possess enough GBU-57 bombs to guarantee the total destruction of the deeply buried canisters from the air, necessitating a ground raid.

---

## Amphibious Operations and the Vulnerability of Kharg Island

In addition to mainland sites, U.S. planners are studying amphibious landings on isolated territories like Kharg Island. Kharg serves as Iran's primary oil export hub and has been heavily damaged by air strikes.

However, an amphibious landing on the island presents tactical challenges. Iranian artillery and anti-ship missile batteries positioned on the mainland can easily target marine concentrations on the island. The vulnerability of these coastal sectors was demonstrated during earlier strikes on bases in the UAE and Bahrain, suggesting that a marine landing on Kharg would face heavy counter-battery fire.

---

## Conclusion: The Operational Standoff Over Enriched Stocks

The runway blockades at Mehrabad International Airport and the deployment of U.S. Marines highlight the transition of the conflict into a physical confrontation over nuclear assets. By blocking fixed-wing runways and preparing air defense networks, Iran has complicated U.S. plans for a rapid commando raid.

As the U.S. evaluates the ventilation issues at Fordow and the logistical challenges of helicopter assaults, the strategic standoff remains unresolved. The difficulty of seizing the 60% enriched uranium without triggering a broader regional ground war suggests that the conflict will remain focused on containment and strategic strikes, with high risks for any landing operations.
