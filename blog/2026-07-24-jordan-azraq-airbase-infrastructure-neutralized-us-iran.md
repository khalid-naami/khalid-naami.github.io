# Blinding Azraq: US Airbase Infrastructure Neutralized

The air campaign in the Middle East has reached a critical bottleneck. In its eighth official statement, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) detailed a series of heavy, successive ballistic missile strikes targeting the Muwaffaq Salti Airbase in Azraq, Jordan. Having previously neutralized the advanced THAAD radar systems at the site, the latest wave of attacks targeted the base’s runways, fuel networks, and maintenance depots, effectively taking the most active U.S. strike hub in the region out of service.

## Neutralizing the Core: The Attack on Azraq Airbase

Muwaffaq Salti Airbase, located in the desert region of Azraq, Jordan, has served as the primary and most active launch platform for U.S. tactical aircraft executing deep strikes inside Iranian territory. Due to its strategic location, it has been the nerve center of U.S. aerial operations. 

In response, the IRGC has prioritized the neutralization of this installation. By launching successive waves of precision ballistic missiles (قصف بالدفعات), Iranian forces have systematically targeted the base's physical infrastructure, disabling runways and hangar facilities to prevent aircraft sorties.

---

## Tactical Blindness: Bypassing the THAAD Radar

The current bombardment represents the second phase of the operation. As confirmed by satellite imagery and international reports, the first phase successfully targeted and disabled the central AN/TPY-2 radar of the U.S. Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD) system deployed at the base. 

Without this advanced early warning radar, the base's air defenses were left blind. This enabled subsequent waves of missiles to bypass defense grids entirely, striking fuel storage tanks and hangar infrastructure with high precision. The disabling of the THAAD radar has compromised the security of U.S. forces across the entire Levantine theater.

## The Interlocking Airbase Grid: Northern and Southern Fronts

The strike on Azraq is part of a broader strategy to isolate central Israel and U.S. assets by establishing an interlocking operational grid:
1.  **The Southern Axis**: Comprising the neutralized **Azraq Airbase** in Jordan and the heavily targeted **Nevatim Airbase** in the Negev desert.
2.  **The Northern Axis**: Comprising **Ramat David Airbase** and the military wing of **Ben Gurion Airport**, both of which are under continuous rocket fire from Hezbollah in Lebanon.

By focusing on these four key installations, the Axis of Resistance has effectively created a defensive perimeter, cutting off flight corridors and forcing commercial and military traffic out of the central region.

---

## Relocating the Threat: U.S. Bomber Operations from Europe

The systematic neutralization of regional bases has forced the U.S. Department of Defense to alter its deployment patterns. With bases in Kuwait, the UAE, Bahrain, and now Jordan heavily damaged or politically restricted by host governments, the U.S. military is shifting its operations westward.

U.S. Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin announced that the coalition will rely heavily on B-52 and B-2 strategic bombers operating out of European airfields. Flying these long-range missions from Europe is logistically complex and expensive, but it represents the only viable option for the U.S. to maintain strike capacity without relying on vulnerable regional runways.

---

## The Prince Sultan Precedent: Strategic Isolation

The campaign against U.S. bases is designed to enforce a strict regional quarantine. Fearing retaliatory strikes, several Gulf nations have restricted the U.S. from using their territories for offensive operations. A key example is Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia.

Because Prince Sultan Air Base has been kept at minimal utility to avoid dragging Riyadh into the war, its THAAD systems were bypassed and left unused. The U.S. currently relies on a shrinking network of bases, with Jordan's Azraq base having been the last highly active installation before the recent strikes took its runways offline.

---

## Shifting to the Blue Domain: Iran's Naval Strategy

If the coalition's airfields are neutralized, the conflict is expected to shift to the maritime domain. Iran's southern naval command has prepared for this eventuality, positioning assets to counter U.S. naval movements in the Gulf and the Arabian Sea.

Rather than engaging in traditional fleet battles, the Iranian Navy is prepared to deploy asymmetric naval warfare: using swarms of suicide fast-boats, smart naval mines, and midget submarines. This strategy is designed to isolate the Persian Gulf, making it impossible for U.S. aircraft carriers to operate safely within strike range of the Iranian coast.

---

## Conclusion: The Disintegration of the U.S. Shield

The neutralization of Azraq Airbase marks a turning point in the conflict. By choosing to target both U.S. and Israeli airbases simultaneously, Iran has prevented the U.S. from using its regional allies as a shield. 

Had Iran focused its strikes solely on Israel, it would have faced a highly integrated regional air defense network. By systematically dismantling the radar nodes and runways at Azraq, Ruwais, and other regional bases, the Axis of Resistance has fractured this defense system, leaving U.S. forces to rely on long-range bombers operating from Europe.
