# Erdogan Foils US-Israel Kurdish Invasion Plan

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan has revealed that backchannel diplomacy by Ankara blocked a joint U.S.-Israeli plan to launch a Kurdish ground invasion into western Iran. The strategy, which aimed to utilize separatist groups based in Iraqi Kurdistan, was aborted after the IRGC threatened to destroy Iraqi Kurdistan's infrastructure. The exposure highlights strategic rifts within the White House regarding regime-change options in Tehran.

## Kurdish Invasion Plan: The Secret Joint Strategy Exposed

The proposed strategy aimed to open a land corridor to pressure the Iranian government from the west:
*   **The Plan**: A joint U.S.-Israeli plan aimed to support a Kurdish ground invasion of western Iran.
*   **Proxy Forces**: The plan relied on Kurdish separatist militias based in northern Iraq.
*   **Timing**: Preparations were scheduled to coincide with the air operations launched in late February.

---

## Turkish Diplomacy: Erdogan's Backchannel Interventions

Ankara intervened to prevent the activation of the proxy corridor:
*   **Erdogan's Role**: President Erdogan utilized intelligence and diplomatic channels to warn Washington.
*   **Malign Maneuver**: Erdogan characterized the plan as a "malign maneuver" that would destabilize regional borders.
*   **Direct Warning**: Turkey warned that the operation would trigger uncontrolled ethnic conflicts.

---

## The IRGC Counter-Threat: Targeting Iraqi Kurdistan Infrastructure

Iranian military commanders issued warnings to regional authorities regarding support for the plan:
*   **IRGC Warning**: The IRGC warned it would target civil infrastructure in Erbil and Sulaymaniyah.
*   **Complete Destruction**: The threat included the destruction of power grids and oil transport hubs.
*   **Deterrent Action**: The warning aimed to prevent the use of Iraqi Kurdistan as a staging ground.

---

## Trump's Air Force One Retreat: Complicating the Conflict

The U.S. President adjusted his stance after receiving reports on the risks of the proxy operation:
*   **Air Force One Comments**: Trump stated on March 8 that the U.S. did not support a Kurdish crossing.
*   **No Complications**: The President noted: "We do not want to complicate the war further."
*   **Withdrawal**: The statement marked a retreat from initial support discussed in early March.

---

## Internal White House Rift: Vance and Witkoff Defeat Barnea's Plan

The decision to drop the plan followed debates within the U.S. national security team:
*   **Vance and Witkoff**: VP JD Vance and Steve Witkoff lobbied against the regime-change plan.
*   **Mossad Lobbying**: Mossad Director David Barnea and Jared Kushner had pushed for the operation.
*   **Strategic Shift**: The victory of Vance's team marked a shift toward containing the conflict.

---

## Kurdish Regional Stance: Erbil Rejects Cross-Border Operations

The Kurdish Regional Government (KRG) in northern Iraq distanced itself from the project:
*   **Erbil Rejection**: KRG authorities officially rejected the use of their territory for attacks on Iran.
*   **Neutrality**: KRG representatives emphasized their commitment to maintaining neighborly relations.
*   **Erdogan's Praise**: Erdogan publically praised the KRG's neutrality during the crisis.

---

## Legalization in Parliament: The PKK Peace Process Framework

Turkey leveraged the crisis to advance its domestic reconciliation efforts:
*   **PKK Peace Process**: Ankara expanded its dialogue with the Kurdistan Workers' Party.
*   **Legal Framework**: The government prepared bills to codify the peace process in parliament.
*   **Regional Stability**: The process aims to reduce the leverage of external powers over Kurdish groups.

---

## Turkish-Kurdish Integration: Bypassing External Security Designs

Ankara's strategy seeks to integrate Kurdish security interests into a national framework:
*   **Integration**: The peace process aims to replace proxy operations with cooperative frameworks.
*   **Bypassing Designs**: The framework limits the ability of external actors to sponsor separatist groups.
*   **Cooperative Security**: The model emphasizes joint security management along the borders.

---

## Conclusion: The Limits of Asymmetric Proxy Operations

The cancellation of the Kurdish ground invasion plan and Turkey's backchannel diplomacy show the limits of asymmetric proxy operations.

By warning of the risks of a Kurdish crossing and advancing the PKK peace process, Erdogan neutralized a joint U.S.-Israeli contingency plan. The IRGC's infrastructure threats and Trump's comments on Air Force One confirm that proxy corridors face significant retaliatory risks. As Ankara prepares the peace framework in parliament, these developments show that regional stability relies on cooperative borders rather than sponsored insurgencies.
