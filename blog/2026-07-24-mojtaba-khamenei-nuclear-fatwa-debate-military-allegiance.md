# The Nuclear Pivot: Will Mojtaba Khamenei Build the Bomb?

The election of Sayyid Mojtaba Khamenei as Iran’s third Supreme Leader has triggered a massive strategic realignment. As U.S.-led coalition heavy bombers strike Iran's infrastructure, the new leader faces a critical decision: will he bypass his father’s historic fatwa banning nuclear weapons? Backed by absolute pledges of allegiance from both the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and the regular Iranian Army (Artesh), Mojtaba's rise signals a transition toward an unyielding defense doctrine.

## Nuclear Shift: Bypassing the Historic Fatwa

For decades, Iran’s nuclear program was constrained by a key ideological boundary: a fatwa issued by Ayatollah Ali Khamenei declaring the acquisition and use of nuclear weapons as forbidden (Haram) under Islamic law. This religious decree served as a diplomatic baseline for international negotiations. 

However, with Iran’s civilian power grids, refineries, and medical facilities being systematically destroyed in the current conflict, the strategic calculus has changed. Internal pressure is growing on the new Supreme Leader to bypass the fatwa. Many within Iran's security establishment argue that in the face of an existential threat, the only way to protect the nation's sovereignty is to cross the nuclear threshold and construct a functional deterrent.

---

## The Armed Forces Pledge: Solidifying Artesh and IRGC Loyalty

To execute a major strategic shift, a leader needs absolute control over the military apparatus. Both major branches of Iran's armed forces have moved quickly to pledge their absolute loyalty (Bay'ah) to Mojtaba Khamenei:
*   **The IRGC**: Issued a formal statement praising the \"young jurist\" and expressing complete readiness to sacrifice their lives under his command. The statement explicitly vowed to continue victories against \"arrogant powers, dictators, kings, and Zionists\"—a clear warning to Gulf monarchies hosting U.S. forces.
*   **The regular Army (Artesh)**: The Joint Staff issued a passionate pledge of loyalty, describing Mojtaba as a wise, just, and pious leader. The Artesh declared they would be more battle-ready, active, and combative than ever under his leadership, refuting Western claims of a division between the regular army and the clerical establishment.

---

## Trump’s Green Light and the UK’s Posture Shift

The response from Washington to Mojtaba's election was immediate. Donald Trump issued a directive to the U.S. military to utilize \"all available means\" against Iran, effectively giving his generals a green light for total, unrestricted warfare. 

Simultaneously, the United Kingdom shifted its military posture. Having previously restricted its participation to defensive support, London authorized offensive strikes alongside the U.S., fulfilling a prior warning that Mojtaba’s election would be treated by the West as a de facto declaration of war. This posture shift has allowed the U.S. to utilize British airbases in the region for offensive bomber sorties.

---

## Overturning Ali Khamenei’s Dynastic Succession Objections

Mojtaba Khamenei’s ascension also represents a departure from his father's personal wishes. Historically, Ali Khamenei was strongly opposed to the idea of dynastic succession, refusing to support his son's candidacy to prevent the Islamic Republic from resembling the hereditary monarchy it overthrew in 1979.

However, the emergency conditions of the war forced the Assembly of Experts to prioritize stability over ideological preferences. Fearing that a prolonged or disputed succession process would leave the state vulnerable to collapse, the Assembly elected Mojtaba. Factions argue that if the emergency of war was sufficient to bypass objections to dynastic succession, it is equally sufficient to bypass the nuclear fatwa.

---

## The Shadow Commander: Mojtaba’s Basij and Wartime Edge

In Israeli intelligence reports, Mojtaba Khamenei is described as a mysterious, highly capable operator who has spent decades working in the shadows. Unlike his father, who frequently engaged in public diplomacy, Mojtaba has maintained a low profile, making it difficult for foreign intelligence to track his movements or predict his decisions.

During the 1980s Iran-Iraq War, Mojtaba served within the elite Habib ibn Mazahir brigade of the IRGC. He later assumed a key role in coordinating the Basij volunteer militia. This background gives him a deep connection to the security forces, making him a formidable leader during wartime.

---

## Hardliner or Shia MBS? The Abdolreza Davari Assessment

Despite his hardline reputation, some analysts argue that Mojtaba Khamenei possesses a pragmatic streak. In an interview with the *New York Times*, political analyst Abdolreza Davari, who is close to the leadership, described Mojtaba as belonging to a younger, more realistic generation of clerics.

Davari compared Mojtaba to Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (MBS), describing him as a leader capable of initiating structural modernization and social reforms, backed by iron-fisted rule. Davari argued that Mojtaba’s family credentials and security connections would immunize him from conservative criticism, giving him the unique authority to negotiate a de-escalation with the West from a position of strength.

---

## Conclusion: The Era of Unyielding Resistance

The election of Mojtaba Khamenei has consolidated Iran's leadership, ending Western hopes of exploiting internal political divisions during the war. By uniting the reformist presidency of Pezeshkian, the regular military, and the IRGC under a single command, Iran has braced itself for a long-term conflict.

Whether Mojtaba Khamenei chooses to follow the path of strategic modernization or decides to cross the nuclear threshold, the strategic landscape has changed. With the military unified under his command, any future diplomatic settlement must be a comprehensive regional agreement, as Iran has made it clear that it will not negotiate under pressure.
