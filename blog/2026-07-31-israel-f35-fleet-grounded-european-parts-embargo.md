# Israel F-35 Fleet Grounded: Parts Embargo

Over 25 percent of the Israeli Air Force's F-35 stealth fighter fleet has been grounded. The grounding, revealed in testimony by outgoing Air Force Equipment Chief Brig. Gen. Shin, is the result of an undeclared embargo on spare parts imposed by European nations. To bypass the embargo, Israel has established under-the-table procurement channels with Ukraine to secure components.

## The Parts Embargo: Silent Restrictions Ground Israeli Jets

European governments have implemented silent restrictions on military exports to Israel:
*   **The Embargo**: Germany, France, the UK, and the Netherlands have blocked spare parts.
*   **F-35 Grounding**: Over 25 percent of the Israeli F-35 fleet is out of service.
*   **Component Deficit**: Missing parts include electronics cards and sensor antennas.

---

## Rome Statute Signatories: Legal Bans Interrupt Logistics

Signatories to the Rome Statute have cited legal obligations to restrict military transit:
*   **Legal Action**: European authorities notified Tel Aviv of transit bans on F-35 parts.
*   **Dutch Injunction**: The court in The Hague blocked the export of F-35 parts from Dutch hubs.
*   **Logistics Break**: The rulings cut access to Lockheed Martin's European storage warehouses.

---

## Ukraine Sourcing: Tel Aviv Establishes an Alternative Channel

Faced with European restrictions, Israel has turned to Ukraine for specialized parts:
*   **The Channel**: Sourcing networks transfer Soviet and Western aviation components from Kyiv.
*   **Electronic Cards**: Ukrainian military units transfer electronic components to Israel.
*   **U.S. Support**: The channel operates with the tacit approval of U.S. procurement officers.

---

## Iranian Fury: Tehran Warns Kyiv Over Military Sourcing

Tehran has warned Ukraine regarding its military cooperation with Tel Aviv:
*   **Tehran's Warning**: Iranian officials stated that sourcing networks make Ukraine an active adversary.
*   **Intelligence Focus**: Iranian intelligence is tracking cargo flights between Kyiv and Tel Aviv.
*   **Caspian Sea Response**: The dispute has intensified tensions over shipping channels in the Caspian Sea.

---

## Armor Wear: D9 Bulldozers and Merkava Transmissions

The spare parts crisis has extended to the IDF's ground vehicle fleet:
*   **Bulldozer Shortage**: Ground operations face a shortage of spare parts for D9 armored bulldozers.
*   **Transmission Wear**: Merkava transmissions and Namer suspensions are worn out.
*   **Operational Mileage**: The armored fleet logged over 1.0 million kilometers during the campaign.

---

## Domestic Limitations: The Failure of Self-Reliance Projects

Israel's defense industry cannot manufacture the critical parts locally:
*   **Production Gap**: Israel builds Merkava hulls but imports engine blocks and optical sensors.
*   **F-35 Dependence**: Local firms build F-35 wings but depend on U.S. software and cards.
*   **Contract Restrictions**: Lockheed Martin's licenses prohibit the replication of F-35 sub-assemblies.

---

## reverse engineering: Israeli Efforts to Replicate Components

The IDF Technical Division has initiated emergency replication programs:
*   **Replication**: Engineers are attempting to reverse-engineer electronic cards.
*   **Testing Gaps**: Locally made parts lack factory certifications and safety approvals.
*   **Aircraft Risks**: Installing unapproved parts increases the risk of system failures during flight.

---

## Trump's Pressure: Washington Attempts to Break the European Ban

The U.S. administration is attempting to pressure European allies to lift the bans:
*   **U.S. Action**: Trump urged European leaders to release F-35 parts bound for Israel.
*   **Defense Contracts**: Washington threatened to restrict European access to U.S. military technology.
*   **Embargo Stance**: European capitals have maintained their bans due to domestic political pressure.

---

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Global Defense Supply Chains

The grounding of Israel's F-35 fleet and the European spare parts embargo show the vulnerability of global defense supply chains.

By identifying the limits of local manufacturing and the impact of legal restrictions, the IDF Equipment Division has highlighted structural dependencies. Sourcing parts from Ukraine and attempting to reverse-engineer components confirm the urgency of the parts famine. As Western capitals maintain their export restrictions, these developments show that advanced military assets remain dependent on global logistics networks.
