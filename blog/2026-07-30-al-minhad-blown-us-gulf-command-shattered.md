# Al Minhad Blown: U.S. Gulf Command Shattered

The Iranian military has executed a precision strike on Al Minhad Air Base in the United Arab Emirates, disabling a South Korean-supplied air defense radar and targeting the U.S. alternative command center. According to Statement 48, the strike has degraded U.S. special operations capabilities in the Gulf, forcing the relocation of air assets to Israel and Jordan. Leaked U.S. military documents from Crete confirm that U.S. casualties have surged, threatening the operational limits established by the Trump administration.

## The Al Minhad Strike: Statement 48 of the Joint Command

The strike on Al Minhad (المنهاد) Air Base, located 30 kilometers outside Dubai, represents a coordinated attack on U.S. forward logistics.

According to Statement 48, the Iranian Aerospace Force deployed a wave of suicide drones and cruise missiles. The primary objective was to disable the air defense grid that protects the northern UAE, opening a corridor for subsequent missile strikes on the base's command installations.

---

## Disabling the South Korean Radar Array

The initial wave of drones targeted the base's primary air defense sensor:
*   **The System**: A South Korean-supplied multi-function radar system, integrated into the UAE's medium-range missile defense grid.
*   **The Destruction**: The radar was hit by a cluster drone, disabling its processing arrays and blinding the base's interceptor batteries.
*   **The Result**: The loss of the South Korean radar left the base's runways and hangars exposed, allowing a second wave of missiles to strike the main command complex.

---

## Hitting Alternative Command Site 237

Following the initial breach, U.S. officers evacuated the main base headquarters, relocating to a pre-designated alternative command center:
*   **The Location**: A secure underground installation designated as Site 237.
*   **The Target**: Iranian reconnaissance drones tracked the relocation, and a tactical missile struck the entrance of Site 237 during a briefing.
*   **SOF Presence**: At the time of the impact, 36 field commanders of the U.S. Special Operations Forces (GIMO) were inside the facility, resulting in multiple casualties.

---

## The Crete Leak: Secret U.S. Casualty Documents

The scale of U.S. casualties has been exposed by a document leaked from the Souda Bay military base in Crete:
*   **Wounded Count**: The document, reported by Greek media, lists **1,134** U.S. personnel wounded since the start of the conflict.
*   **Emergency Cases**: An additional **134** emergency cases are listed in the document's first section.
*   **Total Toll**: With the Al Minhad casualties (approximately 200 light to moderate injuries), the total number of U.S. wounded is estimated between **1,400 and 1,500**.

---

## The 2,000 Casualty Cap and the Six-Week Limit

The rising casualty count has created a political challenge for President Trump:
*   **The Directive**: Trump signed a binding national security directive restricting the campaign to a maximum of six weeks.
*   **The Casualty Cap**: The directive caps U.S. non-fatal casualties at 2,000.
*   **The Manipulation**: To avoid breaching this cap, the U.S. command has ordered **416 wounded soldiers** back to active duty in the field after receiving basic medical treatment, despite being physically unfit for operations.

---

## Relocating Bases to Israel and Jordan

Blinded and exposed in the Gulf, U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) has initiated a strategic withdrawal:
*   **Bases Relocated**: Air assets and command personnel are being relocated from the UAE to Israel and Jordan.
*   **Eastern Mediterranean**: Remaining air wings will operate from carriers in the Eastern Mediterranean, out of range of Iranian ballistic missiles.
*   **The Shift**: The move effectively abandons the Gulf's air defense screen, leaving local partners to defend their own infrastructure.

---

## Financial Toll: The $141 Billion UAE Loss

The economic cost of the campaign in the UAE has exceeded early estimates:
*   **MEE Estimates**: The news outlet *Middle East Eye* reported U.S. financial losses in the UAE at $120 billion.
*   **Pentagon Figures**: Classified Pentagon documents list the actual and projected losses up to April 1 at **$141 billion**.
*   **The Deficit**: The figures include the destruction of advanced aircraft, logistics nodes, and the cost of emergency base relocations.

---

## The $200 Oil Risk of the Kharg Campaign

Despite these losses, President Trump continues to contemplate a ground assault on Iran's Kharg Island oil terminal. However, economists warn of a global crisis:
*   **The $200 Barrel**: A ground invasion of Kharg would immediately push global crude oil prices to $200 per barrel.
*   **Global Recession**: A price spike of this magnitude would trigger an immediate recession in Western markets.
*   **U.S. Market Collapse**: The domestic U.S. market, despite domestic production, would be unable to absorb the energy shock, leading to high inflation.

---

## Conclusion: The Gulf Withdrawal Begins

The missile strike on Al Minhad Air Base and the subsequent relocation of U.S. assets to Israel and Jordan confirm that the U.S. military presence in the Gulf is no longer sustainable.

By targeting the alternative command centers and disabling the air defense radar arrays, Iran has neutralized the U.S. capability to coordinate special operations from UAE territory. The rising casualty figures, combined with the $141 billion economic toll, have forced the U.S. command to bypass its own safety directives. The withdrawal of bases to Israel and Jordan proves that the Gulf is no longer a viable staging ground for Western operations, leaving local partners exposed to the consequences of the war.
