# Stage of Brutality: Segula Strike and Civilian Targets

The U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran has entered what the U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) officially describes as a "stage of brutality." With strategic military options failing to dismantle the Iranian state, coalition forces have shifted their parameters to target vital civilian utilities—including power stations, refineries, and medical centers. In immediate retaliation, Iran launched a devastating cluster ballistic missile strike targeting the Segula industrial zone in Petah Tikva, bringing the realities of the war to the heart of Israel.

## The Escalation to Brutality: Targeting Civilians

In the initial phases of the war, both sides claimed to focus strictly on military installations and command centers. However, as the conflict drags on, the strategic deadlock has forced a transition. Failing to trigger a popular uprising or collapse the government in Tehran, U.S. and Israeli air forces began targeting civilian infrastructure.

Over the past week, strikes have hit energy refineries, water desalination networks, and medical centers (such as kidney dialysis facilities) in cities like Shiraz, Isfahan, and Dezful. This campaign has resulted in over 1,000 civilian deaths in Iran, marking a qualitative shift in coalition tactics toward total economic and humanitarian pressure.

---

## The Segula Cluster Strike: Petah Tikva Under Fire

Iran's response to the destruction of its civilian infrastructure was swift and highly destructive. Air raid sirens sounded across the Dan region, the coastal plain, and Samaria as Iranian ballistic missiles penetrated the air defense envelope. 

A heavy cluster missile, designed to break apart into multiple warheads, detonated over the Segula industrial zone in Petah Tikva, in central Israel. The strike scattered submunitions across eight distinct sites within the industrial complex, causing massive fires and sending thick plumes of black smoke over the city. The attack triggered widespread panic, shattering the illusion of safety that Israel's air defense network had previously provided.

---

## Emergency Mobilization: Command on the Ground

The scale of the Segula strike forced an immediate, high-level emergency response. As unexploded submunitions littered the industrial zone, police, fire crews, and search-and-rescue teams mobilized to secure the area and prevent secondary explosions.

High-ranking commanders rushed to the scene to coordinate the operations, including Major General Amir Cohen (Central/Sharon District Commander), Major General Eli Pinto, and Major General Aviad Katfi (Petah Tikva Police Chief). Their presence highlighted the severity of the security breach, as emergency services struggled to contain the fallout from a single cluster warhead that successfully bypassed defensive systems.

---

## CENTCOM’s Strategic Reinterpretation of International Law

To justify this shift toward targeting residential and civilian-heavy areas, CENTCOM has introduced a controversial legal argument. U.S. military officials claim that because Iranian-aligned groups targeted U.S. military personnel housed in civilian hotels and private residential quarters throughout the Gulf, these civilian areas have lost their protected status.

Admiral Brad Cooper stated that by integrating military operations into civilian spheres, Iran has compromised the safety of its own people. This legal reinterpretation is seen by critics as a preemptive justification for expanding the target list to include residential zones, indicating that the coalition is prepared to tolerate much higher civilian casualties.

---

## The Failure of the Decoy-Based Regime Change Theory

The shift to civilian targeting reveals a failure in the coalition's initial strategic assumptions. Western planners had hoped that destroying Iran's military capacity would leave the government weak and prompt the population to rise up. 

However, because Iran successfully protected its core military assets through a massive decoy campaign, the coalition's first wave was largely ineffective. Left without immediate military targets to exploit, and facing mounting pressure from the White House, commanders shifted their focus to civilian infrastructure in a desperate attempt to force a political collapse.

---

## Netanyahu’s Surprises: Systematic Utility Destruction

The systematic destruction of civilian infrastructure aligns with Benjamin Netanyahu's warnings of upcoming "surprises" in the war. These surprises are now visible: the targeting of domestic energy supplies, hospital facilities, and water distribution networks. 

This strategy is supported by statements from Washington. Speaking on ABC, Donald Trump declared that the next Iranian leader would not survive without U.S. approval, claiming that the military campaign was necessary to prevent Iran from dominating the entire Middle East. This statement reinforces the coalition's commitment to forcing a complete political transition through economic destruction.

---

## Conclusion: The Threat of Mutual Attrition

The strike on Petah Tikva is a warning of the consequences of targeting civilian populations. While the U.S. and Israel struggle to protect critical logistics at Al-Azraq Airbase in Jordan and various installations in the Gulf, Iran is proving that it can retaliate against Israel's industrial heartland.

As both sides expand their target lists to include civilian utilities, the war is turning into a campaign of mutual economic destruction. If the escalation continues, the resulting humanitarian crisis will spread far beyond Iran's borders, engulfing the entire region in an uncontrollable war of attrition.
