# Hormuz Standoff: U.S. Destroyer Rebutted by Satellite

The naval confrontation in the Strait of Hormuz has intensified as satellite tracking data directly contradicts U.S. Navy claims of having breached the Iranian-imposed blockade. While Washington declared its warships successfully transited the chokepoint, tracking logs from MarineTraffic show the destroyer USS Michael Murphy (DDG-112) turned back near the Omani coast. The incident follows leaks from a secret 21-hour negotiation session where Donald Trump directly intervened six times, demanding a share of Hormuz revenues and a 20-year freeze on Iranian enrichment.

## Delayed Maritime Engagement: Iran's Asymmetric Strategy

Iranian planners deliberately avoided a direct naval confrontation during the initial weeks of the conflict:
*   **Tactical Delay**: Despite U.S. and Israeli conventional strikes hitting 30,000 targets over 38 days, Iran held back its naval assets.
*   **Conserving Power**: The strategy was designed to draw Western forces deeper into the region before activating maritime choke points.
*   **The Focus**: Preserving maximum strength for the defense of the Strait of Hormuz, which Tehran views as its primary geopolitical shield.

---

## The IRGC Navy Pivot: Transitioning to Mosaic Defense

The defense of the strait has been transferred from the regular Iranian Navy to specialized IRGC units:
*   **Asymmetric Focus**: The IRGC Navy relies on mobile missile launchers, sea mines (ألغام), and swarm drones rather than large surface warships.
*   **Decentralized Commands**: Coastal batteries operate under independent regional commands, preventing a central grid collapse.
*   **The Warning**: The IRGC announced that any foreign military vessel attempting to transit the strait without clearance will be treated as a ceasefire violation.

## Blockade for Blockade: Trump's Unilateral Decree

The U.S. administration has responded to the shipping restrictions by threatening a counter-blockade:
*   **Trump's Decree**: The White House announced that the U.S. Navy will blockade all commercial vessels entering or leaving the Persian Gulf starting Monday.
*   **Blockade for Blockade**: The strategy, labeled "blockade for blockade," aims to deny Iran any economic activity if Western oil tankers are blocked.
*   **U.S. Isolation**: The UK and other European NATO members have refused to commit naval assets to the blockade, leaving the U.S. Navy to execute the operation alone.

## The Satellite Rebuttal: USS Michael Murphy Turns Back

Publicly available maritime tracking data has challenged U.S. military statements regarding transit operations:
*   **The Claim**: The Pentagon announced that two U.S. destroyers successfully crossed the Strait of Hormuz into the Persian Gulf.
*   **The Reality**: Satellite tracking data from MarineTraffic showed the USS Michael Murphy reached the coast of Oman before turning back to the Arabian Sea.
*   **Verification**: Space-based imagery confirmed the destroyer did not enter the narrow chokepoint, verifying the accuracy of the IRGC logs.

## The 21-Hour Secret Session: Trump's Six Interventions

Iranian MP Mahmoud Nabavian leaked the details of a secret 21-hour negotiation session:
*   **Trump's Interventions**: The U.S. president personally intervened six times during the sessions to adjust the negotiation terms.
*   **Sovereignty Rejection**: The U.S. delegation pushed for terms that the Iranian representatives characterized as demands for complete surrender.
*   **Negotiation Breakdown**: The sessions ended without an agreement due to the divergence between U.S. demands and Iranian sovereign rights.

## Unrealistic Demands: 20-Year Suspension & Revenue Share

The U.S. negotiation draft included significant demands regarding Iran's resources and nuclear program:
*   **Enrichment Freeze**: A demand for a 20-year suspension of all uranium enrichment rights.
*   **Uranium Export**: The immediate export of all Iranian stockpiles of 60% enriched uranium.
*   **Revenue Sharing**: A proposal for the U.S. to receive a shared portion of the transit fees collected at the Strait of Hormuz, which the Iranian team rejected.

## Targeting China's Oil: The Geopolitical Core of the Blockade

The primary target of the U.S. blockade is not Tehran, but the Chinese economy:
*   **Chinese Reliance**: Beijing relies heavily on Iranian crude imports to power its industrial hubs.
*   **Timetable Pressure**: U.S. planners calculate that a blockade lasting 55 to 60 days will trigger severe energy shortages in China.
*   **Summit Leverage**: Trump seeks to use this economic pressure to secure concessions ahead of the rescheduled China-U.S. summit in May.

## Putin's Mediation Offer: Building Channels to the White House

Russian President Vladimir Putin has offered to act as an intermediary between the two nations:
*   **The Proposal**: In a phone call with Iranian President Pezeshkian, Putin proposed establishing a Moscow-managed diplomatic channel.
*   **Trump Alignment**: Putin seeks to use the mediation to build a closer relationship with Donald Trump.
*   **Asymmetric Shipping**: The offer follows reports that Russian military shipments to Iran were half-empty and did not contain advanced combat systems, prompting caution in Tehran.

## Conclusion: The Asymmetric Stalemate in the Strait

The standoff in the Strait of Hormuz highlights the limits of U.S. naval power against asymmetric defense strategies.

By decentralized IRGC units deploying mines and drones, Iran has established a credible defense, forcing the USS Michael Murphy to turn back near Oman despite U.S. transit claims. The failure of European allies to support Trump's counter-blockade leaves Washington isolated in its attempt to disrupt China's energy supplies. As Iran rejects U.S. demands for a 20-year enrichment freeze and revenue sharing, the failure of U.S. naval movements to breach the chokepoint confirms that the Strait of Hormuz remains under regional control.
