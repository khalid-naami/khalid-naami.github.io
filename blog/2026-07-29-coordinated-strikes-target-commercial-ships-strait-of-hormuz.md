# Hormuz Blockade: Three Cargo Ships Struck in Gulf

The maritime confrontation in the Persian Gulf has escalated following a coordinated series of strikes targeting commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. In a clear demonstration of control over the vital trade corridor, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy (IRGCN) targeted three commercial vessels that attempted to cross the strait without obtaining clearance from Iranian authorities, ignoring active naval warnings.

## Coordinated Strikes: Targeting Vessels in the Strait

The coordinated operation was executed against three cargo vessels within a few hours. According to international maritime monitors and trade publication *TradeWinds*, the ships were targeted after they failed to respond to radio warnings issued by the IRGCN patrol units in the strait.

The strikes were executed with high precision, designed to damage the vessels' structures and force them to divert to safe anchorages without causing them to sink, avoiding loss of life or environmental pollution. The action serves as a direct enforcement of Iran's declaration that all shipping in the strait must obtain prior authorization.

---

## Star Joint: A Strike on Israeli-Linked Greek Shipping

The first vessel targeted was the *Star Joint*, a Greek-owned bulk carrier managed by Star Bulk under the leadership of Petros Pappas. The vessel operates with partial Israeli capital, making it a high-priority target under Iran's regional embargo rules.

The bulk carrier was struck by a precision projectile, causing a localized fire and a small hull breach. While the damage did not threaten the ship with sinking or release hazardous materials, the crew was forced to abort their planned transit and navigate the vessel to a safe anchorage, demonstrating the vulnerability of joint-capital shipping.

---

## Express Rome and Maureen Ari: The Price of Ignoring Warnings

The other two vessels targeted in the operation were the *Express Rome* and the Thailand-flagged container ship *Maureen Ari*. Both vessels chose to proceed with their transits, relying on public assurances from the U.S. administration that coalition naval forces would secure their passage.

The *Express Rome* was struck approximately 25 nautical miles northwest of Ras Al Khaimah, UAE, suffering a hull breach above the waterline. Simultaneously, the *Maureen Ari* was hit while anchored or in transit, resulting in a localized fire that the crew managed to control. Both vessels aborted their journeys and sought safe anchorage, confirming that coalition promises could not guarantee safe passage.

---

## Tangsiri’s Declaration: Enforcing Iranian Sovereignty

The political context of the strikes was reinforced by a public statement from Rear Admiral Alireza Tangsiri, Commander of the IRGC Navy. In a post on social media, Tangsiri warned shipping companies against ignoring maritime directives.

Tangsiri stated: "Did the ships find peace in crossing the Strait of Hormuz today? We must ask the sailors of *Express Rome* and *Maureen Ari*, who trusted empty promises, ignored warnings, and tried to cross, only to get entangled. Any vessel wishing to cross the Strait must request permission from Iran."

---

## The Panic in the Gulf: AIS Spoofing and Transit Risks

The coordinated strikes have caused significant concern among international shipping lines. In response to the growing risk of interception, ship owners have tightened security protocols, with several vessels choosing to disable their transponders or transmit false data.

During the past 24 hours, 5 out of 15 commercial vessels transiting the Gulf region utilized false Automatic Identification System (AIS) data to mask their actual locations. Despite these spoofing attempts, Iranian surveillance networks successfully tracked and identified the vessels, showing that electronic masking is insufficient to bypass local tracking.

---

## The US-Iran Escalation: Fast Boats and Dammam Strikes

The strikes on commercial vessels occurred alongside direct U.S.-Iranian military exchanges. Prior to the shipping incidents, the U.S. military claimed to have destroyed 16 Iranian fast attack craft configured for mine-laying operations near the strait.

In response to the U.S. action against its patrol craft, the IRGC launched a ballistic missile strike targeting a U.S. logistical facility in Dammam, eastern Saudi Arabia. This direct exchange demonstrates that the maritime confrontation is connected to the broader regional conflict, with both sides striking logistical assets.

---

## Conclusion: The New Rules of Navigation in the Strait

The coordinated strikes in the Strait of Hormuz have established a new operational reality for global trade. By using targeted, non-lethal strikes to enforce its authorization requirements, Iran has shown it can disrupt shipping without triggering an immediate environmental crisis.

While the U.S. administration calls for open transit and promises protection, the practical risk of damage and rising insurance rates will force shipping companies to comply with Iranian directives. As long as the regional war continues, the Strait of Hormuz will remain a highly contested chokepoint, with Iran utilizing its geographic advantage to enforce its security terms.
