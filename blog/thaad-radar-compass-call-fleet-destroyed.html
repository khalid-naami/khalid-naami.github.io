THAAD Radar and Compass Call Fleet Destroyed

July 30, 2026 · 5 min read
Khalid Naami
Founder, Owner, and CEO at Dashboard Options

Satellite intelligence has confirmed that the AN/TPY-2 radar—the critical "eyes" of the THAAD missile defense system—was destroyed at Prince Sultan Air Base (Al-Kharj) in a March 1 Iranian missile attack. Both American and Russian sources corroborate the near-complete destruction. Simultaneously, Ukraine's Defense Express revealed that a single Iranian strike destroyed half of the U.S. Air Force's EC-130H Compass Call electronic warfare fleet—a loss so severe that the newest EA-37B replacements were immediately deployed to the theater in their first operational mission.

THAAD Destroyed: The Eyes Go Blind​

High-resolution satellite imagery has confirmed the destruction of the THAAD system's primary sensor:

  • The Target: The AN/TPY-2 radar at Prince Sultan Air Base—the most critical component of the THAAD missile defense architecture.
  • The Evidence: Satellite images show extensively charred surfaces and severe structural deformation of the high-tech equipment.
  • The Consequence: The disabling of this radar leaves a large sector of airspace completely vulnerable to ballistic threats—all Saudi targets are now "easy targets."
  • Irreparable: Replacement or field repair of such equipment is physically impossible.

The Financial Cost: $136 Million in Radar Alone​

The economic impact of the THAAD destruction is staggering:

  • Radar Arrays: The damaged antenna arrays alone cost approximately $136 million—excluding associated electronic systems and logistics.
  • Personnel: Satellite imagery confirmed at least one soldier was neutralized in the explosion zone.
  • Strategic Cost: The destruction of a system specifically designed to intercept ballistic missiles raises fundamental questions about the reliability of the American security umbrella in the region.

Half the Compass Call Fleet: One Strike​

The destruction of the EC-130H Compass Call fleet represents one of the most significant U.S. Air Force losses in decades:

  • The Strike: A single Iranian missile destroyed two EC-130H Compass Call electronic warfare aircraft at Prince Sultan Air Base.
  • The Fleet: Of the 14 EC-130H aircraft ever built, only 2-4 remained in active service. Iran has destroyed more than half the operational fleet in a single strike.
  • The Mission: The EC-130H, built on the C-130 Hercules frame with a 13-person crew, was designed to suppress enemy air defenses, jam radar systems, and disrupt communications.

EA-37B Deployment: The Emergency Replacement​

The loss of the EC-130H fleet triggered an immediate deployment of its successor:

  • The Replacement: Two EA-37B Compass Call aircraft—the newest electronic warfare platform in the USAF inventory—departed the United States as obvious replacements.
  • First Operational Deployment: The EA-37B was deployed to the CENTCOM area of operations on March 31, 2026, marking the type's first combat deployment.
  • Superior Capabilities: The EA-37B flies at double the altitude (40,000+ feet) and double the speed of the EC-130H, covering far greater areas while remaining outside direct threat ranges.

The Electronic Warfare Gap: S-300 Blindness​

The destruction of the EC-130H fleet has immediate operational consequences:

  • The S-300 Problem: The Compass Call aircraft were responsible for "blinding" Iran's S-300 air defense systems, enabling strike aircraft to operate with reduced risk.
  • The Gap: Until the EA-37B replacements achieve full operational capability, U.S. strike aircraft face Iranian air defenses at their full capability.
  • The Irony: Hegseth had claimed all Iranian air defenses were neutralized—yet the loss of the electronic warfare fleet that was supposed to maintain that suppression proves the claim was premature.

The Israeli Admission: Iranian Technology Exceeds U.S.​

A Hebrew-language source published what amounts to the first Israeli admission of Iranian technological superiority:

  • The Admission: Israeli analysis acknowledged that current Iranian technology exceeds American defensive systems in specific domains.
  • The Conclusion: The new U.S. Joint Chiefs has concluded that Iranian military technology must be "destroyed in its cradle" before it proliferates.
  • The Fear: This technology threatens to end U.S. leadership in defense technology globally—if it becomes commercially available, it will render American systems obsolete.

KC-135 and E-3 Sentry: Maintaining the Narrative​

The U.S. military has released imagery designed to counter the narrative of catastrophic losses:

  • The Image: A photograph of a KC-135 Stratotanker refueling an E-3 Sentry AWACS aircraft on March 31 was released to demonstrate continued operational capability.
  • The E-3 Sentry: The aircraft carries a 30-foot rotating radar dome for airborne surveillance—the last major sensor system available after the THAAD radar's destruction.
  • The Message: The U.S. is signaling that despite the loss of ground-based radar and electronic warfare aircraft, its airborne sensors remain operational.

Trump's Retaliation: Targeting Civilian Infrastructure​

The strategic loss of the THAAD radar and electronic warfare aircraft forced a change in U.S. strategy:

  • The Retaliation: Faced with the destruction of high-tech assets, President Trump ordered retaliatory strikes against civilian and dual-use Iranian infrastructure.
  • The Rationale: This shift was intended to project strength to domestic audiences and offset the visible failure of U.S. defensive capabilities.
  • The Consequence: Targeting civilian infrastructure has hardened Iranian political resolve and accelerated their development of asymmetric counter-technologies.

Conclusion: The Electronic Shield Shattered​

The confirmed destruction of the THAAD radar and half the EC-130H Compass Call fleet at Prince Sultan Air Base represents the most significant material loss suffered by the U.S. military in the Iran campaign.

The AN/TPY-2 radar—irreplaceable in the field—has left Saudi Arabian airspace defenseless against ballistic threats. The loss of the Compass Call fleet has created an electronic warfare gap that exposes U.S. strike aircraft to Iranian air defenses at full capability. The emergency deployment of the EA-37B—in its first-ever operational mission—confirms the severity of the crisis. The Israeli admission that Iranian technology has surpassed American systems in specific domains marks a turning point in the global defense technology competition, with implications that extend far beyond this conflict.
