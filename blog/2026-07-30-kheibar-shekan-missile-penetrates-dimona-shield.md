# Kheibar Shekan Hits: Dimona Nuclear Zone Breached

In Wave 77 of its retaliatory campaign, the IRGC deployed its most advanced solid-fuel ballistic platform, the Kheibar Shekan, alongside Qiam decoy missiles, to successfully breach the air defense grid protecting the Shimon Peres Negev Nuclear Research Center in Dimona. While U.S. and Israeli batteries succeeded in intercepting a Qiam missile targeting Eilat, the maneuvering warheads of the Kheibar Shekan bypassed all defensive layers, striking within the Dimona and Yeruham nuclear security sectors. The strike represents a direct response to U.S.-Israeli plans to target Iranian nuclear assets, proving that Iran's precision weapons can reach Israel's strategic core.

## Wave 77: The Breakthrough of the Kheibar Shekan

The escalation sequence began after a ten-hour operational lull, which the IDF described as a temporary halt in Iranian launches. The silence was broken by a coordinated, multi-axis ballistic barrage launched from western Iran. 

The core of the strike package was the Kheibar Shekan, a third-generation solid-fuel missile known for its high terminal speed and maneuverability. While Channel 12 reported that some incoming missiles were intercepted and others landed in open areas, the military cabinet meeting convened by Netanyahu confirmed that the strikes achieved direct impacts within the Negev nuclear security zone, causing significant concern among defense planners.

---

## The Dual-Missile Formula: Gulf vs. Levantine Vectors

The IRGC's targeting strategy for Wave 77 utilized a dual-missile deployment formula designed to address different defensive layouts:
*   **The Gulf Vector**: Targeted U.S. coalition facilities using a combination of Qiam liquid-fuel missiles and Zolfaghar precision guided missiles, supplemented by suicide drone swarms.
*   **The Levantine Vector**: Targeted Israeli installations from north to south, relying on the high-speed, maneuvering Kheibar Shekan to bypass the Arrow-3 and David's Sling batteries.

This division of labor allowed the IRGC to match the specific characteristics of its missile inventory to the air defense environments of different target zones, maximizing the penetration rate of its primary warheads.

---

## Zolfaghar Strikes on Allied Gulf Bases

Simultaneously with the strikes on Israel, the IRGC targeted three major U.S. and coalition installations in the Gulf using Zolfaghar solid-fuel short-range ballistic missiles (SRBMs):
1.  **Ali Al Salem Air Base** (Kuwait).
2.  **Prince Sultan Air Base / Al-Kharj** (Saudi Arabia).
3.  **Al Dhafra Air Base** (UAE).

The Zolfaghar strikes utilized precision-guided warheads to target logistics hubs and hangar facilities. U.S. CENTCOM has maintained strict operational security regarding these strikes, refusing to release details of the precision defense systems used or the extent of the damage to the bases.

---

## Tel Nof and Al-Azraq: Targeting the F-35 Infrastructure

The air-dominance infrastructure of the U.S.-Israeli coalition was a primary focus of Wave 77. Following previous strikes on Ramat David Airbase, the IRGC directed its precision assets against two critical fighter bases:
*   **Tel Nof Airbase**: Located in central Israel, this base houses primary squadrons of F-15 and F-35 fighter jets. The strike targeted the hardened aircraft shelters (HAS) and maintenance facilities.
*   **Al-Azraq Airbase**: Located in eastern Jordan, serving as a key hosting facility for allied coalition fighter jets conducting patrols over western Iraq and the Jordan-Syria border.

By striking the home bases of the coalition's premier air-superiority fighters, Iran is attempting to disrupt the sorting and maintenance cycles required to sustain the air campaign.

---

## The Battle over Eilat: Intercepting the Qiam Decoy

The sole confirmed interception of the wave occurred over the southern port city of Eilat. Sirens sounded across the city and its surroundings as a single missile approached from the east. The IDF confirmed that the missile, identified as an older-generation Qiam, was successfully destroyed by an Arrow battery.

This interception, however, fits the IRGC's operational planning. The Qiam missile was deployed as a high-signature decoy designed to force the air defense radar to lock on and expend its interceptors. While the defense network was focused on neutralizing the Qiam over Eilat, the more advanced Kheibar Shekan warheads bypassed the tracking grid further north to reach Dimona.

---

## Breaching the Shimon Peres Negev Nuclear Research Center

The strategic center of the strike was the Shimon Peres Negev Nuclear Research Center, located in the sands of the northern Negev near Dimona. The facility houses Israel's nuclear reactor, plutonium production facilities, and underground reprocessing installations.

The Kheibar Shekan missiles penetrated the security zone surrounding the reactor, with impacts registered near Dimona and Yeruham. The fact that the air defense network did not claim any interceptions over the nuclear complex confirms that the maneuvering warheads bypassed the shield. The strike represents a deliberate demonstration of capability, proving that the physical core of Israel's strategic deterrence is within conventional striking range.

---

## Arad's Proximity: Staging Ground for Nuclear Personnel

The city of Arad, located approximately 35 km northeast of the Dimona reactor, is an integral part of the nuclear complex's security and logistical environment. Arad serves as a residential center for:
*   **Nuclear scientists** and technical personnel working at the reactor.
*   **Military officers** and security personnel assigned to the Negev defense sector.
*   **Mossad and intelligence operatives** associated with the defense of the program.

The strike on Arad, which caused severe damage to residential structures, directly targeted the human infrastructure of the nuclear program. By bringing the war to the neighborhoods where the reactor's staff live, Iran is demonstrating that the safety of the program's personnel can no longer be guaranteed.

---

## The Collapse of the Nuclear Safe Zone

Prior to Wave 77, the 60-kilometer security zone surrounding Dimona was treated by Israeli defense planners as an untouchable sanctuary. It was assumed that any conventional attack on the nuclear heartland would trigger an escalation that no adversary would risk.

The Kheibar Shekan strikes have shattered this safe zone. The strikes targeted the logistical and human environment of the reactor, demonstrating that the nuclear threshold has been crossed conventionally. This penetration forces Israel to face the reality that its nuclear infrastructure can be systematically targeted and damaged by conventional precision missiles without triggering a nuclear response.

---

## Conclusion: The Iskander of the Middle East

The deployment of the Kheibar Shekan in Wave 77 has established its reputation as the Middle Eastern equivalent of the Russian Iskander missile—a highly maneuverable, solid-fuel platform capable of defeating Western air defense grids.

By successfully breaching the Dimona nuclear security zone while decoy systems drew fire over Eilat, the Kheibar Shekan has redefined the strategic balance. If the joint U.S.-Israeli air defense network cannot protect the Shimon Peres Nuclear Center from conventional strikes, the strategic viability of the entire campaign is in doubt. The path of escalation has reached the reactor gates, and the cost of further conflict may soon exceed the survival limits of the region's nuclear infrastructure.
