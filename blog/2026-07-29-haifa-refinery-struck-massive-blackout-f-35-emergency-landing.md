# Haifa Refinery Struck: Blackout and F-35 Emergency Landing

Iran has launched three heavy waves of ballistic missiles targeting northern, central, and southern Israel, bypassing air defense networks to disable the Haifa Oil Refinery and trigger blackouts. The escalation occurs as U.S. intelligence agencies distance themselves from President Trump's warnings of imminent threat. Concurrently, a damaged U.S. F-35 fighter jet made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia after being struck by Iranian air defenses, forcing Israel to lower its diplomatic terms and seek ceasefire talks with Lebanon.

## The Missile Salvos: Target Haifa, Ashdod, and Ashkelon

Despite Trump's efforts to freeze strikes on energy infrastructure to stabilize oil prices, the IRGC launched tripartite missile salvos across Israel. The strikes targeted maritime and energy hubs:
*   **Ashdod Seaport**: Missiles struck the harbor zone, disrupting logistics.
*   **Ashkelon Terminal**: Fuel transfer docks on the beach were damaged by direct hits.
*   **Haifa Industrial Zone**: Multiple warheads struck refining and storage sites.

The coordinate tracking demonstrates that Iran's military is systematically targeting Israel's domestic energy distribution network, creating immediate supply disruptions.

---

## Haifa Oil Refinery Disabled: Blasts and Widespread Blackouts

The most significant impact occurred at the Haifa Oil Refinery. A heavy ballistic warhead struck the refinery's central distillation tower, causing explosions that spread through the facility.

The strike disabled the refinery, halting all fuel processing operations. The damage also severed power links, causing blackouts across large sectors of Haifa. The loss of refining capacity forces Israel to import processed fuel, raising security and economic challenges during active operations.

---

## The Censorship Battle: Banning Refinery Images

Following the explosions, the IDF military censor issued orders banning local media from publishing images or reports concerning the destruction at the Haifa refinery. The objective was to prevent the IRGC from conducting post-strike damage assessments and to manage public concern.

Despite these restrictions, leaked visual evidence surfaced on Telegram, demonstrating the scale of the fires. These leaks forced the Israeli public to confront the vulnerability of their critical infrastructure, undermining official claims of air defense superiority.

---

## The Intelligence Rift: DIA Takes Distance from Trump

The escalation has highlighted a rift between Trump and the U.S. intelligence community. Officials from the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) leaked assessments indicating that Trump's claims of an imminent Iranian threat to the U.S. homeland were politically motivated and lacked intelligence support.

Trump has responded by distancing his administration from Israel's goals, declaring that "Israel's target list is not America's target list." This statement follows the initial "division of labor" agreement, where Israel focused on northern/western Iran (missiles and nuclear sites) while the U.S. operated in the east and south (Hormuz naval operations).

---

## F-35 Emergency Landing: Iranian Air Defenses Claim Success

In a separate development, CNN reported that a U.S. F-35 Lightning II stealth fighter jet sustained damage during operations over the region. The aircraft was forced to execute an emergency landing at Prince Sultan Airbase in Saudi Arabia.

This incident represents the first time that Iranian air defense systems have successfully engaged and damaged an F-35. The successful strike confirms that Tehran's radar and air defense networks have bypassed Western stealth technology, matching the previous tracking breakthroughs achieved against B-2 bombers.

---

## De-escalation Signals: Netanyahu Requests Ceasefire Talks

Faced with the loss of its primary refinery and the failure of ground operations in southern Lebanon, the Israeli government has modified its diplomatic stance. Netanyahu has requested the immediate resumption of ceasefire negotiations with Lebanon.

Using French diplomatic mediation, Israel is seeking an agreement to halt the conflict along its northern border. This request shows how the combination of deep rocket strikes and armored losses in Lebanon has affected Israel's strategic planning, forcing it to seek de-escalation.

---

## Conclusion: The Limits of the Joint Strike Strategy

The ballistic strike on the Haifa refinery and the emergency landing of the F-35 show the limits of the U.S.-Israeli air campaign. Despite their technology, the coalition has not been able to protect its own assets from Iranian retaliation.

As General Jabbari warned that Iran has used only a small portion of its rocket arsenal, the strategic risks are clear. The vulnerability of Israel's energy infrastructure suggests that continuing the campaign will carry high economic and military costs, forcing both Washington and Tel Aviv to reconsider their regional goals.
