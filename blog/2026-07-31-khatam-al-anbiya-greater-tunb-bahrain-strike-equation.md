# Hormuz Equation: Greater Tunb vs. Bahrain

Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters has issued a warning that any strike on Iranian infrastructure will be met with "asymmetric, overwhelming superiority" that will target Gulf infrastructure. The warning follows the establishment of a "90-minute equation": after U.S. forces struck Greater Tunb for 90 to 110 minutes, Iran responded by bombarding U.S. installations in Bahrain. The escalation occurred as the UAE rejected calls to join the naval operations.

## The Infrastructure Warning: Khatam al-Anbiya Issues Stern Alert

Iran's central military command has adjusted its guidelines regarding infrastructure targets:
*   **The Warning**: Khatam al-Anbiya Headquarters warned against targeting Iranian utilities.
*   **Overwhelming Superiority**: The command declared that responses will not be restricted to proportional scale.
*   **No Hesitation**: Planners stated that strategic targets in the Gulf will face immediate strikes.

---

## The Ninety-Minute Equation: Greater Tunb Standoff Leads to Bahrain Hits

The conflict over the disputed Gulf islands has generated a direct retaliatory link:
*   **Greater Tunb Strike**: U.S. forces conducted a 90-to-110 minute bombardment of Greater Tunb.
*   **Bahrain Response**: Iran responded by targeting U.S. navy assets in Bahrain.
*   **The Link**: Tehran declared that operations against the islands will trigger strikes on Bahrain's infrastructure.

---

## Fifth Fleet Targeted: Bombarding the Main U.S. Naval Node in the Gulf

The retaliatory strikes targeted the primary command center for U.S. naval operations:
*   **Manama Base**: Precision salvos hit logistics yards near the U.S. 5th Fleet headquarters.
*   **Command Disruption**: The strikes forced command staff to activate backup bunker installations.
*   **Asymmetric Scale**: The volume of the response aimed to demonstrate the vulnerability of the base.

---

## UAE Refusal: Abu Dhabi Rejects the Island Liberation Campaign

U.S. planners attempted to leverage the dispute over the three islands to draw the UAE into the war:
*   **Island Coalition**: The U.S. urged the UAE to launch a ground campaign on Greater Tunb.
*   **Abu Dhabi Rejection**: UAE leadership refused, characterizing the proposal as a tactical trap.
*   **Diplomatic Stance**: The UAE maintained that the island dispute must be resolved through diplomacy.

---

## Gulf Diplomatic Stance: Qatar and Oman Avoid Direct Participation

Regional partner states have maintained a neutral stance to prevent domestic escalation:
*   **Doha Mediation**: Qatar restricted U.S. combat flights from Al Udeid to avoid Iranian counter-strikes.
*   **Oman Neutrality**: Muscat closed its airspace to offensive sorties targeting southern Iran.
*   **Local Stance**: Both states emphasized the necessity of maintaining channels with Tehran.

---

## JD Vance Criticism: Disagreements Inside the U.S. Administration

The escalation has highlighted internal policy divisions within the U.S. executive branch:
*   **Vance Statement**: Deputy Secretary JD Vance criticized the reliance on military options.
*   **Israel Policy**: Vance warned that supporting unilateral plans could exhaust U.S. assets.
*   **Diplomatic Path**: The statement advocated for a return to the parameters of the memorandum.

---

## Gulf Infrastructure Risk: Targeting Energy and Water Installations

Iranian planners have updated their targeting banks to include regional critical infrastructure:
*   **Energy Nodes**: Power generation and grid links are marked for potential strikes.
*   **Desalination Plants**: Water supply installations are included in the expanded target list.
*   **Economic Impact**: The threat targets the infrastructure supporting Gulf economic centers.

---

## Strategic Redefinition: Bypassing Proportionality in Gulf Confrontations

The latest round of clashing has altered the baseline of regional deterrence:
*   **Asymmetric Doctrine**: Iranian forces have abandoned the "measure-for-measure" response baseline.
*   **Target Expansion**: Strikes now target command nodes in response to local shipping incidents.
*   **Containment Limits**: The shift limits the effectiveness of U.S. maritime patrols in the Gulf.

---

## Conclusion: The Vulnerabilities of Integrated Regional Outposts

The infrastructure warning from Khatam al-Anbiya and the Greater Tunb-Bahrain equation show the vulnerabilities of integrated regional outposts.

By establishing a direct link between operations on Greater Tunb and strikes on U.S. bases in Bahrain, Iran has challenged the containment network. The UAE's refusal to join the campaign and JD Vance's criticism of military options confirm that the U.S. alliance structure faces internal strains. As both sides evaluate their infrastructure vulnerabilities, these developments show that Gulf containment cannot be enforced without putting local bases at risk.
