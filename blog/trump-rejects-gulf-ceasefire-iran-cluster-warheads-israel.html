Trump Rejects Gulf Ceasefire as Iran Shifts to Clusters

July 29, 2026 · 7 min read
Khalid Naami
Founder, Owner, and CEO at Dashboard Options

President Trump has rejected a joint Qatari-Emirati ceasefire initiative, backed quietly by Saudi Arabia, declaring that a halt to operations is "not on the table." In the same 24-hour window, Iran doubled its missile launch rate against Israel to 20 missiles per day, with 70% carrying cluster warheads designed for maximum area damage. IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamiri stated publicly that "we have not even reached the halfway point," while Israeli Channel 12 called it the hardest day of the war. The convergence of diplomatic rejection, escalating firepower, and a promised U.S. ground operation on Kharg Island signals that this conflict is entering a new and more dangerous phase.

The Gulf Ceasefire Initiative: Qatar and UAE Move​

Emir Tamim of Qatar and President Mohammed bin Zayed of the UAE launched a coordinated diplomatic initiative to halt the war immediately. The proposal, supported by contacts with the Russian leadership, called for an immediate ceasefire and negotiations under fire, a framework that Iran indicated it could accept.

The initiative represented a significant break from the Gulf states' earlier posture. Both Qatar and the UAE had been targeted by Iranian retaliatory strikes on their energy infrastructure, giving them direct motivation to end the conflict. Saudi Arabia and Kuwait, while not formally joining the initiative, were waiting for its success before attaching their names.

Trump's Rejection: "Not on the Table"​

Trump dismissed the Gulf initiative outright, siding instead with IDF Chief Zamiri's assessment that the war must continue. This rejection carries several implications:

  • Gulf allies humiliated: Qatar and the UAE invested significant diplomatic capital, including Russian coordination, only to be publicly overruled.
  • Saudi and Kuwaiti bases remain active: Trump reinforced operations at Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait and Prince Sultan Air Base (Al Kharj) in Saudi Arabia, signaling that these countries remain operational platforms regardless of their diplomatic preferences.
  • Zamiri over Trump's own timeline: Trump had previously claimed the war would set Iran back ten years. Zamiri publicly contradicted this, stating the damage amounts to eight months of capacity, and that without continuation, Israel would face another round within a year. Trump accepted Zamiri's assessment over his own.

Iran Strikes Aviation Fuel Depots in Kuwait and Saudi Arabia​

In a direct operational response to the Gulf states' failure to secure a ceasefire, Iran struck aviation fuel storage depots at both Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait and Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia. These strikes targeted the critical logistics chain: without jet fuel, the U.S. and Israeli air campaigns cannot sustain their sortie rates.

The strikes serve a dual purpose. Militarily, they degrade the operational capacity of forward-deployed air wings. Politically, they punish Kuwait and Saudi Arabia for continuing to host U.S. strike operations despite the diplomatic push for peace. The message to Riyadh and Kuwait City is clear: hosting American operations makes your infrastructure a legitimate target.

The 70% Cluster Shift: Fewer Warheads, Greater Destruction​

Israeli defense sources, confirmed by security correspondent Eyal Zamir, revealed that 70% of Iranian missiles now carry cluster munitions. This represents a fundamental shift in Iran's targeting doctrine:

  • Cluster warheads: Each missile disperses fewer but larger submunitions, each capable of producing wider blast damage upon ground impact.
  • Area denial: Unlike precision strikes against point targets (refineries, radar stations), cluster munitions are designed to saturate large areas, maximizing casualties and infrastructure damage.
  • Psychological impact: Israeli Channel 12 described the current phase as "the hardest days of the war," driven by the shift from precision to area-effect weapons.

The Israeli military noted that while the individual submunition count per missile is lower than standard cluster configurations, each warhead is significantly larger, producing greater destructive force per impact point.

20 Missiles Per Day: The Redirect from Gulf to Israel​

Iranian missile launches against Israel doubled to approximately 20 per day, roughly twice the rate of the previous phase. Israeli defense analysts attributed this increase to a deliberate redirect: Iran reduced its strikes on Gulf states and concentrated firepower on Israel.

Since the beginning of the war, Iran has conducted approximately 400 launch operations. The redirect toward Israel coincides with the Gulf ceasefire initiative, suggesting that Tehran is using the reduced pressure on the Gulf as leverage to encourage Qatar and the UAE to press harder for a diplomatic solution, while simultaneously punishing Israel for rejecting negotiations.

Zamiri's Admission: "Not Even Halfway"​

IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamiri's public statement that the war has not reached its midpoint is the most significant Israeli military assessment to date. The implications are severe:

  • Eight months of damage, not ten years: Zamiri confirmed that the combined U.S.-Israeli campaign has degraded Iranian military capacity by approximately eight months of production, far short of Trump's claim of a decade-long setback.
  • Another round inevitable: Israeli security sources stated that if the war stops now, Israel will face another confrontation with Iran within eight months to a year.
  • Continuation imperative: The Israeli military establishment is pushing for extended operations, including a naval and air blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and the Caspian Sea, estimating that control of Hormuz and Kharg Island would take approximately two weeks.

Trump's Promise: A Ground Operation on Kharg Island​

The most consequential development is Trump's reported promise to Netanyahu: a joint ground operation targeting Kharg Island and the Iranian coastline to break Tehran's control over the Strait of Hormuz. This represents the maximum escalation scenario.

The strategic logic is that a naval blockade combined with a ground seizure of Kharg, through which 90% of Iran's oil exports flow, would trigger an economic collapse that could bring down the Iranian government. In exchange, Trump agreed to Netanyahu's demand to avoid striking oil and gas infrastructure directly, focusing instead on industrial and missile manufacturing facilities.

The Israeli-Iranian Target Bank War​

Both sides are now engaged in a parallel industrial targeting campaign:

  • Israel struck: Military-industrial facilities across Tehran and central Iran, including Industrial Zone C in Isfahan, where the IRGC Air Force commander was reportedly present.
  • Iran responded: With precision strikes against Israeli radar installations, satellite communication nodes, and the deputy chief of military intelligence, using Ghadr and Khaybar Shekan missiles in coordinated waves (Wave 9).
  • Israeli sortie reduction: Israeli air operations dropped by approximately 30% from previous phases due to Iranian strikes on radar and satellite communication infrastructure, forcing a target bank update from field-level intelligence rather than signals intelligence.

Gulf Realignment: Qatar and UAE Isolated​

Trump's rejection of the ceasefire initiative has created a new dynamic among Gulf states:

  • Qatar and UAE: Diplomatically wounded, having coordinated with Russia and put their proposal on Trump's desk, only to be dismissed.
  • Saudi Arabia and Kuwait: Waiting on the sidelines, unwilling to formally join an initiative that Trump rejected, but increasingly uncomfortable with hosting operations that draw Iranian retaliation.
  • Russia: Supports the Gulf ceasefire initiative as part of its broader strategy to freeze Western operations in the Middle East.

The result is a potential isolation of Qatar and the UAE from the U.S.-Israeli war coalition, with both states potentially restricting base access or reducing logistical support as their own populations absorb the consequences of continued conflict.

Conclusion: The War Deepens​

Trump's rejection of the Gulf ceasefire initiative, combined with Zamiri's admission that the war is far from over, signals that the conflict is entering its most intensive phase. Iran's shift to cluster warheads and the doubling of its missile rate against Israel indicate that Tehran is prepared to absorb losses while inflicting maximum damage. The promised ground operation on Kharg Island, if executed, would represent the largest U.S. amphibious operation since the 2003 invasion of Iraq, with consequences that could reshape the entire region.
