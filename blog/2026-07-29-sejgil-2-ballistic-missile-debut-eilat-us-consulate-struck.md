# Sejgil-2 Debut: Eilat Struck, US Consulate Hit

The current conflict has seen the operational debut of Iran's Sejgil (سجيل) series, a two-stage, solid-fuel medium-range ballistic missile (MRBM). While used in previous rounds, the Sejgil has made its first appearance in the current campaign, with launches targeting Eilat's "Black Industrial Zone." Concurrently, consecutive missile waves directed at Tel Aviv have resulted in heavy structural damage to the U.S. Consulate building, highlighting the intensity of the latest salvos.

## The Combat Debut: Sejgil Ballistic Missiles Enter the Conflict

Iranian state television officially announced that the IRGC has integrated the Sejgil ballistic missile series into its active operations. Although Sejgil units were deployed during Operation True Promise 3 on June 18, 2025, they had not been utilized in the current round of fighting until now.

The deployment of the Sejgil series represents a significant escalation in the warhead payload and terminal velocity directed at coalition coordinates. The missile's high-speed reentry profile has prompted Israeli military investigators to audit the guidance systems utilized during the latest strike.

---

## Eilat’s Black Industrial Zone Targeted

The target of the first Sejgil strike was the "Black Industrial Zone" in the southern port city of Eilat. The zone contains critical logistics, storage, and industrial infrastructure that supports both civilian trade and military staging.

Despite local air defense coverage, the warhead penetrated the airspace and impacted the industrial sector. The strike caused extensive fires and structural damage, demonstrating the capability of long-range ballistic missiles to hit specific industrial assets at distances exceeding 2,000 kilometers from launch points in central Iran.

---

## The Technical Audit: Sejgil-1 vs. Sejgil-2 Guidance

Israeli military and intelligence services are conducting an audit to determine whether the missile was a Sejgil-1 or the newer Sejgil-2 variant:
*   **Sejgil-1**: The baseline variant has a Circular Error Probable (CEP) of approximately 50 meters, with its warhead remaining unguided during the terminal phase of flight.
*   **Sejgil-2**: The upgraded variant features aerodynamic control surfaces (fins) on the warhead, reducing the CEP to under 10 meters. It utilizes a combined guidance system of Inertial Navigation (INS) and GPS, operating solely on INS during the final phase to bypass GPS jamming.

---

## Penetrating Air Defenses: Evasion of Arrow-3 Networks

The Sejgil's terminal reentry velocity of Mach 11 to Mach 13–14 presents a severe challenge for terminal air defense systems. The high velocity reduces detection times and leaves little margin for interceptor calculations.

While Israeli defense officials claimed that the Arrow-3 (Hetz-3) system intercepted the threat, local reports and damage tracking confirmed the warhead impacted its target. The penetration of the defensive envelope shows that current interceptor models cannot consistently neutralize high-velocity, maneuvering solid-fuel targets. Only the under-development Arrow-4 system is designed to engage threats in this speed class.

---

## Solid-Fuel Propulsion: Strategic Speed and Mobility

The defining feature of the Sejgil is its two-stage, solid-fuel composite rocket motor. Solid-fuel propulsion offers significant advantages over older liquid-fueled missiles like the Shahab series:
*   **Launch Readiness**: Solid-fuel missiles do not require fueling before launch and can be prepared and fired from mobile launchers within minutes.
*   **Mobility**: The stable solid fuel allows launchers to transit rough terrain without risk of fuel leaks, making the batteries difficult to locate and destroy before launch.
*   **Safety**: The composite fuel is safer to store and handle, reducing the logistical footprint of launch units.

---

## Strike on the U.S. Consulate in Tel Aviv

The deployment of the Sejgil occurred alongside a continuous 90-minute wave of attacks targeting central Israel since midnight. Heavy missile strikes directed at Tel Aviv resulted in a direct hit near the U.S. Consulate building.

A large shrapnel fragment from a heavy ballistic missile impacted the facility, causing significant structural damage to the consulate's exterior and offices. While Israel claimed to have intercepted the incoming missile, the size of the fragment indicates a heavy warhead was involved, and the strike coincided with cluster rocket salvos launched by Hezbollah against northern coordinates.

---

## Conclusion: The Expanding Range of Iranian Missiles

The combat debut of the Sejgil-2 in the current war highlights the limitations of the coalition's defensive network. By demonstrating the ability to prepare, launch, and guide a Mach 13 solid-fuel missile from central Iran to Eilat in under seven minutes, the IRGC has established a persistent strategic threat.

As the U.S. and Israel assess the damage to Eilat and the Tel Aviv consulate, the strategic calculations for the conflict are changing. The availability of high-velocity, solid-fuel systems suggests that future targets will remain vulnerable to precision strikes, regardless of the density of the air defense networks deployed to protect them.
