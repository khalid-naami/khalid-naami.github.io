# Prince Sultan Hit: U.S. Tankers Damaged by Missile

A single Iranian precision-guided ballistic missile has successfully targeted the Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia. The strike bypassed local air defense batteries, wounding 12 U.S. personnel and damaging five aerial refueling tankers. This strike, occurring in the interior of the Arabian Peninsula, highlights the vulnerability of U.S. air operations. It comes alongside a leaked U.S. intelligence report confirming that despite thousands of coalition strikes, only one-third of Iran's ballistic missile inventory has been destroyed or disabled.

## The Friday Strike: Prince Sultan Air Base Hit

The strike on Prince Sultan Air Base occurred last Friday afternoon. An Iranian ballistic missile, launched from a hardened silo in western Iran, traveled across the Gulf and bypassed the Patriot and THAAD air defense batteries protecting the installation:
*   **The Impact**: The missile's warhead detonated in the vicinity of the active tarmac and refueling bays.
*   **Airfield Damage**: The blast created a large crater, scattering shrapnel across the refueling pads.
*   **Tactical Failure**: The failure to intercept the missile confirms that Iran's latest guidance systems can bypass established regional air defense grids.

---

## The Wounded Discrepancy: WSJ vs. Reuters Reports

Initial media reports regarding casualties at the base reflected a deliberate effort by U.S. and Saudi commands to manage the news:
*   **The WSJ Version**: The *Wall Street Journal* reported that ten U.S. soldiers were wounded in the strike, including two with life-threatening injuries.
*   **The Reuters Version**: *Reuters* reported that 12 U.S. personnel were injured.
*   **The Discrepancy**: The difference was due to the presence of two high-ranking officers: a senior U.S. commander and a representative from the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) who were wounded alongside two Saudi officers. The administration initially withheld their identities due to operational security.

## Refueling Wing Damaged: Five U.S. Tankers Disabled

The primary objective of the strike was the base's aerial refueling wing, which supports U.S. tactical aircraft executing missions over Iran:
*   **Tankers hit**: Five U.S. Air Force KC-135 and KC-46 refueling tankers sustained severe shrapnel damage while parked on the tarmac.
*   **Fuel ignition**: The blast punctured fuel lines on two tankers, triggering secondary fires that damaged the airframes.
*   **Sortie reduction**: The loss of these aircraft restricts the U.S. Air Force's capability to sustain long-range sorties over western Iran, forcing aircraft to rely on carrier-based refueling assets.

## The 1% Threat: Trump's Cabinet Mathematical Warning

The successful strike on a major base like Prince Sultan illustrates a mathematical warning Trump delivered during a televised cabinet meeting on Thursday.

Trump noted: "Even if our air campaign manages to destroy 99% of Iran's missile arsenal, a remaining 1% is unacceptable. That 1% represents a single missile that can bypass our defenses and hit the hull of a $1 billion U.S. warship or a major airbase." The strike in Saudi Arabia proves that even a single missile lock can bypass air defense systems and disable critical assets.

## The Leaked Intelligence: One-Third of Iran's Arsenal Destroyed

A confidential U.S. intelligence report leaked to *Reuters* by four anonymous officials confirms the limits of the coalition's strategic bombing campaign:
*   **Confirmed destruction**: U.S. and Israeli intelligence can only verify the destruction of approximately **one-third (33%)** of Iran's total ballistic missile arsenal.
*   **Damaged or buried**: Another third is estimated to have sustained minor damage or been buried in underground mountain tunnels.
*   **Intact inventory**: The final third remains fully intact, hidden inside reinforced underground silos (missile cities) that are invulnerable to current air strikes.

## Moulton's Warning: Strategic Retention of the Missile Inventory

Representative Seth Moulton (Democrat) commented on the intelligence report, warning against underestimating Iran's operational capacity:
*   **Controlled deployment**: Moulton noted that Iran is intentionally holding back its primary strategic assets.
*   **Tactical patience**: Tehran is not deploying its entire inventory but is conserving its best missiles and drones to sustain a prolonged war of attrition.
*   **The Threat**: This strategy ensures that Iran will maintain its strike capability even if the U.S. doubles its target list, waiting for the U.S. to deplete its interceptor stockpiles.

## Rubio's Assessment: Two to Four Weeks of High Attrition

Incoming U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio addressed the conflict's timeline, stating that the war is expected to continue for another **two to four weeks**:
*   **High intensity**: Rubio warned that the remaining weeks will involve intense air and missile operations.
*   **The post-war challenge**: He emphasized that the primary challenge after the cessation of hostilities will be securing the Strait of Hormuz.
*   **The toll threat**: Rubio described Iran's proposal to impose tolls on transit through the strait as "illegal and dangerous to the global economy," confirming that the U.S. will resist any Iranian maritime authority in the Gulf.

---

## The Chinese Turning: Blocking Container Transits in Hormuz

To enforce its blockade, Iran has begun targeting vessels linked to nations supporting the U.S. coalition, leading to a significant diplomatic incident:
*   **Chinese vessels turned back**: The IRGC Navy intercepted and turned back two Chinese-owned container vessels attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz.
*   **The Message**: By blocking Chinese shipping, Tehran is demonstrating that its blockade will be enforced strictly, regardless of diplomatic ties.
*   **Strategic leverage**: The action forces Beijing to press Washington to seek a rapid ceasefire, as Chinese exporters face rising shipping costs due to the closure of the Gulf route.

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Regional Staging Hubs

The ballistic missile strike on Prince Sultan Air Base and the leaked intelligence report on Iran's remaining arsenal confirm the limits of the U.S. regional strategy.

By bypassing air defense networks and damaging five refueling tankers, Iran has proved that the continental bases in the Gulf are vulnerable to precision interdiction. With only one-third of the Iranian missile inventory confirmed destroyed, the U.S. faces a prolonged campaign of attrition. As Rubio predicts another two to four weeks of conflict, the Pentagon must accept that continuing the war from regional staging hubs will result in further casualties and the degradation of its strategic transport and refueling assets.
