# U.S. Grand Nuclear Package Proposal Sent to Tehran

The Pakistani Army Chief has arrived in Tehran carrying a comprehensive package proposal from the U.S. administration designed to end the military standoff. The document outlines a tiered system linking the duration of Iran's uranium enrichment freeze to specific levels of sanctions relief and Western capital investment. The proposal comes as negotiators in Islamabad attempt to bridge the gap between Iran's initial five-year freeze offer and the fifteen-year compromise approved by Israel.

## ## The Pakistani Army Chief Shuttle: Delivering the U.S. Draft

The diplomatic channel in Islamabad has utilized regional military leadership to convey the proposal:
*   **The Mission**: The Chief of Staff of the Pakistani Army travelled to Tehran to deliver the official U.S. proposal.
*   **Direct Delivery**: The draft was presented directly to President Masoud Pezeshkian and security advisors.
*   **Mediator Role**: Pakistan has acted as the primary intermediary, leveraging its security relationship with Saudi Arabia and China.

---

## ## Uranium Enrichment Durations: The 10-to-15 Year Compromise

The core of the negotiation is the duration of the uranium enrichment freeze:
*   **Iran's Position**: Tehran initially proposed a five-year freeze on high-level enrichment.
*   **U.S. Demand**: The White House, under Israeli pressure, demanded a twenty-year suspension.
*   **The Compromise**: The draft proposes a duration of 10 to 12 years, with Israel agreeing to a 15-year maximum target.

---

## ## Sanctions Scaling: The Zero-Sanctions 15-Year Tier

The U.S. proposal features a tiered system linking freeze duration to economic benefits:
*   **Five-Year Freeze**: High-level sanctions remain active, with only minor humanitarian exemptions.
*   **Ten-Year Freeze**: Partial sanctions relief, with approximately 15% of secondary sanctions remaining in place.
*   **Fifteen-Year Freeze**: Complete sanctions relief (zero sanctions) and the removal of restrictions on Iranian banking.

---

## ## Western Investment: Opening Iran's Oil and Petrochemicals

The major economic incentive in the draft is the reentry of Western corporations:
*   **Capital Reentry**: A 15-year freeze would permit Western oil and petrochemical firms to invest in Iran.
*   **Infrastructure Reconstruction**: The package includes provisions for U.S. and European companies to rebuild Iran's energy infrastructure.
*   **Technology Transfer**: The deal would lift restrictions on importing civilian energy technologies.

---

## ## Hormuz Revenue and Oversight: The Shared Transit Proposal

The maritime agreement is linked to the nuclear resolution:
*   **Transit Sharing**: The U.S. seeks a joint revenue-sharing and oversight model for the Strait of Hormuz.
*   **Omani Rejection**: Since Oman refused to share its transit revenues, the U.S. is seeking a portion of Iran's share.
*   **Oversight Grid**: The draft includes provisions for joint monitoring of commercial transit in the Persian Gulf.

---

## ## Internal Realignment: Pezeshkian's Economic Strains

The blockade has created internal political debates within the Iranian administration:
*   **Economic Strain**: President Pezeshkian faces pressure due to the blockade's impact on state wage payments.
*   **Civilian Concerns**: The civilian administration seeks a resolution to secure sanctions relief and stabilize the currency.
*   **Military Stance**: The IRGC maintains that any restriction on Iranian shipping must be met with a complete closure of the strait.

---

## ## The Legislative Safeguard: Ghalibaf and Parliamentary Review

Iran's parliament holds veto power over any final agreement:
*   **Ghalibaf's Role**: Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf has insisted on a formal review of the draft.
*   **Legislative Approval**: The Iranian team stated that no deal will be signed without a vote in the Majles.
*   **Constitutional Review**: Parliament seeks to ensure that any freeze does not compromise Iran's sovereign nuclear rights.

---

## ## Meticulous Drafting: U.S. Experts Revise the Iranian Text

The technical quality of the documents has required detailed review by U.S. planners:
*   **Iranian Drafts**: Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi's team submitted drafts that U.S. analysts described as highly detailed.
*   **Legal Scrutiny**: U.S. research centers spent several days reviewing the text to identify potential loopholes.
*   **Revision Phase**: The U.S. team revised the drafts to ensure that verification mechanisms are clearly defined.

---

## ## Conclusion: The Structural Parameters of the Grand Deal

The Pakistani Army Chief's shuttle mission to Tehran has defined the framework for a potential U.S.-Iran agreement.

By linking sanctions relief directly to the duration of the uranium enrichment freeze, the U.S. package seeks to secure a long-term nuclear freeze. While President Pezeshkian faces economic pressure, Ghalibaf's insistence on legislative review ensures that any deal must protect Iran's sovereignty. As U.S. experts review the detailed Iranian drafts, the success of the package depends on resolving the dispute over Hormuz transit fees and the final duration of the enrichment freeze.
