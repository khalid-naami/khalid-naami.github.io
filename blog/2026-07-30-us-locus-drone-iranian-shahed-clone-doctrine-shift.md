# U.S. Locus Drone: Cloning Iran's Shahed-136

The entry of low-cost kamikaze drones has transformed modern defense procurement, forcing Western militaries to copy Iranian technology. An analysis by the Ukrainian defense outlet *Defense Express* has detailed how the U.S. developed its own direct analog to the Iranian Shahed-136, named "Locus" (لوكاس). Used in combat during recent Gulf strikes, the $35,000 U.S. clone represents a significant shift in Pentagon planning toward mass-produced, expendable strike platforms.

## The Low-Cost Attack Doctrine: America's Locus Clone

The U.S. military has adapted its strike programs to include low-cost, long-range platforms:
*   **The Locus Drone**: The U.S. Air Force has deployed the Locus, a direct reverse-engineered copy of Iran's Shahed-136.
*   **Unit Cost**: Each Locus unit is priced at approximately $35,000, aligning with the low-cost attack doctrine.
*   **Asymmetric Strikes**: The platform allows U.S. forces to execute saturated strikes without expending multi-million dollar cruise missiles.

## Defense Express Disclosures: The Cost of Reverse Engineering

The Ukrainian defense outlet *Defense Express* has published details regarding the development budget:
*   **Low Development Cost**: Unlike traditional U.S. programs, the Locus project cost only tens of millions of dollars to develop.
*   **Simple Components**: The design avoids expensive Western components, utilizing commercial off-the-shelf electronics to lower unit costs.
*   **Rapid Scale**: The low budget has enabled the rapid acquisition of initial production batches.

## Scorpion Strike Task Force: Scribing the First Squad

The Locus drone has been integrated into active operational units in the Middle East:
*   **Task Force**: The drones are operated by the newly established "Scorpion Strike" Task Force (TFS).
*   **Deployment**: The first operational squadron was deployed to U.S. bases in the region in late 2025.
*   **Mission Profile**: The squadron is tasking with executing long-range strikes on hostile radar installations and assembly zones.

## The Horowitz Files: From Ukrainian Loot to Pentagon Labs

The origin of the Locus program lies in the capture of Iranian hardware in the European theater:
*   **The Transfer**: In early 2024, Ukrainian intelligence transferred captured Shahed-136 wreckage to U.S. defense laboratories.
*   **Michael Horowitz**: The program was managed by Michael Horowitz, then Director of the Emerging Capabilities Policy Office at the Pentagon.
*   **Reverse Engineering**: Horowitz's team dismantled the wreckage to map the guidance, engine, and structural profiles of the drone.

## Unprecedented Speed: Concept to Combat in Under Two Years

The development timeline of the Locus drone represents an exception to standard U.S. procurement:
*   **The Timeline**: The U.S. took one year from receiving the captured hardware to developing the first flying prototype.
*   **Combat Entry**: The drone was deployed in active combat operations in under two years from the program's initiation.
*   **System Shift**: This rapid turnaround bypassed standard multi-year testing protocols, showing the urgency felt by planners.

## French Counter-Drone Strategy: Arming the MQ-9 Reaper

To counter the Shahed threat, France has adapted its existing drone fleet:
*   **The Test**: On April 2, the French DGA conducted tests near Île du Levant in the Mediterranean.
*   **Armed Reaper**: France modified its MQ-9A Reaper drones to carry AGM-114 Hellfire missiles configured for air-to-air drone interception.
*   **Interception Success**: The test successfully intercepted and destroyed a Banshee target drone simulating a Shahed flight profile.

## Conclusion: The Global Proliferation of the Shahed Model

The development of the U.S. Locus drone and the adaptation of French systems confirm that the Iranian Shahed-136 has reshaped global military planning.

By reverse-engineering captured Ukrainian samples in under two years, the Pentagon's Locus program has adopted a low-cost attack doctrine to bypass high manufacturing costs. The proliferation of the Shahed model shows that low-cost, mass-produced platforms have replaced complex systems as the primary tool of modern attrition warfare.
