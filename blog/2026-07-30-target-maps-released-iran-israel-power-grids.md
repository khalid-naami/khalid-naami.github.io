# Target Maps: Iran & Israel Threaten Power Grids

The threat of a regional energy shutdown has intensified following the release of detailed targeting maps by both Iranian and Israeli state-affiliated media. In response to an Israeli Channel 12 broadcast outlining U.S.-Israeli plans to target Iran's 110 gas-fired power plants, the Iranian state channel Al-Alam published a counter-map. This document pinpoints coordinates for key power stations in Israel and Jordan, indicating they will be targeted for destruction. The publication of these maps has fueled fears of a "Black Monday" on energy markets, with Brent crude projected to spike past $140 per barrel if either side executes these plans.

## The Map Standoff: Al-Alam vs. Channel 12

The media exchange began when Channel 12 correspondent Tomer Almagor presented a map listing the primary targets of the proposed U.S.-Israeli campaign against Iran's infrastructure. According to CNN data, the plan focuses on targeting Iran's 110 gas-fired power generation plants and its single operational nuclear reactor.

Al-Alam TV responded by publishing a detailed coordinate map of Jordan and Israel. The document identified specific power plants that are now included in the IRGC's target bank under the "Great Jihad" doctrine. This exchange of coordinates indicates that both military commands have integrated civilian power grids into their primary targeting structures.

---

## Alon Tavor: Striking a Chinese-Funded Hub

The first major target identified on the Iranian map is the Alon Tavor Power Station, located in the Alon Tavor industrial zone in northern Israel. The site contains two separate but connected facilities: a major private combined-cycle power plant and a smaller co-generation unit.

The site's ownership structure makes it an unexpected target:
*   **Operating Consortium**: Managed by the MRC Group, which includes China Harbour Engineering Company (CHEC), Mivtach Shamir Holdings, and Rapac Energy.
*   **Operational Capacity**: 583 MW, employing combined-cycle gas turbines with diesel backup for peak loads.
*   **Privatization**: The 1990s-era plant was privatized in 2019, with plans to expand its capacity.
*   **RD Energy**: The adjacent co-generation unit generating 70-74 MW using Siemens technology.

By listing Alon Tavor, Tehran is signaling that its strategic targeting is entirely independent. The presence of Chinese state capital (via CHEC) is not a shield; Iran will target the facility despite its relations with Beijing if U.S.-Israeli strikes hit its own grid.

---

## Eshkol and Eshkol 2: Israel's Largest Gas Power Hub

The second major Israeli target on the Al-Alam map is the Eshkol Power Station, located in the Ashdod industrial zone near the Lachish River. Generating 1,693 MW using natural gas, it is Israel's largest gas-fired power station.

The site is a major economic asset:
*   **Ownership**: Transferred in June 2024 from the state-owned Israel Electric Corporation (IEC) to Dalia Power Energies' subsidiary "Eshkol Power" for 9 billion shekels.
*   **Expansion (Eshkol 2)**: A $1.5 billion combined-cycle expansion project designed to generate an additional 850 MW, funded in 2025 and scheduled to start production in 2028-2029.

Striking the Eshkol complex would disable over 10% of Israel's total power generation capacity, cutting off the industrial and maritime infrastructure around Ashdod Port.

## Shams Ma'an: Targeting Jordan's Solar Grid

The Iranian map also extends into Jordan, identifying the Shams Ma'an solar power plant in the southern district. The 66 MW photovoltaic facility, opened in 2016 at a cost of $170 million, provides a significant portion of Jordan's renewable energy supply.

The plant is owned by an international consortium:
*   **Diamond Generating Europe**: A subsidiary of Japan's Mitsubishi Corporation.
*   **Nebras Power**: The Qatari international energy investment company.
*   **Kawar Group**: The Jordanian domestic partner.
*   **First Solar**: The American company that developed the site.

By targeting Shams Ma'an, Iran is issuing a direct warning to Amman regarding its cooperation with the U.S. military command. The strike would demonstrate that hosting U.S. assets carries immediate consequences for Jordan's domestic infrastructure.

## Iran's Power Grid: The WSJ and CNN Target Lists

The Israeli Channel 12 broadcast, referencing U.S. intelligence sources, detailed the targets in the U.S.-Israeli strike bank. The plan envisions targeting Iran's highly centralized gas-fired power grid to halt its industrial manufacturing.

According to the Wall Street Journal, the target list includes:
*   **Damavand Power Plant**: Located 70 km east of Tehran, this facility generates 4% of Iran's total electricity.
*   **Shahid Salimi (Neka) Power Plant**: The largest power plant in northern Iran, bordering the Caspian Sea.
*   **Shahid Rajaee Power Plant**: A major thermal and combined-cycle plant in Qazvin province.
*   **Kerman Power Plant**: A key generation hub in southeastern Iran.
*   **Ramin Power Plant**: The largest thermal power station in Khuzestan province, near Ahvaz.

## The Damavand and Russian-Built Bushehr Plants

The inclusion of the Bushehr Nuclear Power Plant on the U.S.-Israeli target list has caused severe diplomatic friction. Bushehr, operated in partnership with Russia's Rosatom, provides a portion of southwestern Iran's base power load.

Russian President Putin reportedly contacted regional leaders to express concern over the targeting of nuclear facilities, noting that striking Bushehr would create an ecological hazard in the Persian Gulf. However, the U.S. command has maintained the site on its options list, arguing that the reactor provides electrical power that supports the IRGC's missile command infrastructure.

## Gulf Energy Standoff: UAE, Saudi, Qatar, and Kuwait

If its own power grid is hit, Iran has threatened to target the energy infrastructure of the Gulf states. Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned that the destruction of Iran's grid will lead to the systematic disablement of the entire Gulf energy network.

This threat includes:
*   **Barakah Nuclear Power Plant**: The UAE's primary nuclear energy facility.
*   **Major thermal power plants**: Across Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Kuwait.
*   **Oil and gas processing facilities**: That support global exports.

Tehran's calculation is that the global economy cannot absorb the simultaneous loss of both Iranian and Gulf energy production, forcing the U.S. to choose between de-escalation and a global economic collapse.

---

## The Desalination Weapon: Water Security in the Gulf

A secondary, highly lethal component of the energy war is the vulnerability of the Gulf's water supply. The Gulf states rely on approximately 400 desalination plants to sustain their populations, all of which require continuous electrical power.

The level of dependence is absolute:
*   **Kuwait**: Relies on desalination for 90% of its drinking water.
*   **Oman**: Relies on desalination for 86% of its supply.
*   **Saudi Arabia**: Relies on desalination for 70% of its water.

An Iranian strike that disables the power grids supporting these desalination facilities would leave approximately 100 million people without access to drinking water within days. While some analysts discount this scenario as too extreme, the IRGC's "Great Jihad" statement confirms that these facilities are included in the retaliatory target bank.

## Conclusion: Symmetrical Destruction of Modern Grids

The publication of these target maps marks a transition to a war of total economic destruction. By identifying Alon Tavor, Eshkol, and Shams Ma'an, Iran has demonstrated that it has mapped Israel and Jordan's energy architecture with precision.

As the U.S. military evaluates the potential fallout and Brent crude prices climb toward the $140 threshold, the limits of military options are clear. Striking Iran's grid will not disable its military command; it will trigger a regional blackout that shuts down Israel's economy, cuts Jordan's power, and deprives the Gulf of both electricity and water. In the era of interconnected grids, the destruction of one country's power is the destruction of all.
