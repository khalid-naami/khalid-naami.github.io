# Araghchi Beijing Visit: China Post-War Plan

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has arrived in Beijing for a diplomatic visit, his first official travel to China since the U.S.-led military campaign began. The visit is focused on coordinating a new regional security architecture to manage the post-war phase in the Persian Gulf. Bypassing U.S. demands, China and Iran have co-drafted a security proposal designed to establish long-term stability based on regional sovereignty and economic integration.

## Landmark Visit: Araghchi Arrives in Beijing Amid Truce

The Iranian delegation's arrival in Beijing has drawn attention from regional observers:
*   **The Delegation**: Abbas Araghchi led a high-level diplomatic team, meeting with Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi.
*   **Timing**: The meetings took place during the active ceasefire, utilizing the window to coordinate political strategies.
*   **The Mission**: Bypassing Western frameworks to establish a regional security structure with Chinese backing.

---

## Rejecting the Fourteen Points: Iran Dismisses Surrender Terms

The coordination efforts are designed to counter demands proposed by Washington:
*   **The Fourteen Points**: The U.S. administration had proposed a 14-point plan requiring disarmament and shipping concessions.
*   **The Rejection**: Araghchi stated that Iran will not accept terms that limit its missile program or restrict transit rights.
*   **Sovereign Veto**: Tehran maintains that any agreement must recognize its national defense requirements.

---

## The Chinese Security Initiative: Four Core Principles

Beijing's proposal focuses on four central concepts to manage regional stability:
*   **The Proposal**: The Chinese government presented a four-point initiative for Gulf security.
*   **Core Concepts**: The plan emphasizes respect for international law, sovereignty, non-interference, and economic integration.
*   **Tension Reduction**: The initiative seeks to establish multilateral dialogue mechanisms to resolve disputes.

---

## The Five-Point Expansion: Pakistan's Diplomatic Integration

The proposal was expanded following consultations with other regional partners:
*   **Pakistan's Input**: Pakistani Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar visited Beijing to coordinate on the framework.
*   **The Fifth Point**: The plan was expanded to include joint anti-terrorism protocols along shared borders.
*   **Regional Bloc**: The coordination links Iran, Pakistan, and China in a unified diplomatic approach.

---

## Indivisible Security: Respecting Regional Sovereignty Limits

The framework is based on the concept of indivisible security:
*   **Indivisible Security**: The principle asserts that the security of one nation cannot be achieved at the expense of another.
*   **Opposing Coalitions**: The document opposes the creation of exclusive military alliances in the region.
*   **Sovereign Balance**: The plan requires that foreign forces respect the territorial limits of regional states.

---

## The UAE Mediation: President Xi's Negotiations with Abu Dhabi

China has used its diplomatic influence to manage tensions between Gulf neighbors:
*   **Abu Dhabi Talks**: President Xi Jinping met with UAE Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed in Beijing.
*   **Preventing Escalation**: Xi proposed the security framework to the UAE delegation to reduce tensions with Tehran.
*   **Economic Corridors**: The talks highlighted the integration of both nations into the Belt and Road Initiative.

---

## Nuclear Threshold Parity: The Japan-Iran Strategic Comparison

The negotiations have linked the Iranian program to international precedents:
*   **The Precedent**: Iranian diplomats argued that their nuclear status should match that of Japan.
*   **Threshold Status**: The framework proposes that Iran maintain threshold capabilities without weaponization.
*   **International Rights**: Tehran asserts that its peaceful nuclear program is a sovereign right under international supervision.

---

## Global Times Editorial: The Value of Precious Mutual Trust

Chinese state media has supported the diplomatic coordination:
*   **Mutual Trust**: The *Global Times* published an editorial describing the relationship with Tehran as based on "precious trust."
*   **Strategic Partner**: The editorial asserted that China will support its strategic partners against unilateral pressure.
*   **Alternative Order**: The media outlet argued that regional security should be managed by local states rather than foreign commands.

---

## Conclusion: The Emerging Sino-Iranian Post-War Framework

Abbas Araghchi's visit to Beijing highlights China's role in the post-war diplomacy of the Middle East.

By co-drafting the expanded five-point security proposal and rejecting the U.S. 14-point terms, Iran and China have established an alternative path for regional stability. The inclusion of Pakistan's anti-terror protocols and mediation efforts with the UAE show that Beijing is seeking to build a broad regional consensus. As the framework proposes nuclear threshold parity similar to Japan, the initiative confirms that future security arrangements will be influenced by Sino-Iranian cooperation.
