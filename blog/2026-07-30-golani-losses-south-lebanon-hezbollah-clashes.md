# Golani Bled: Israel's Ground Crisis in Lebanon

Israel's ground offensive in southern Lebanon is facing a severe operational crisis. A limited advance by the 91st and 36th Divisions into the second line of border villages was met with a series of coordinated, close-quarters counterattacks by Hezbollah, inflicting significant casualties. The IDF has officially confirmed the death of Sergeant Ori Greenberg of the Golani patrol unit during clashes in Deir Siriane, near Marjayoun. The tactical friction points to a deeper strategic vulnerability, as the Israeli military struggles to balance its ground operations in Lebanon with its air campaign against Iran.

## The Clash at Deir Siriane: Sgt. Greenberg's Death

The vulnerability of Israel's elite infantry was exposed during a search operation in the eastern sector village of Deir Siriane, located near Marjayoun. A patrol unit from the Golani Brigade entered a residential structure, triggering a close-quarters ambush:
*   **Direct engagement**: Hezbollah fighters engaged the patrol at point-blank range using small arms and hand grenades.
*   **Immediate fatality**: Sergeant Ori Greenberg (رقيب أوري غرينبرغ) sustained multiple fatal wounds.
*   **Logistical failure**: Due to intense fire from surrounding ridges, the field medical team could not evacuate Greenberg, forcing them to declare his death on the spot.

---

## The 36th Division: Casualties at Maroun al-Ras

Simultaneously, elements of the 36th Division encountered heavy resistance in the central sector. During an attempted clearing operation near Maroun al-Ras, a command post was targeted by a precision mortar and rocket strike:
*   **Command casualties**: The strike seriously wounded a reserve battalion commander (قائد كتيبة احتياط) and two reserve soldiers.
*   **Dangerous evacuations**: The intense fire prevented standard vehicular evacuation, requiring multiple hazardous helicopter sorties to extract the wounded.
*   **Defensive constraints**: The base of the 91st Division in the sector has been forced to shift from offensive maneuvers to static defense, limiting the advance.

---

## Sheikh Naim Qassem: Rejecting Negotiations Under Fire

On the political level, Hezbollah's leadership has hardened its stance. The group's Secretary-General, Sheikh Naim Qassem, delivered a major address rejecting any diplomatic negotiations while active combat operations continue.

This rejection of "negotiating under fire" represents a major challenge for U.S.-Israeli diplomatic efforts. The U.S. had hoped to use air strikes to pressure Hezbollah into accepting a unilateral withdrawal north of the Litani River. Qassem's statement confirms that the group is prepared to sustain the military confrontation indefinitely, neutralizing the diplomatic leverage of the air campaign.

---

## The Intelligence Blindspot: Who is Naim Qassem?

The resilience of Hezbollah's command structure highlights a significant intelligence failure for AMAN (Israeli Military Intelligence). Despite weeks of high-intensity operations and deep strikes across Lebanon, Israeli agencies have been unable to locate or target Sheikh Naim Qassem.

The failure to disrupt the leadership's communications confirms that Hezbollah's command network remains secure. The group has managed to prevent leaks and maintain its internal coordination, allowing it to execute complex ground maneuvers and coordinate missile strikes despite Israel's electronic surveillance dominance.

---

## The Air Force Overstretch: Lebanon vs. Iran

The primary structural constraint on Israel's military performance is the overstretch of the Israeli Air Force (IAF). The IAF is forced to divide its resources between two high-intensity campaigns:
*   **The Iranian Front**: Requiring long-range sorties, refueling coordination, and air defense suppression over Isfahan and western Iran.
*   **The Lebanese Front**: Requiring continuous close air support (CAS) and reconnaissance flights over the southern border.

Because the IAF cannot sustain both operations at peak capacity, the Lebanese front has experienced a reduction in close air support. This reduction has left ground units like the Golani Brigade exposed to ambushes, leading to a rise in casualties.

---

## Armor Vulnerabilities: The ATGM Threat

The tactical mobility of the IDF's armored units has been severely restricted by Hezbollah's anti-tank guided missile (ATGM) teams. The group's deployment of advanced Kornet and Almas missiles has targeted Israeli armor:
*   **Wednesday's losses**: Ten Merkava tanks were confirmed destroyed or disabled in a single day of clashes.
*   **Thursday's losses**: An additional four tanks were hit during attempted advances in the western sector.
*   **Logistical gridlock**: The high loss rate has forced commanders to restrict the movement of armored columns, slowing the pace of the ground offensive.

---

## The Engineering Attrition: Targeting the D9 Bulldozers

To advance through the heavily mined border villages, the IDF relies on armored D9 bulldozers to clear paths and detonate improvised explosive devices (IEDs). Hezbollah has focused its fire on these slow-moving engineering assets:
*   **Daily losses**: An average of two to three D9 bulldozers are destroyed or disabled daily.
*   **Tactical delay**: Without bulldozers to clear the roads, infantry units are forced to advance along exposed paths, increasing their vulnerability to ambush.
*   **Supply line exposure**: The loss of engineering vehicles prevents the reinforcement of advanced positions, leaving forward units isolated.

---

## The Galilee Exodus: Bidding Farewell to the North

The failure to suppress Hezbollah's short-range rocket launchers has had immediate consequences for Israel's domestic front. Semyon (Abi) Ashkenazi, the military correspondent for *Maariv*, summarized the domestic impact: "Israel bids farewell to the North" (إسرائيل تودع الشمال).

The continuous launch of 100 to 150 rockets daily has forced the preemptive evacuation of the remaining border communities. Thousands of families have packed their bags and relocated to temporary shelters in Tel Aviv and Tiberias, creating a housing and civil defense crisis in the center. The empty towns of the Galilee confirm that the military has failed to establish a secure border zone.

---

## Conclusion: The Limits of the Ground Offensive

The heavy casualties sustained by the Golani Brigade and the losses of the 36th and 91st Divisions demonstrate the limits of Israel's ground strategy in Lebanon.

By failing to secure the border villages and coordinate air support due to the overstretch on the Iranian front, the IDF has entered a costly war of attrition. Hezbollah's ability to maintain its leadership structure, sustain its rocket launches, and destroy armored vehicles has neutralized the political objectives of the campaign. The ground war has not pushed Hezbollah north of the Litani; instead, it has brought the war deep into the Galilee, leaving the northern border abandoned and the ground forces bleeding in the valleys of southern Lebanon.
