F-35 Kill Decoded: Passive Thermal Imaging Ambush

July 29, 2026 · 8 min read
Khalid Naami
Founder, Owner, and CEO at Dashboard Options

Both Israeli and Iranian analysts have now published their technical assessments of how Iran's air defenses brought down the F-35 Lightning II. The convergence of the two analyses reveals a kill methodology that bypasses every layer of the F-35's defensive architecture: a fully passive electro-optical thermal imaging system that emits zero radar waves, denies the pilot any warning of lock-on, and guides a proximity-fuze missile to detonate near the tail, disabling the aircraft through fragmentation without requiring a direct hit. This is the anatomy of the world's first confirmed passive kill of a fifth-generation stealth fighter.

Two Schools, One Conclusion​

The Israeli analysis, published through defense channels, and the Iranian analysis, published through Tasnim News Agency (close to the IRGC), approach the event from different angles but arrive at the same technical conclusion.

The Israeli reading: Iran has transitioned from traditional infrared heat-seeking to full thermal imaging of the target. The system no longer follows a heat source; it constructs a complete thermal picture of the aircraft.

The Iranian reading: The engagement was conducted via a purely electro-optical passive system that emitted zero radar waves, rendering the F-35's radar warning receiver and electronic warfare suite entirely useless.

Both analyses confirm the same outcome: the pilot had no awareness of being tracked, no warning of missile launch, and no opportunity to deploy countermeasures.

The Critical Distinction: Infrared Tracking vs. Thermal Imaging​

The technical breakthrough lies in the difference between two generations of sensor technology:

Traditional Infrared (IR) Tracking​

  • Method: Detects the thermal radiation emitted by the aircraft's engine exhaust and hot surfaces.
  • Limitation: Follows a heat point source without constructing an image of the target.
  • Countermeasures: Defeated by flares, reduced exhaust signatures, and IR suppression systems.
  • Example: The R-60 (Aphid) missile uses this legacy IR guidance and is largely ineffective against low-signature targets.

Modern Thermal Imaging (IIR — Imaging Infrared)​

  • Method: Produces a complete thermal photograph of the target, capturing its full shape, structural geometry, and temperature profile.
  • Advantage: Tracks the target through its silhouette, not just its heat emission. Even with reduced thermal signatures, the aircraft's physical form remains visible.
  • Counter-resistance: Flares and IR decoys cannot replicate the full thermal shape of an aircraft, making them ineffective.
  • Example: The 9K333 Verba (ATGM/MANPADS) uses IIR seekers of this generation, and similar technology is confirmed in Iranian systems.

The IRGC video did not show a simple heat blob. It displayed a full thermal-optical image (IIR) of the F-35, meaning the system that tracked the aircraft produced a detailed thermal photograph showing the airframe structure, wing geometry, and tail profile.

Zero Radar Emissions: The Passive Ambush​

The most devastating element of the Iranian method is its complete absence of radar emissions. This is the foundation of what can be called a "passive offensive ambush":

  1. No radar waves transmitted: The tracking system operates entirely in electro-optical mode, receiving thermal radiation passively without emitting any signal.
  2. Radar Warning Receiver (RWR) blind: The F-35's AN/ASQ-239 electronic warfare system is designed to detect, classify, and geolocate radar emissions directed at the aircraft. When no radar waves exist, the system has nothing to detect.
  3. No lock-on warning: In conventional engagements, when a radar achieves fire-control lock, the pilot receives an audible and visual warning through the RWR. With electro-optical tracking, no such warning is generated.
  4. No electronic warfare response: The pilot cannot jam a radar that is not transmitting. The entire electronic countermeasures suite, designed to deceive or overwhelm radar signals, becomes irrelevant.

The F-35 was flying in what its pilot believed was a threat-free environment. In reality, Iranian ground-based passive sensors had constructed a full thermal image of the aircraft and were tracking it with precision, in complete electromagnetic silence.

The Kill Sequence: Proximity Fuze Fragmentation​

The Iranian analysis describes the engagement sequence with precision:

  1. Thermal imaging acquisition: The passive electro-optical system acquires the full thermal silhouette of the F-35.
  2. Silent tracking: The system tracks the aircraft's flight path, speed, and altitude without emitting any signal that would alert the pilot.
  3. Missile launch: A surface-to-air missile is launched on a computed intercept trajectory guided by the electro-optical track data.
  4. Proximity fuze detonation: The missile does not seek a direct impact. Instead, it detonates near the aircraft's tail section using a proximity fuze (صمام تقاربي).
  5. Fragmentation damage: The warhead disperses high-velocity fragments that strike the tail, control surfaces, and rear fuselage, disabling the aircraft without destroying it entirely.

The choice of proximity-fuze fragmentation over direct impact is deliberate and consistent with the passive methodology. Since the missile's terminal guidance does not rely on active radar, a proximity detonation maximizes the kill probability by covering a larger engagement volume around the target.

Why the Tail: The F-35's Rear Vulnerability​

The damage images show that the F-35 was struck from behind, with fragmentation concentrated on the tail section. This is consistent with the engagement method for several reasons:

  • Afterburner signature: The IRGC analysis noted that the aircraft was flying in an offensive combat profile with afterburner engaged, producing a maximum thermal signature from the rear.
  • Reduced stealth from rear aspect: The F-35's RCS optimization is focused on the forward hemisphere (frontal aspect). The rear, with its engine exhaust nozzle and structural joints, presents a significantly larger radar and thermal cross-section.
  • Fragmentation effectiveness: The tail section houses flight control actuators, stabilizers, and hydraulic lines. Fragmentation damage to these components can render the aircraft uncontrollable without requiring a direct hit to the fuselage.

The Pilot's Impossible Situation​

The passive kill chain creates a scenario in which the F-35 pilot has no defensive options:

Traditional EngagementPassive Engagement (Iran)
Radar lock triggers RWR warningNo radar emissions = no warning
Pilot deploys chaff/flaresNo heat-seeking = flares useless
EW suite jams radar frequencyNo radar to jam
Pilot executes evasive maneuverNo awareness of threat = no maneuver
Missile seeks direct impactProximity fuze = wider kill zone

The pilot was flying with full confidence in a clean threat environment. The first indication of an engagement was the detonation itself. By that point, the fragmentation had already struck the tail, and the aircraft's only option was an emergency landing.

The Iranian Fragmentation Doctrine​

The proximity-fuze fragmentation approach is consistent with Iran's broader warfighting doctrine. The same principle is visible in the 70% cluster munition warheads now being used against Israel: fewer warheads per missile, but each with larger fragmentation effect.

Whether in air defense or ballistic missile strikes, Iran's approach prioritizes fragmentation and area effect over direct-hit precision. In the air defense context, this means:

  • A missile does not need to hit the aircraft directly.
  • A proximity detonation within the fragmentation envelope is sufficient.
  • The fragments disable critical subsystems rather than destroying the entire airframe.
  • The aircraft is rendered combat-ineffective and forced to withdraw.

This doctrine is more efficient than seeking a direct kill: it uses less sophisticated terminal guidance while achieving the strategic objective of removing the aircraft from the fight.

The Comparison: MQ-9 and Verba-Class Systems​

The Israeli analysis drew comparisons to Western platforms that use similar thermal imaging technology:

  • MQ-9 Reaper: The U.S. drone uses high-resolution thermal imaging (FLIR) to track both ground and aerial targets with full silhouette resolution.
  • 9K333 Verba: The Russian MANPADS uses an IIR seeker that constructs a thermal image of the target, making it resistant to flare countermeasures.

The fact that Iran has achieved the same thermal imaging capability in a ground-based air defense system means that every low-observable aircraft operating over Iranian airspace, including the B-2 Spirit and the F-22 Raptor, is now subject to the same passive detection methodology.

Conclusion: The Silent Kill​

The F-35 was designed to dominate adversaries who rely on radar. Its entire defensive architecture, RWR, electronic warfare, chaff, and flares, is optimized against radar-guided and traditional IR threats. Iran's passive electro-optical thermal imaging system bypasses this architecture entirely.

The kill sequence is elegant in its simplicity: image the aircraft thermally, track it silently, launch a missile without any electromagnetic emission, detonate by proximity fuze, and disable through fragmentation. The pilot receives no warning, deploys no countermeasures, and executes no evasive maneuver. The first evidence of the engagement is the sound of shrapnel striking the tail.

This is not a refinement of existing air defense technology. It is a paradigm shift: from active detection to passive imaging, from direct impact to proximity fragmentation, from radar warfare to electromagnetic silence. The F-35's stealth remains effective against radar. It is now irrelevant against an adversary that has stopped using radar entirely.
