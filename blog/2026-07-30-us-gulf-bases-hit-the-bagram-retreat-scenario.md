# U.S. Gulf Bases Hit: The Bagram Retreat Scenario

A major investigative report published by the *New York Times* has admitted to severe, structural damage inflicted on 13 U.S. military bases across the Persian Gulf by Iranian precision missile strikes. The destruction of these installations has forced U.S. forces to evacuate their primary facilities and coordinate operations from temporary offices and commercial hotels in regional capitals. This displacement has raised operational costs by up to 45% and raised fears of a humiliating, "Bagram-style" exit from the entire Persian Gulf.

## The NYT Exposé: 13 U.S. Bases Damaged

The *New York Times* report details the extent of the damage sustained by the U.S. military infrastructure in the region. According to the document, 13 primary installations—including facilities in Kuwait, Bahrain, and the UAE—have been struck by Iranian precision-guided missiles.

The strikes focused on:
*   **Maintenance hangars** and testing bays, halting repair work.
*   **Fuel storage depots** and pumping stations, disrupting logistics.
*   **Runway guidance networks** and command towers.

The scale of the destruction confirms that Iran's missile forces successfully penetrated the air defense networks protecting the bases, placing the U.S. military's regional infrastructure out of service.

---

## Operating from Hotels: The Operational Cost Spike

Due to the destruction of their primary bases, U.S. commands have been forced to relocate their personnel and communication centers to temporary facilities, including commercial hotels and civilian office buildings.

This displacement has resulted in a significant increase in operational costs. The logistics of maintaining secure communication lines, housing personnel, and transporting equipment from scattered civilian sites have raised the cost of operations by:
*   **15% to 20%** for units relocated in Kuwait and Bahrain.
*   **Up to 45%** for forces operating from temporary quarters in Saudi Arabia.

These off-budget cost increases place additional pressure on the U.S. defense budget, which has not received legislative supplements for the campaign.

---

## Prince Sultan Air Base: Structural Destruction

Among the damaged installations, Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia sustained some of the heaviest strikes. The base, which serves as a major hub for U.S. Air Force operations, was struck by a barrage of heavy liquid-fuel missiles.

The strikes destroyed the main maintenance hangars and disabled the primary runway, preventing the deployment of refueling tankers and tactical aircraft. Rebuilding these facilities will require years of construction, leaving the U.S. Air Force without its primary staging platform in the central Gulf sector.

---

## The Island Naval Firing Line Strategy

Faced with the vulnerability of its continental bases, the Pentagon is attempting to establish a "new naval firing line" by occupying islands in the Persian Gulf. The plan involves deploying 1,000 paratroopers from the 82nd Airborne Division to secure:
*   **The three disputed islands** (Abu Musa, Greater Tunb, Lesser Tunb) in coordination with the UAE.
*   **Kharg and Qeshm islands** to establish forward artillery and radar posts.

The objective is to draw Iranian missile fire away from the main continental bases and toward these island outposts and naval vessels, creating a buffer zone to protect the remaining logistics hubs.

---

## The Sitting Duck Vulnerability of Gulf Islands

However, military experts warn that the island strategy is highly dangerous. By placing U.S. ground forces on small, isolated islands like Kharg or Abu Musa, the Pentagon is turning its soldiers into "sitting ducks."

The IRGC has already reinforced the islands with dense minefields and shoulder-fired missiles. Any attempt to land U.S. troops will result in immediate close-quarters clashes, exposing the paratroopers to concentrated missile and artillery fire from the Iranian mainland, which is only a few kilometers away.

---

## The Bagram-Style Exit Scenario

The most significant strategic risk of the island campaign is a chaotic, "Bagram-style" withdrawal. If U.S. ground forces land on the islands and suffer heavy casualties, the administration may face domestic political pressure to withdraw from the region entirely.

This retreat would mimic the chaotic withdrawal from Bagram Airfield in Afghanistan in 2021 or the exit from Vietnam. A forced withdrawal from the Gulf bases would represent the end of U.S. military hegemony in the Middle East, leaving the region's energy resources under the influence of Iran, China, and Russia.

---

## Targeting U.S. Personnel in Civilian Hotels

The IRGC has indicated that U.S. forces operating from civilian hotels and commercial offices are within its targeting coordinates. The Iranian command has called on regional populations to identify and map the locations where U.S. military personnel are housed.

By declaring civilian hotels as legitimate military targets, Iran is demonstrating that the U.S. cannot secure its forces by dispersing them into civilian areas. The threat of strikes on regional hotels raises the political risk for host nations like the UAE and Saudi Arabia, who fear the impact of such attacks on their domestic stability and tourism sectors.

---

## Symmetrical Destruction of Gulf State Infrastructure

The U.S. insistence on continuing the campaign from regional bases threatens the stability of the host nations. If the U.S. continues to launch strikes from facilities in Bahrain, Kuwait, and the UAE, Iran has warned it will target the primary infrastructure of those countries.

A sustained missile campaign targeting desalination plants, power grids, and commercial ports in the UAE and Bahrain would turn these wealthy financial hubs into "failed states" economically. The host nations are beginning to realize that the price of hosting U.S. forces may be the destruction of their own economies.

---

## Conclusion: The Collapse of the Gulf Shield

The *New York Times* exposé on the destruction of U.S. bases and the threat of a Bagram-style exit confirm that the U.S. military presence in the Gulf is facing its most significant challenge.

By choosing to fund the war through off-budget Gulf resources and attempting to establish a new naval firing line on disputed islands, Trump is executing a high-risk strategy. The physical destruction of 13 bases proves that the air defense shield is no longer functional. If the administration continues to press for a ground confrontation on the islands, it must accept that the result may not be the surrender of Iran, but a humiliating withdrawal that will end the U.S. military presence in the Persian Gulf forever.
