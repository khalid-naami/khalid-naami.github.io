# Lincoln Hit: Yemen Warns Bush Carrier in Red Sea

The naval theater of the Middle East conflict has experienced a dramatic expansion. Israeli and regional sources have confirmed a successful Iranian missile strike on the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln in the Persian Gulf, forcing the vessel to change its deployment position. Concurrently, the IRGC Navy announced a complex operation under Wave 84 of the \"True Promise 4\" campaign, targeting U.S. assets in Kuwait and Dubai. In Sana'a, the Yemeni Armed Forces have declared a strict red line, warning the U.S. Navy against deploying the USS George H.W. Bush to the Red Sea.

## The Persian Gulf Hit: USS Abraham Lincoln Struck

The strike on the USS Abraham Lincoln represents a major event in contemporary naval warfare. Launched from coastlines in southern Iran, multiple precision cruise missiles targeted the carrier group in the Persian Gulf:
*   **The Impact**: Official reports confirm that at least two missiles successfully struck the carrier, causing localized damage.
*   **Repositioning**: The impact forced the carrier command to immediately reposition the vessel, moving it further out into the Gulf of Oman to escape the coastal missile envelope.
*   **Previous strikes**: This hit follows the March 13 combined missile and drone strike on the Lincoln and Fifth Fleet escorts, confirmed by the White House.

---

## Admiral Irani's Statement: Active Missile Range Targeting

Following the strike, the Commander of the Iranian Navy, Admiral Shahram Irani, delivered a public address detailing the tactical environment:
*   **Continuous tracking**: Irani stated that the U.S. fleet is under constant radar and optical surveillance by Iranian coastal bases.
*   **Target locks**: He warned that as long as U.S. carriers operate within the range of Iran's land-based anti-ship cruise missile (ASCM) batteries, they remain target locks.
*   **Deterrence posture**: The admiral emphasized that the strike on the Lincoln was a demonstration of capability, and that any future hostile actions would result in a larger barrage.

---

## The Wave 84 Complex Strike: Targeting Port Shuaiba

Simultaneously, the IRGC Navy executed a coordinated strike package designated as Wave 84 of the \"True Promise 4\" campaign. The operation targeted U.S. logistical infrastructure in the northern Gulf:
*   **The Location**: Port Shuaiba (ميناء الشيوخ) in Kuwait, a primary maritime logistics hub for U.S. ground forces in the region.
*   **The Munitions**: The IRGC deployed Ghadr-380 (قدر 380) ballistic and cruise missiles to bypass local air defense arrays.
*   **The Objective**: Targeting the landing craft docks housing U.S. Army transport and supply vessels.

---

## The Sinking of Three U.S. Landing Craft (LCU)

The strike on Port Shuaiba resulted in significant damage to U.S. maritime transport capability:
*   **Targeted fleet**: Six U.S. Landing Craft Utility (LCU) vessels were targeted while docked at the port.
*   **The Damage**: Field reports confirm that three LCU vessels sank immediately due to structural damage from the missile impacts.
*   **Secondary fires**: The remaining three vessels sustained heavy fire, disabling their propulsion and navigation systems.

The loss of these landing craft limits the U.S. Army's capability to execute amphibious logistics and transport heavy equipment across the northern Gulf.

---

## The Dubai Drones: Targeting U.S. Drone Officers

In a separate phase of Wave 84, a drone strike targeted a temporary U.S. military command node in Dubai. The operation utilized low-flying suicide drones:
*   **The Target**: A civilian hotel used as a temporary headquarters housing officers and operators from a U.S. military drone unit.
*   **The Strike**: The drones bypassed detection systems, striking the upper floors of the building where the communications array was established.
*   **Casualties**: The IRGC statement confirmed that the strike \"neutralized\" several U.S. officers and technicians operating the regional drone network.

---

## Yemen's Red Sea Line: Banning the USS George H.W. Bush

In Sana'a, the leadership of the Yemeni Armed Forces (Ansar Allah) issued a warning to the U.S. Navy regarding its deployment plans in the Red Sea.

Yemen has warned the U.S. against deploying the aircraft carrier USS George H.W. Bush to the Red Sea to replace the departing USS Gerald R. Ford. The statement declared that the Red Sea is a \"closed military zone\" for U.S. carriers, and that the Bush would be targeted immediately upon entry, using Yemen's land-based anti-ship ballistic missiles.

---

## The Gerald R. Ford Maintenance Withdrawal to Crete

The U.S. requirement to deploy a new carrier is driven by the withdrawal of the USS Gerald R. Ford:
*   **Crete deployment**: The Ford, the largest aircraft carrier in the world, has returned to a naval base in Crete, Greece, for emergency maintenance.
*   **Operational wear**: The carrier's propulsion and electrical launch systems sustained wear during weeks of high-intensity operations against Iranian and regional targets.
*   **The Gap**: The withdrawal of the Ford leaves a significant gap in the U.S. naval presence in the Eastern Mediterranean and Red Sea, which the Pentagon hoped to fill with the George H.W. Bush.

---

## Neutralizing the Red Sea: Yemen's Strategic Intent

Yemen's warning to the Bush carrier is part of a plan to isolate the Red Sea from the regional conflict. Sana'a has clarified its rules of engagement:
*   **Israeli and U.S. targets**: Yemen's weapons are strictly focused on U.S. and Israeli military assets.
*   **No regional threat**: The leadership has assured Saudi Arabia and the UAE that their commercial and oil shipping through Bab el-Mandeb will not be targeted.
*   **European neutrality**: Yemen has warned European naval forces to remain out of the conflict, stating that any European participation in strikes against Iran will result in their vessels being targeted in the Bab el-Mandeb strait.

---

## Conclusion: Shifting the Naval Geography of the Gulf

The strike on the USS Abraham Lincoln and Yemen's warning to the USS George H.W. Bush demonstrate the changing geography of U.S. naval operations in the Middle East.

By targeting carriers in the Persian Gulf and establishing a blockade in the Red Sea, Iranian and Yemeni forces are forcing the U.S. Navy to operate from the Indian Ocean and the Arabian Sea. This shift increases the transit time for tactical aircraft, reduces the effectiveness of U.S. air support, and protects the Iranian mainland from immediate naval strikes. If the Pentagon chooses to challenge Yemen's red line by sending the Bush carrier into the Red Sea, it must accept the risk of a major naval confrontation that could result in the loss of a second carrier group.
