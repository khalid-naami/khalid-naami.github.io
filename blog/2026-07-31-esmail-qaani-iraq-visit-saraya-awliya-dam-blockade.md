# Esmail Qaani Iraq Visit: Iraqi Factions Alert

In a development that has captured the attention of Western and regional intelligence agencies, IRGC Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani has made an unannounced visit to Baghdad. The visit, his first official travel outside Iran since the ceasefire went into effect, comes as Iraqi resistance groups declare full readiness to resume military operations. The coordination efforts seek to unify the political and military stances of Baghdad's factions before the temporary truce expires on Wednesday.

## The Unannounced Visit: Esmail Qaani in Baghdad

The Quds Force commander arrived in Baghdad to coordinate regional defense structures:
*   **Disproving Rumors**: The visit put an end to rumors circulating in Western media that Qaani had been injured or detained.
*   **Direct Channel**: Qaani held immediate sessions with faction leaders and members of the Shia Coordination Framework.
*   **The Focus**: Establishing a unified command structure to manage potential escalation scenarios with the U.S. and Israel.

---

## Awliya al-Dam Alert: Preparing for Wednesday's Resumption

Iraqi factions have announced they are ready to resume operations when the ceasefire ends:
*   **Full Readiness**: Abu Mehdi al-Jaafari, spokesperson for Saraya Awliya al-Dam, declared their forces are on standby.
*   **The Deadline**: Operations are scheduled to resume if the U.S. does not lift its maritime blockade on Iran by Wednesday.
*   **Call for Mobilization**: The group called for continued support for resistance forces in both Lebanon and Iran.

---

## Resolving the Deadlock: Shia Coordination Framework Unity

A key goal of the visit is resolving political disputes within Baghdad's leadership:
*   **Political Deadlock**: Iraq's parliament remains divided over the selection of a new prime minister.
*   **Framework Cohesion**: Qaani worked to unite the Shia Coordination Framework to prevent internal political fragmentation.
*   **Resisting Pressures**: The factions agreed to maintain a unified front against Western diplomatic demands.

---

## The Prime Minister Standoff: Rejecting the Maliki Veto

The selection of Iraq's next leader has become a point of contention with Washington:
*   **The Threat**: Trump's administration threatened to cut off economic and financial aid if a candidate like Nouri al-Maliki is selected.
*   **The Rejection**: Faction leaders rejected the U.S. veto, asserting that the prime minister selection is a sovereign decision.
*   **Compromise Search**: Qaani is assisting in identifying a consensus candidate who can protect national interests.

---

## Fiber-Optic Drones: Deploying Jamming-Resistant Weapons

Factions are preparing to introduce new technology to counter U.S. defense systems:
*   **New Arsenal**: Saraya Awliya al-Dam announced they will deploy advanced weapons systems for the first time.
*   **Fiber-Optic Guidance**: Factions have received drones guided by fiber-optic tethers, which are immune to electronic jamming.
*   **Reconnaissance Focus**: The new systems allow operators to collect high-definition imagery of U.S. logistical centers.

---

## Defensive Coordination: Factions and the Official Security Network

The coordination efforts seek to integrate faction activities with state structures:
*   **Sovereign Cover**: Factions are coordinating with official security organs, utilizing their legal status in the defense system.
*   **Avoiding Clashes**: Faction leaders agreed to avoid unilateral actions that could trigger direct U.S. strikes on civilian centers.
*   **Joint Command**: A joint operations room has been established to monitor U.S. troop rotations in the region.

---

## Rear-Guard Logistics: Securing Iran's Border Provinces

Iraqi groups are expanding their role to assist in securing Iran's border regions:
*   **Border Security**: Fighters have been deployed to southwest Iran to assist in securing border provinces.
*   **Counter-Insurgency**: The deployment aims to neutralize armed Kurdish cells supported by foreign intelligence.
*   **Logistical Rear**: Iraq continues to serve as a secure logistical transit route for material destined for regional defense sectors.

---

## Parliamentary Mandate: Expelling Foreign Military Forces

The legal basis for the resistance remains focused on removing foreign bases:
*   **The Mandate**: Factions are demanding the complete implementation of the parliamentary vote to expel foreign troops.
*   **Beyond the U.S.**: The demand has expanded to include all coalition military forces stationed in the country.
*   **Sovereign Control**: Factions argue that regional stability requires the complete removal of foreign command facilities.

---

## Conclusion: The Asymmetric Frontline in Post-Truce Iraq

The unannounced visit of Esmail Qaani to Baghdad highlights Iraq's role in the wider confrontation.

By coordinating the Shia Coordination Framework and aligning faction operations, Qaani seeks to build a resilient rear-guard for Iran. The deployment of fiber-optic guided drones by Saraya Awliya al-Dam shows that the factions have acquired new technical options during the ceasefire. As the Wednesday deadline approaches, the rejection of U.S. vetoes over candidates like Nouri al-Maliki confirms that political control in Baghdad remains linked to regional security dynamics.
