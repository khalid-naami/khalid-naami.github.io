# Blinded Eyes: US Radar Struck, Warning Times Reduced

In a significant admission, Israeli military censors have permitted the publication of reports confirming the destruction of a vital U.S. early-warning radar in the Negev Desert. The facility, known as Site 512, was targeted by a precision Iranian missile strike. The loss of this advanced sensor has directly impacted regional air defenses, leading to a drastic reduction in warning times for incoming missile threats across Israel.

## The Acknowledgment: Confirming the Site 512 Radar Hit

For the first time, Israeli Hebrew media, including Channel 12, has acknowledged that a precision strike by Iranian forces targeted and disabled the early-warning radar at U.S. Site 512. The facility is located on Mount Har Qeren (جبل جرن) in the Negev Desert, approximately 20 kilometers from Gaza and 30 kilometers southwest of Beersheba.

Although Israeli officials previously downplayed the site's significance by claiming it primarily serves U.S. strategic interests, the acknowledgment of the strike highlights the connection between the facility and Israel's domestic air defense network.

---

## The Role of the AN/TPY-2 Radar in Israel's Defense

The disabled system is the AN/TPY-2, a high-performance, mobile X-band radar developed by Raytheon, costing approximately $300 million. This system serves as the primary sensor for the Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD) missile defense network.

The role of the AN/TPY-2 is to detect, track, and classify long-range ballistic missiles launched from Iran or its regional allies. Because of its high resolution and long range, it provides early-warning tracking data directly to both U.S. and Israeli air defense batteries, allowing interceptors to be calculated and launched early in the threat's flight path.

---

## Why Warning Times Have Been Drastically Reduced

The destruction of the AN/TPY-2 radar has created a critical gap in early-warning coverage. Without the long-range tracking data provided by Site 512, local air defense systems must rely on shorter-range sensors, which detect incoming missiles much later in their flight.

This reduction in early tracking capability has directly translated into a shorter warning window for Israeli civilians. The home front command has had to adjust warning protocols, leaving civilians with significantly less time to seek shelter after sirens are activated, explaining why warnings have become shorter and less predictable.

---

## Khatam al-Anbiya HQ's Coordinated Strike on Five U.S. Bases

According to official declarations from Iran’s Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters, the hit on Site 512 was part of a coordinated operation targeting five strategic U.S. bases in the region. The strikes combined precision ballistic missiles and suicide drone swarms.

Alongside the radar complex in the Negev Desert, the operation targeted U.S. troop concentrations and logistics depots at Camp Arifjan in Kuwait. The coordinated nature of the strikes demonstrated the ability of Iranian planners to execute simultaneous actions against regional logistics nodes.

---

## Non-Conventional U.S. Airstrikes and Iran's Warhead Standardization

The escalation has occurred alongside a U.S. heavy bomber campaign targeting Iranian infrastructure. U.S. forces have conducted intensive strikes using B-1B Lancer and B-2 Spirit bombers, striking thousands of civilian and industrial targets to degrade Iran's economic capacity.

In response to this campaign, Iranian commanders have adjusted their missile doctrine. The IRGC has ceased deploying warheads weighing under one ton (1,000 kilograms) for strategic strikes, standardizing on heavier payloads to maximize the destructive impact on coalition bases and key infrastructure hubs.

---

## Air Defense Deficits: Shifting Patriot Batteries from East Asia

The systematic destruction of air defense assets in the Middle East—including 11 radar systems over the past weeks—has created an urgent demand for replacement components. To cover these gaps, the U.S. military has had to redeploy Patriot batteries from East Asian theatres.

Patriot systems have been moved from South Korea and Japan to the Persian Gulf to protect key bases and shipping lanes. This redeployment has reduced the air defense coverage available in East Asia, leaving regional allies with limited coverage as the Pentagon prioritizes the Middle East theater.

---

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Regional Defense Networks

The neutralization of the AN/TPY-2 radar at Site 512 highlights the vulnerability of centralized early-warning nodes. By targeting the sensor array of the THAAD system, Iranian forces bypassed the interceptor batteries, degrading the entire defense network.

The reduction in warning times for civilians and the redeployment of air defense assets from other global theaters indicate that the conflict is straining coalition resources. As long as these key radar and communication hubs remain out of service, the effectiveness of regional air defenses will remain degraded, shaping the tactical calculations of both sides.
