# Gulf Battle: The Fight for the Strategic Islands

The geopolitical confrontation in the Middle East has moved beyond the containment of Iran's nuclear program toward a battle for physical control over the Persian Gulf. U.S. President Donald Trump has proposed a strategic plan to seize the three disputed Gulf islands—Abu Musa, Greater Tunb, and Lesser Tunb—currently controlled by Iran. This initiative aims to establish direct U.S. sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz, redefining the maritime architecture of global energy transport.

## Seizing the Three Islands: Trump’s Gulf Master Plan

The three strategic islands—Abu Musa, Greater Tunb, and Lesser Tunb—hold a vital position near the shipping lanes of the Strait of Hormuz. Control over these islands grants the occupying power direct oversight over all commercial and military transit entering or exiting the Persian Gulf.

Donald Trump's strategy aims to seize these islands during the current military escalation, maintaining U.S. control even after a potential ceasefire. By establishing permanent military installations on the islands, the U.S. seeks to secure shipping lanes under its own authority, bypassing the navigational challenges posed by the IRGC Navy's coastal defenses.

---

## The Funding Deal: UAE and GCC Financing Agreements

To fund the U.S. military campaign, the Trump administration has proposed a strategic transaction to the United Arab Emirates (UAE) and other member states of the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC). Under the proposed agreement, the Gulf states will finance the multi-trillion dollar U.S. military operations in the region.

In return, the United States will secure the three disputed islands and transfer formal sovereignty to the UAE. This deal aims to settle a long-standing territorial dispute between Abu Dhabi and Tehran, utilizing U.S. military power to redraw regional borders while shifting the financial burden of the war onto local allies.

---

## Ghalibaf’s Warnings and Tehran’s Defensive Mobilization

Iranian leadership has reacted strongly to the proposed U.S. plan. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Speaker of the Islamic Consultative Assembly (Parliament), warned that any attempt to seize the islands would result in unrestricted regional warfare.

Ghalibaf stated that Iran has discarded previous operational limits, warning that "the blood of U.S. invaders will mix with the waters of the Persian Gulf." Simultaneously, the Iranian Ambassador to Moscow confirmed that Tehran has reinforced its defenses on the three islands, preparing its ground forces to engage any U.S. landing operations.

---

## The Florida Backchannel: U.S.-Russian Energy Negotiations

As military preparations continue, high-level diplomatic representatives from the United States and Russia held backchannel negotiations in Florida. The talks focused on resolving the global oil supply crisis caused by shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz.

The Russian delegation sought to secure U.S. sanction waivers to export oil to major developing markets, including India and Bangladesh. Trump signaled a willingness to issue temporary waivers to lower global fuel prices, provided Moscow refrains from offering military or logistical support to Tehran during the current escalation.

---

## The European Veto: Adhering to G7 Oil Price Caps

However, the proposed U.S.-Russian energy agreement has faced opposition from the European Union. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen issued a veto against the relaxation of energy sanctions, demanding that all partners adhere to G7 oil price caps.

European representatives argued that granting waivers for Russian oil would undermine G7 sanctions and provide Moscow with funds for its military campaign in Ukraine. Furthermore, European officials described their previous phase-out of domestic nuclear energy as a "strategic mistake," noting that energy dependence has limited Europe's strategic autonomy during regional crises.

---

## Targeting Taleghan-2: The Air Campaign Against Infrastructure

While diplomatic talks continue, the coalition air campaign has targeted Iran's remaining industrial and research infrastructure. Strategic B-52 bombers and Israeli strike aircraft executed precision strikes targeting the Taleghan-2 (طالقان 2) research complex, a key facility connected to Iran's historical nuclear program.

The strikes focused on neutralizing underground research facilities, utilizing heavy bunker-busting ordnance. While U.S. planners have spared Iran's civilian electrical grid to prevent total humanitarian collapse, almost all other industrial, transport, and military infrastructure has been targeted, degrading the country's economic base.

---

## Conclusion: The Threat of a Third Gulf War

The plan to seize the three strategic Gulf islands and the failure of backchannel energy negotiations suggest that the conflict is moving toward a broader confrontation. By proposing a GCC-funded campaign to redraw maritime borders, the U.S. administration has expanded its objectives beyond simple containment.

As Iran reinforces its positions on Abu Musa and the Tunbs, the risk of a direct ground clash remains high. The outcome of this confrontation will determine whether the Strait of Hormuz remains a contested chokepoint or transitions under permanent Western military authority, shaping global trade routes for decades.
