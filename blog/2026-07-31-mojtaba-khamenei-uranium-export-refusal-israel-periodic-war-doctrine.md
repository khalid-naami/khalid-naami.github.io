# Iran Refuses Uranium Export: Mojtaba Decree

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has issued a final directive rejecting U.S. demands to export its highly enriched uranium stockpile. The directive comes amid strategic differences between Washington and Tel Aviv regarding military planning. While the U.S. has proposed a three-stage campaign focused on economic blockade and opening the Strait of Hormuz, Israel is planning a campaign of periodic strikes targeting nuclear sites.

## The Uranium Ultimatum: Mojtaba Khamenei Refuses Stockpile Export

The political leadership in Tehran has established a firm boundary regarding its nuclear materials:
*   **The Directive**: Mojtaba Khamenei ruled out the export of Iran's 60% enriched uranium stockpile.
*   **Local Processing**: Iran proposed it could dilute the material domestically under IAEA supervision.
*   **No concessions**: The decision rejects President Donald Trump's demand to ship the material abroad.

---

## Israel's Periodic War: The Strategic Plan for Regime Change

Israeli military planners view the standoff as an ongoing conflict rather than a single campaign:
*   **Periodic War**: Israel's military doctrine proposes launching strikes every 12 to 24 months.
*   **Regime Exhaustion**: The strategy aims to deplete Iran's military and economic resources over time.
*   **Shadow Campaigns**: The doctrine incorporates cyber operations, sabotage, and target assassinations.

---

## U.S. Three-Stage Campaign: Economic Blockade and Energy Dominance

The U.S. administration has drafted a separate strategy focused on pressure and containment:
*   **Economic Blockade**: The first stage focuses on restricting oil exports and digital financial assets.
*   **Energy Production**: The second stage calls for expanding U.S. domestic energy output to stabilize prices.
*   **Hormuz Transit**: The final stage involves military operations to protect shipping lanes in the Gulf.

---

## The US-Israel Strategic Rift: Fordow Strikes vs. Hormuz Enforcement

Differences between the two allies have emerged regarding the prioritization of targets:
*   **Strategic Split**: Tel Aviv advocates for strikes on the Fordow and Natanz enrichment centers.
*   **U.S. Caution**: Washington seeks to avoid actions that could lead to nuclear escalation or oil spikes.
*   **Strait Focus**: The Pentagon prioritizes opening the Strait of Hormuz rather than targeting underground sites.

---

## The Ninety Percent Redline: Escalating to Weapons-Grade Level

Tehran has warned that any strike on its facilities will trigger immediate nuclear escalation:
*   **Ninety Percent Enrichment**: Iranian officials stated they will enrich uranium to 90% if attacked.
*   **Bypassing the Fatwa**: The leadership has confirmed that national survival overrides previous fatwas.
*   **Nuclear Assembly**: The material would be transferred to assembly sites to construct operational warheads.

---

## Targeting Dimona: Threatening Mutually Assured Destructive Parity

Iranian commanders have selected target options to establish strategic parity:
*   **Dimona Response**: Iran warned that a strike on Fordow would result in a strike on the Dimona facility.
*   **Security Perimeter**: Previous retaliatory strikes have demonstrated the ability to reach the Negev area.
*   **Preventing Strikes**: The threat of mutual nuclear facility damage is intended to prevent initial air strikes.

---

## Domestic Political Unity: Pezeshkian Meets Regular Army Commanders

Western expectations of divisions within the Iranian administration have not materialized:
*   **Pezeshkian's Meeting**: President Masoud Pezeshkian met with the commanders of the regular Army.
*   **Unified Preparation**: The president confirmed that all military branches are prepared to execute defense plans.
*   **No Factional Rift**: The coordination shows that reformist and hardline factions are aligned on national security.

---

## Russian Strategic Deterrence: Putin's Warning from China Summit

Moscow has indicated it will support its regional partners in the event of escalation:
*   **Putin's Comments**: President Vladimir Putin warned against actions that could destabilize the region.
*   **Nuclear Safeguards**: Russia indicated it might deploy deterrent assets to manage balance in the region.
*   **Strategic Partnership**: The statements highlight the growing security coordination between Moscow, Beijing, and Tehran.

---

## Conclusion: The Nuclear and Geopolitical Standoff in the Gulf

Mojtaba Khamenei's directive prohibiting the export of Iran's enriched uranium highlights the strategic limits of negotiations.

By rejecting Trump's export demands and preparing for domestic dilution, Tehran has defined its nuclear redlines. The U.S. three-stage campaign and Israel's "periodic war" plan show the different strategic approaches of the allies. As President Pezeshkian coordinates with military commanders and Russia warns against nuclear escalation, the standoff confirms that future security arrangements will be defined by strategic deterrence.
