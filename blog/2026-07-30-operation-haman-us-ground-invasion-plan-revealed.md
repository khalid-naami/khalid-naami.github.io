# Operation Haman: U.S. Ground Invasion Plan Revealed

An investigative report published by the Iranian state-owned newspaper *Tehran Times* has revealed details of a U.S.-Israeli joint military plan code-named \"Operation Haman\" (عملية هامان). The plan outlines a targeted ground invasion of Iran, beginning with the deployment of 5,000 U.S. Marines and special operations troops to seize critical airports and target the IRGC's underground missile cities. In response, Tehran has launched a mass mobilization campaign designated as \"Janb\" (جنب), warning that any attempt to execute the invasion will result in symmetrical nuclear retaliation.

## The Leaked Invasion Plan: Operation Haman Unveiled

The *Tehran Times* report, corroborated by U.S. defense officials speaking to the *Washington Post*, exposes the operational draft of \"Operation Haman.\" The plan represents a shift from strategic bombing to direct ground and amphibious interdiction.

The plan is designed to execute high-impact, localized raids rather than a full-scale territorial occupation on the model of the 2003 Iraq war. The objective is to neutralize Iran's strategic launching capabilities on the coast and in the interior, forcing the leadership into accepting U.S. terms.

---

## First-Phase Objectives: Seizing Regional Airfields

The first phase of \"Operation Haman\" relies on airborne and amphibious operations to secure key logistical nodes:
*   **Southern sector**: Seizing the primary port and regional airport of Bandar Abbas to cut off naval operations.
*   **Northwestern sector**: Executing airborne landings to secure airfields in Kermanshah, Urmia, and Tabriz.
*   **The Mission**: Once the airfields are secured, they will be used as staging hubs to deploy special operations teams deep into the interior.

---

## The Tactical Nuclear Option: Sparing the Silos

According to the leaked draft, the U.S. command has prepared for the \"ultimate scenario\": the deployment of limited tactical nuclear weapons (الضربات النووية التكتيكية المحدودة).

These low-yield, earth-penetrating nuclear munitions would be used to target the IRGC's \"missile cities\"—highly secure, underground launcher complexes built deep inside mountain ranges. Because these facilities are invulnerable to conventional bunker-buster munitions, Pentagon planners view tactical nuclear strikes as the only viable method to neutralize Iran's second-strike capability.

## Tehran's \"Janb\" Mobilization: Self-Sacrifice and Defense

In response to the disclosure of \"Operation Haman,\" the Iranian leadership has declared a state of national mobilization under the designation \"Janb\" (جنب):
*   **The Concept**: The term translates to \"self-sacrifice\" or martyrdom, drawing comparison to the Japanese Kamikaze or Samurai traditions in 1945.
*   **The Mobilization**: The government has established checkpoints and recruitment stations across major cities, preparing the population for guerrilla defense.
*   **Strategic intent**: Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf stated: \"We are preparing for the arrival of U.S. ground forces, and our people are ready to defend their country.\"

---

## The Domestic Consolidation: Reformists and Moderates Unite

The threat of a U.S. ground invasion and the potential use of tactical nuclear weapons has had an immediate impact on Iran's domestic political landscape:
*   **Unifying the front**: The external threat has neutralized internal divisions, uniting reformists, moderates, and the youth behind the defense establishment.
*   **Volunteer rise**: Thousands of citizens who previously opposed the administration's foreign policy have registered at mobilization centers.
*   **Failure of Western policy**: The consolidation has disproved U.S. intelligence predictions that strategic bombing would trigger a domestic uprising against the government.

---

## Symmetrical Nuclear Retaliation: Dimona in the Crosshairs

Tehran has warned that any use of tactical nuclear weapons by the U.S. will result in immediate, symmetrical retaliation targeting nuclear assets in the region:
*   **The Dimona Reactor**: The IRGC confirmed that Israel's primary nuclear research facility at Dimona has been programmed into its ballistic missile targeting arrays.
*   **Gulf Nuclear Assets**: The warning extends to coalition partners hosting U.S. forces, including the Barakah nuclear power plant in the UAE.
*   **The Red Line**: The statement confirms that if the U.S. crosses the nuclear threshold, Iran will target all nuclear facilities within its strike envelope, risking regional contamination.

## The Tel Aviv Protests: A Psychological Diversion Game

As the military tension rises, large-scale public protests have emerged in Tel Aviv. However, military analysts warn that these demonstrations are a psychological game:
*   **Coalition approval**: The protests have been permitted by the Home Front Command, bypassing the standard restrictions on public gatherings during wartime.
*   **Strategic intent**: The Netanyahu government is utilizing the protests to project a false narrative of domestic instability, attempting to confuse Iranian intelligence and divert missile strikes away from Haifa and Tel Aviv's industrial sectors.

---

## The Yemeni Strategic Trap: Declining the Third Front

The Israeli military command is facing a strategic dilemma regarding Yemen's Ansar Allah (Houthi) forces:
*   **The Provocation**: The Houthis have launched cruise missiles and drone swarms targeting Israeli ports.
*   **The Trap**: If Israel launches a major retaliatory campaign against Yemen (located 2,000 kilometers away), it will exhaust the IAF's resources and reduce the intensity of strikes on the primary fronts in Lebanon and Iran.
*   **The Decision**: To avoid this trap, the IDF has decided to limit its response in Yemen, focusing its overstretched forces on the northern and eastern fronts, disproving claims that it can fight on seven fronts simultaneously.

---

## Conclusion: The Limits of Conventional Escalation

The disclosure of \"Operation Haman\" and Iran's \"Janb\" mobilization confirm that the regional conflict is approaching a critical transition point.

By preparing for a ground invasion and drafting tactical nuclear options, the U.S. and Israel have pushed the war to the limits of conventional escalation. However, the domestic consolidation of the Iranian public under the \"Janb\" banner and the threat of symmetrical strikes on Dimona have created a powerful deterrent. If the Trump administration chooses to initiate the ground phase of the campaign, it must accept that the result may not be the capitulation of Iran, but a nuclear confrontation that will destabilize the Middle East and end U.S. influence in the region.
