# Operation Khaybar-1: Hezbollah Rockets Northern Israel

Hezbollah has officially launched "Operation Khaybar-1," executing a coordinated wave of heavy surface-to-surface missile strikes and suicide drone operations targeting military command hubs and air defense installations across northern Israel. The operation is a direct response to Israel's ground offensive, which aims to disarm the group south of the Litani River. The escalation has occurred alongside a shadow intelligence and electronic warfare standoff that has claimed key leaders on both sides.

## Operation Khaybar-1: Hezbollah’s Ballistic Response

Operation Khaybar-1 represents a transition to high-intensity defensive operations by Hezbollah. In the opening salvos, the group launched over 100 surface-to-surface ballistic missiles and deployed combat suicide drones targeting logistics hubs in northern Israel.

Rather than concentrating on border coordinates, the strikes targeted deep military assets in Safed, Haifa, and Galilee. The volume of the salvos was designed to saturate Israel's air defense grid, allowing precision warheads to reach their targets.

---

## Striking the Dadu Command and Northern IDF Bases

The primary military targets hit during the operation include:
*   **Dadu Command (Safed)**: The IDF's Northern Command Headquarters was struck by rocket salvos.
*   **Meron Air Surveillance Base**: Struck by heavy salvos, disrupting airspace monitoring and air support coordination.
*   **Ghabah Base**: Struck by suicide drones east of Safed.
*   **Anzeteem Base**: Struck by rocket salvos north of Safed.
*   **Amimim and Shimshon Bases**: Struck with rocket salvos around Lake Tiberias.
*   **Ma'alot-Tarshiha Air Defense Site**: Struck to degrade local interceptor cover.

---

## Israel’s Southern Lebanon Offensive: Roar of the Lion

The Israeli ground offensive, named "Roar of the Lion" or "Massive Wrath," began in March 2026 under the leadership of the 91st and 36th Divisions. The campaign aims to enforce the 2024 ceasefire terms by dismantling Hezbollah's military infrastructure south of the Litani River.

However, the ground offensive has faced resistance. Hezbollah's anti-tank and sniper units have restricted Israeli advances to border villages like Khiam. The IDF has resorted to mass demolition and evacuation orders, targeting local infrastructure to degrade Hezbollah's support network.

---

## The Shadow Intelligence War and Electronic Warfare

The physical confrontation is mirrored by a shadow intelligence and electronic warfare (EW) battle:
*   **Hezbollah Intelligence**: Despite leadership losses, the group has reconstructed its visual intelligence (VISINT) capabilities, utilizing "Hodhod" reconnaissance drones, thermal cameras, and civil camera networks to track IDF armored movements.
*   **IDF Counter-Intelligence**: The IDF's Unit 869 (Shapak) is deployed along the border to locate anti-tank positions in real-time, while Unit 1200 operates under civilian maritime covers to gather naval intelligence.
*   **EW and Malware**: Both sides utilize signal interception, malware, and fake social media accounts to disrupt communication loops.

---

## Intelligence Casualties: The Mokalled and Rayan Assassinations

The intelligence duel has resulted in high-ranking casualties on both sides. In March 2026, Israel assassinated Hezbollah’s Head of Intelligence, Hassan Mokalled. Mokalled was targeted after attempting to penetrate the IDF's encrypted communication networks.

Simultaneously, Israeli strikes targeted the command centers of the elite Radwan Force. These strikes killed Abu Ali Rayan, the commander of the southern sector, along with several staff officers in six command centers.

---

## The Diplomatic Ceiling: Ceasefire Terms and Unconditional Withdrawal

Following the launch of Operation Khaybar-1, Hezbollah has raised its diplomatic ceiling. The group's leadership declared that any ceasefire talks are contingent upon specific conditions:
1.  **Unconditional Withdrawal**: The absolute withdrawal of Israeli troops from all newly occupied coordinates and the original five border points.
2.  **Complete Halt**: A total cessation of Israeli air and ground hostilities across Lebanon.

This position rejects compromise, with the group demanding a unilateral Israeli withdrawal similar to the 2000 exit.

---

## Conclusion: The Limits of the Border Land Offensive

The launch of Operation Khaybar-1 and the strikes on the Dadu command show the limits of Israel's ground offensive. By maintaining the capability to coordinate and launch over 100 ballistic missiles despite leadership losses, Hezbollah has demonstrated that its command structures remain operational.

As the IDF manages the armored losses and the intelligence challenges of the campaign, the strategic options are narrowing. The reliance of both sides on high-intensity strikes and electronic warfare suggests that the conflict will remain focused on attrition, with high costs for any attempt to advance beyond the Litani River.
