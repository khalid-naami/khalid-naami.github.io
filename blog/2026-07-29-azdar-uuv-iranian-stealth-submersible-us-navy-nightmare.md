# Stealth Sub: The UUV Straining the US Navy in Hormuz

As the maritime confrontation in the Strait of Hormuz intensifies, the U.S. Navy faces an asymmetric threat that challenges its traditional dominance in regional waters. Israeli and Western defense analysts have identified Iran's new stealth Unmanned Underwater Vehicle (UUV), named \"Azdar\" (أزدار), as a significant threat to U.S. carrier strike groups. Operating silently in the shallow waters of the Persian Gulf, this autonomous system has shifted the balance of naval power.

## The Azdar Stealth UUV: A New Undersea Paradigm

The Azdar is a stealth autonomous submersible designed to operate in high-threat littoral zones. Developed by the IRGC Navy, the UUV is engineered to bypass active sonar and magnetic anomaly detectors used by coalition anti-submarine warfare (ASW) units.

The deployment of the Azdar UUV reflects a broader shift in Iran's naval doctrine, moving away from large surface vessels toward low-cost, high-volume autonomous systems. In the narrow and shallow confines of the Strait of Hormuz, these quiet submersibles can patrol undetected, monitoring shipping lanes and positioning themselves to intercept hostile naval forces.

---

## Lithium Power and Silent Subsurface Patrols

The primary technological feature of the Azdar UUV is its propulsion system, which utilizes high-capacity lithium batteries. Unlike diesel-electric submersibles that must regularly snorkel to recharge or produce acoustic signatures, the Azdar operates in near-complete silence:
*   **Operating Speed**: The submersible can cruise at speeds between 18 and 25 knots.
*   **Endurance**: It can conduct autonomous patrols for up to four days on a single charge.
*   **Range**: The lithium battery pack allows the UUV to cover over 600 kilometers silently, keeping its acoustic signature below the background noise of the busy shipping lane.

---

## Autonomous AI Wolfpacks and the CL-20 Threat

The IRGC is developing advanced variants of the Azdar UUV, leveraging solid-state battery technology to increase energy density. These upgrades will support miniature \"wolfpack\" swarms weighing approximately 350 kilograms.

These swarms will operate autonomously using integrated AI, communicating via low-profile snorkels while submerged to coordinate attacks on hostile targets. The submersibles will be armed with specialized warheads containing CL-20 explosives. With high energy density, a 50-kilogram CL-20 warhead can deliver sufficient explosive force to penetrate the double-hulled designs of advanced warships, including Virginia-class submarines.

---

## The Threat to Arleigh Burke-Class Destroyers

The primary tactical target for the Azdar UUV network is the Arleigh Burke-class guided-missile destroyer. These destroyers serve as the primary defensive screen for U.S. aircraft carriers, including the USS *Gerald R. Ford*, USS *Abraham Lincoln*, and USS *George Washington* groups operating in the region.

Iran has configured the guidance and sensor arrays of the Azdar specifically to target the acoustic and electromagnetic profiles of these destroyers. By neutralizing these defensive screens, the UUV network aims to expose U.S. aircraft carriers to direct ballistic and cruise missile strikes, bypassing their primary air defense shields.

---

## Target Scarcity and the Shift to Civilian Infrastructure

The deployment of these naval assets occurs as the U.S. administration faces target scarcity in its bombing campaign. Following the failure of the secret commando raid to extract 450 kilograms of enriched uranium, Donald Trump declared that there were few remaining military targets in Iran after striking its surface navy and airbases.

To maintain pressure, U.S. and Israeli planners are shifting their target list toward civilian infrastructure. Plans are being drafted to strike Iranian commercial ports and financial institutions, aiming to secure a decisive victory image before negotiating a ceasefire.

---

## The Deterrence Balance and Commercial Ports Retaliation

Tehran has warned that any strike targeting its civilian commercial ports will result in immediate retaliation against regional maritime hubs. The IRGC has identified commercial ports in the Gulf, such as Abu Dhabi, as potential targets in the event of an attack on Iranian facilities.

This threat has established a deterrence balance. To protect their logistics networks, U.S. carrier groups have stayed more than 1,000 miles away from the Persian Gulf. Any vessel carrying oil or goods destined for the U.S. or Israel remains vulnerable to UUV strikes if they attempt to transit the Strait of Hormuz without authorization.

---

## Conclusion: The Twilight of Conventional Naval Supremacy

The deployment of the Azdar UUV in the Strait of Hormuz highlights the limits of conventional naval power in contested waters. By utilizing quiet, low-cost autonomous submersibles, Iran has created a threat that cannot be easily countered by carrier strike groups.

As the U.S. and Israel consider strikes against civilian ports, the threat of mutual economic damage remains a key constraint. The presence of stealth UUV networks in the strait ensures that any attempt to bypass Iran's shipping regulations will carry a high risk of vessel loss, forcing a reassessment of naval operations in the region.
