# China Warns U.S. Over Philippines Davao Fuel Depot

The strategic confrontation between the United States and China has expanded from the Strait of Hormuz to the Western Pacific. China's Defense Minister, Dong Jun, issued a warning to Washington, declaring that Beijing will not comply with the U.S. blockade of Iranian shipping. Concurrently, China's Foreign Ministry has warned of severe consequences regarding reports that the U.S. military plans to build a new logistical fuel depot in Davao, Philippines, to support the First Island Chain defense network.

## Dong Jun's Declaration: Legitimacy of Iranian Hormuz Control

China's Defense Ministry has challenged the legal basis of the U.S. maritime blockade in the Gulf:
*   **The Statement**: Minister Dong Jun declared that Iran maintains operational sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz.
*   **International Legitimacy**: China rejects any U.S.-led efforts to restrict passage, asserting that bilateral trade treaties remain valid.
*   **No Compliance**: Beijing made clear it will not alter its commercial shipping patterns to accommodate unilateral U.S. restrictions.

---

## Respecting Trade Treaties: China's Crucial Energy Supply

China's reliance on Gulf crude dictates its refusal to accept shipping blockades:
*   **Energy Imports**: Iran provides approximately 10% to 15% of China's total oil imports, sold at stable market prices.
*   **Economic Interest**: Accepting U.S. sanctions would disrupt energy security and manufacturing costs in Chinese industrial hubs.
*   **Escort Willingness**: Beijing has warned it is prepared to deploy naval escorts to protect its commercial vessels if they are targeted.

---

## The Davao Fuel Depot: U.S. Indo-Pacific Logistical Base

U.S. planners are expanding their logistical network in the Western Pacific to manage regional containment:
*   **Davao Facility**: Reports indicate the U.S. military is planning a new fuel storage facility in Davao, Philippines.
*   **Logistical Security**: The facility is designed to secure fuel supplies for U.S. Navy and Air Force assets in the South China Sea.
*   **Local Cooperation**: The project relies on defense agreement expansions negotiated with the Philippine government.

---

## The First Island Chain: Confronting the Davao Grid

The proposed Davao facility is key to the U.S. defense posture in East Asia:
*   **Island Chain Focus**: The facility supports operations along the First Island Chain, stretching from Japan to the Philippines.
*   **Forward Deployments**: Stockpiling fuel in Davao allows U.S. warships to maintain operations without returning to distant hubs like Guam.
*   **Beijing's View**: China views the project as a direct attempt to establish offensive logistics hubs near its maritime borders.

---

## China Foreign Ministry Warning: High Risk of Armed Clash

China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson has warned regional governments against hosting foreign military hubs:
*   **The Warning**: Hosting U.S. military assets increases the risk of regional conflict for the host nation.
*   **Outside Interference**: Beijing stated that inviting non-regional powers to build bases damages local stability.
*   **National Interest**: China warned that regional states risk their own security by participating in U.S. containment networks.

---

## Unilateral Hegemony: The Proliferation of Military Hubs

Beijing and Moscow have criticized the expansion of U.S. bases as a form of unilateral hegemonism:
*   **Base Proliferation**: The U.S. operates hundreds of military bases globally to enforce its unilateral policies.
*   **Destabilization**: Eurasian diplomats argue that these bases trigger local arms races and political instability.
*   **The Alternative**: China and Russia advocate for a multi-polar security model that respects regional sovereignty.

---

## Sino-Russian Strategic Coordination: Redefining Global Governance

The Foreign Ministers of Russia and China held meetings to coordinate their diplomatic responses:
*   **The Meeting**: The ministers discussed the U.S.-Iran conflict, the Ukraine crisis, and global governance reform.
*   **Joint Strategy**: The two powers agreed to coordinate their positions ahead of the upcoming summit between their heads of state.
*   **Opposing Hegemony**: Both states pledged to coordinate resistance against U.S. unilateral sanctions and blockade policies.

---

## Joint Gulf Drills: Challenging U.S. Unilateral Rules

To demonstrate their coordination, China and Russia are preparing joint naval maneuvers:
*   **The Drills**: The two navies have proposed joint exercises with the Iranian Navy in the Persian Gulf.
*   **Freedom of Navigation**: The drills seek to assert international transit rights against U.S. unilateral restrictions.
*   **Force Presence**: The deployment of advanced warships highlights the multi-polar nature of maritime security in the Gulf.

---

## Conclusion: The Geo-Strategic Pivot from Hormuz to Davao

The confrontation over the Davao fuel depot and the Strait of Hormuz highlights the link between Middle Eastern and Pacific security.

While Dong Jun's warnings confirm China's refusal to respect the Hormuz blockade, U.S. plans for a fuel depot in Davao show how Washington is reinforcing its First Island Chain posture. China's warning to regional states against hosting U.S. hubs highlights the growing risk of conflict in the Western Pacific. As China and Russia coordinate their diplomatic and naval actions, the expansion of the standoff confirms that unilateral military blockades face structured resistance from Eurasian alliances.
