# Secret Document: Redrawing the Middle East Map

A highly sensitive military document declassified by the Israeli Defense Forces (IDF) has confirmed that the primary objective of the ongoing regional conflict is a long-term geopolitical and territorial restructuring of the Middle East. According to the document, the war was never solely about Iran's nuclear or ballistic program, which was already degraded. Instead, the U.S. and Israel have executed a plan to secure absolute Western hegemony over West Asia, severing China's \"Belt and Road\" trade corridor and redrawing the maritime boundaries of the Persian Gulf.

## The Declassified Plan: Beyond the Nuclear Program

The declassified IDF document reveals that the public focus on Iran's nuclear enrichment facilities was a tactical narrative. The true, strategic goal of the campaign is a structural redrawing of the Middle East map.

The document confirms that U.S. and Israeli planners targeted the nuclear facilities as a entry point to initiate a wider conflict. The ultimate objective is the removal of Iran as a regional power, allowing the U.S. to establish a new, centralized security architecture that aligns the region with Western economic interests.

---

## Blocking China's Belt and Road in West Asia

A primary strategic goal of the restructuring is to block China's economic expansion into the West Asian sector. The Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) relies on secure transit corridors through Iran and Iraq to access European markets.

By maintaining a state of high-intensity conflict and establishing U.S. military control over these transit zones, Washington has successfully blocked China's trade routes. This strategy mirrors the U.S. national security interest in securing Greenland to control northern maritime routes, showing a consistent focus on blocking Eurasian integration.

---

## The $922 Billion UAE Off-Budget Funding

The declassified document exposes the financial mechanism supporting the campaign. The United Arab Emirates has committed a massive **$922 billion** to finance U.S. military operations and regional reconstruction:
*   **Off-budget funding**: The capital was allocated through sovereign channels, bypassing U.S. Congressional oversight.
*   **Targeted distribution**: The funds support U.S. bases in Syria, Kurdish forces in Iraq, and the reconstruction of Israeli civil infrastructure.
*   **The Incentive**: In exchange for this funding, the U.S. has agreed to permanently secure the UAE's territorial claims in the Gulf.

---

## The Territorial Payout: Seizing the Three Islands

The primary territorial incentive for the UAE's financial commitment is the permanent acquisition of the three disputed Gulf islands: Abu Musa, Greater Tunb, and Lesser Tunb.

Under the joint plan, U.S. ground forces (including the 82nd Airborne Division) will occupy the islands, ejecting the Iranian garrisons that have held them since 1971. Once secured, the islands will be transferred to UAE sovereignty, establishing a forward naval screen to monitor maritime traffic in the central Gulf.

---

## Renaming the Strait: The \"Trump Strait\" Plan

The plan includes a symbolic and legal rebranding of the region's primary maritime corridor. Under the draft agreement, the Strait of Hormuz will be renamed the **\"Trump Strait\" (مضيق ترامب)**.

This rebranding is accompanied by a new legal framework that would allow U.S. and allied naval forces to impose maritime tolls on commercial shipping transiting the strait. The tolls would be used to finance the permanent U.S. naval presence, establishing U.S. authority over the primary energy corridor.

---

## The Saudi-UAE Rivalry for Gulf Identity

The massive financial commitment by the UAE has intensified a rivalry between Abu Dhabi and Riyadh over the leadership of the post-war Gulf:
*   **MBZ's Bid**: UAE President Mohammed bin Zayed (MBZ) is utilizing the $922 billion package to position the UAE as the primary U.S. security partner.
*   **MBS's Stance**: Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (MBS) is operating behind the scenes, supporting the U.S. campaign but resisting UAE dominance.
*   **Rhetoric Shift**: Israeli and Western media have aligned with this shift, systematically replacing references to the \"Persian Gulf\" with the \"Arabian Gulf\" in their official documentation.

---

## Pakistan's Decoupling and the Bagram Prize

The geopolitical restructuring extends to South Asia, where Pakistan is adjusting its strategic alignment. To secure its position, Islamabad has decoupled its security policy from Tehran:
*   **The Kashmir Ceasefire**: Pakistan has leveraged the Trump-brokered ceasefire with India to stabilize its eastern border.
*   **The Western Orbit**: In exchange for financial support from Saudi Arabia and the U.S., Pakistan will maintain its nuclear deterrent within the Western security system.
*   **The Bagram Corridor**: The U.S. plans to utilize this alignment to regain access to Bagram Airfield in Afghanistan, establishing a security buffer to isolate western China.

---

## The Symmetrical Threat: Burning the Gulf Oil

The Iranian leadership has warned that any attempt to execute this geopolitical redrawing will result in a regional conflict. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf declared that the war has entered its most sensitive phase.

The IRGC has prepared a scorched-earth plan: if U.S. forces attempt to seize the islands or rename the strait, Iran will target all oil and gas installations in the UAE, Bahrain, and Saudi Arabia (including Aramco). The destruction of these facilities would trigger a global energy collapse, rendering the $922 billion UAE funding useless and forcing a U.S. retreat.

---

## Conclusion: The Final Strategic Choice

The declassified IDF document confirms that the Middle East is facing its most significant geopolitical transition since the end of World War II.

If the U.S. and Israel succeed in enforcing the 15-point capitulation framework, they will establish a unilateral regional order, severing China's trade routes and securing U.S. maritime control over the new Trump Strait. However, if the IRGC executes its symmetrical threat to target Gulf energy infrastructure, the campaign will result in the collapse of CENTCOM's regional presence, forcing a U.S. retreat to Djibouti and ending Western military influence in West Asia.
