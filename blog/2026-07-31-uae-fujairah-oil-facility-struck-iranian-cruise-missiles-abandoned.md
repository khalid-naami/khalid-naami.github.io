# UAE Fujairah Struck: US and Israel Abandon Abu Dhabi

The security architecture of the Persian Gulf is facing realignments following a series of missile and drone strikes on the United Arab Emirates. Although local media reported the interception of several threats, official sources confirmed that four Iranian cruise missiles struck oil facilities in Fujairah, triggering fires. Despite leaks suggesting immediate U.S. and Israeli retaliation, both allies have declined to intervene, exposing the limits of security partnerships in the region.

## The Fujairah Strike: Four Cruise Missiles Hit Oil Facilities

Iranian cruise missiles penetrated regional air defense networks to strike energy infrastructure:
*   **The Attack**: Four land-attack cruise missiles hit storage areas at the Fujairah oil terminal.
*   **Active Fires**: The impact triggered fires in the storage tanks, requiring emergency containment.
*   **Defense Failures**: Local defense systems, including deployed Iron Dome units, failed to intercept the low-altitude missiles.

---

## Tanker Targets: The Barakah Struck by Iranian Drones

Offshore maritime operations also faced direct attacks during the incident:
*   **The Barakah**: The empty, UAE-owned oil tanker *Barakah* was hit by two one-way attack drones.
*   **UKMTO Report**: The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported a separate strike on a tanker 144 miles north of Fujairah.
*   **No Casualties**: The state oil company confirmed that no crew members were injured, though operations were suspended.

---

## The 24-Hour Promise: CNN Leaks of U.S.-Israeli Retaliation

Diplomatic sources initially indicated that a combined military response was imminent:
*   **The Leak**: UAE officials leaked to CNN and *Maariv* that a joint U.S.-Israeli response would occur within 24 hours.
*   **Public Statements**: Regional media highlighted the reports to reassure local financial markets.
*   **The Delay**: As the deadline passed without military action, the reports were retracted by official spokespersons.

---

## The Glass House: Iranian Military Warnings to Abu Dhabi

Iranian military commanders issued warnings to regional governments regarding cooperation with foreign forces:
*   **Glass House Warning**: Iranian spokespersons stated that the UAE operates in a "fragile glass house" dependent on stability.
*   **Target Selection**: The command warned that if local bases are used by U.S. or Israeli forces, all UAE infrastructure will be targeted.
*   **Pretext Refuted**: Iranian media denied launching the strikes, claiming the incidents resulted from unauthorized U.S. naval activity.

---

## Israeli Abandonment: Home Front Command Holds Defensive Policy

The Israeli military has chosen to avoid involvement in the Gulf confrontation:
*   **Defensive Stance**: The Home Front Command confirmed there is no change in defensive guidelines, maintaining status quo until May 7, 2026.
*   **Aman Assessment**: Military intelligence assessed that the conflict is regional and does not pose a direct threat to Israel.
*   **No Action**: The government declined to authorize airstrikes or deploy assets to defend UAE airspace.

---

## Centcom's Stance: Restricting Action to Direct U.S. Navy Targets

U.S. military commands have limited their operations to direct self-defense:
*   **Self-Defense Focus**: Central Command stated it would only engage targets that threaten U.S. Navy vessels.
*   **No Gulf Protection**: Centcom has not launched retaliatory strikes on Iranian territory in response to the Fujairah hits.
*   **Command Limits**: Planners are conserving remaining interceptor stocks for high-priority U.S. assets.

---

## Sinking the Speedboats: Trump's Claims in the Strait of Hormuz

The U.S. President has focused on localized engagements while avoiding wider escalation:
*   **Speedboat Sinking**: Trump announced that U.S. vessels sank seven small Iranian speedboats in the Strait.
*   **Annihilation Threat**: Trump warned on Fox News that any direct attack on U.S. warships would lead to the annihilation of Iran.
*   **Avoiding Land Strikes**: The administration has avoided authorizing strikes on Iranian land bases despite the Fujairah damage.

---

## Market Repercussions: Brent Crude Spikes to One Hundred Fourteen

The strikes on oil infrastructure had an immediate effect on global energy markets:
*   **Price Spike**: Brent crude oil prices rose from $110 to $114 per barrel within one hour of the reports.
*   **Insurance Hikes**: Underwriters increased war risk premiums for all vessels loading cargo in the Gulf.
*   **Logistical Halts**: Several shipping lines suspended transit bookings through the Strait of Hormuz.

---

## Conclusion: The Limits of Allied Protection for the Gulf States

The Fujairah strikes and the lack of U.S. or Israeli retaliation highlight the challenges facing Gulf security.

By choosing not to respond to the four cruise missiles that hit the Fujairah terminal, the U.S. and Israel have demonstrated that their security guarantees are limited. While Trump issues warnings on Fox News and claims local speedboat engagements, the refusal of the Home Front Command to alter its stance until May 7 confirms that Israel will not risk its assets for Abu Dhabi. As Brent crude rises to $114, the incident shows that Gulf states remain exposed to regional threats despite hosting foreign defense systems.
