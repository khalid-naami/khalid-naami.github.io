# Oil Diplomacy: The Secret Trump-Putin Deal on Iran

The convergence of military escalation in the Persian Gulf and shifting global oil prices has triggered intense backchannel diplomacy. Facing mounting domestic pressure to lower fuel prices, U.S. President Donald Trump initiated a crucial phone call with Russian President Vladimir Putin. This conversation has laid the groundwork for a potential grand bargain, connecting regional settlements in both Ukraine and Iran to stabilize global energy markets.

## The Secret Call: Trump’s Oil Price Challenge

With domestic polls indicating that 67% of Americans anticipate rising gasoline prices, and only 29% supporting the ongoing military campaign in the Middle East, the U.S. administration faced a political emergency. To cool the market, Trump contacted Russian President Vladimir Putin.

During the call, the two leaders discussed a coordinated de-escalation plan. In exchange for Russian mediation with Iran and assistance in stabilizing oil prices, Trump signaled a willingness to offer concessions to Moscow regarding the conflict in Ukraine. The proposal, a 6-point plan drafted in coordination with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, aims to establish a joint framework for ceasefires in both theatres.

---

## Market Volatility: The Brent Crude Rollercoaster

The impact of the conflict on energy markets has been highly volatile. On Monday, Brent crude surged by nearly 30%, peaking at $119.50 per barrel, its highest level in three years, driven by threats to shipping in the Persian Gulf.

However, following news of the Trump-Putin call and coordinated G7 announcements, prices fell by more than 10% the following day, dropping below $89.85 per barrel. Analysts indicate that this rapid fluctuation reflects the market's sensitivity to political interventions, oscillating between fears of a total energy blockade and hopes for a negotiated settlement.

---

## Coordinated Interventions: G7 and IEA Strategic Reserves

To counter price increases, the United States coordinated a joint response with the Group of Seven (G7) and the International Energy Agency (IEA). The coalition threatened to release massive strategic petroleum reserves to flood the market and offset any supply deficits.

U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright reassured markets that the price spikes would be temporary, citing record U.S. domestic production and abundant global supplies. These assurances, combined with predictions that shipping through the Strait of Hormuz would gradually normalize, helped cool speculative trading and stabilize contract prices.

## Iran’s Economic Warfare and Ceasefire Redlines

Iran's military strategy has focused on leveraging its position in energy markets to force an end to the war. The IRGC previously threatened to block all transit through the Strait of Hormuz, which handles approximately 20 million barrels of oil per day.

However, once Trump signaled an accelerated timeline for ending operations—claiming in a CNBC interview that the military campaign was ahead of schedule—Tehran clarified its position. Iranian officials stated that if U.S. and Israeli airstrikes on Iranian infrastructure cease, Iran will halt its retaliatory strikes, preferring a frozen conflict over continued economic escalation.

---

## The Strait of Hormuz Threat and OPEC Production Cuts

The threat to the Strait of Hormuz remains a primary concern for energy analysts. A prolonged blockade of this vital maritime chokepoint could lead to severe disruptions in global supply chains.

These concerns are compounded by significant production cuts among Gulf producers. Saudi Arabia has reduced output by 2 to 2.5 million barrels per day, Iraq by 2.9 million, the UAE by 500,000 to 800,000, and Kuwait by 500,000. These cuts, totaling approximately 6.7 million barrels per day (about a third of their combined capacity), have limited the market's spare capacity, making it vulnerable to supply disruptions.

## Israel’s Alternative Axis: Sidelining Turkey

To protect its economy from maritime blockades and shipping disruptions, Israel has developed an alternative trade and supply network. This system relies on a strategic quad-alliance:
1.  **Azerbaijan**: Supplies 100% of the crude oil needed for Israeli domestic consumption and military operations.
2.  **Greece**: Absorbs transit costs and secures shipping routes through the eastern Mediterranean.
3.  **Ethiopia**: Serves as a logistical air hub, receiving international cargo and flying it directly to Israeli airports.
4.  **India**: Acts as a key partner for manufacturing, technology, and industrial components.

This network has sidelined Turkey's President Erdogan, who had expected to position Turkey as a key intermediary under the Trump administration, only to see Washington support this alternative axis.

## Conclusion: The Geopolitical Price of Crude

The fluctuations in global energy markets highlight the connection between geopolitics and commodity prices. While military operations can drive rapid price increases, backchannel diplomacy and coordinated reserve releases can stabilize markets.

As negotiations continue, the strategic balance remains delicate. The U.S. administration aims to conclude its operations quickly to prevent domestic economic backlash, while Israel seeks to isolate Iran from international markets. The outcome of the Trump-Putin negotiations will determine whether the region transitions toward a negotiated ceasefire or continues to experience localized energy shocks.
