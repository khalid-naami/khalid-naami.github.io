# China Energy autarky & North Korea Neutrality

While the closure of the Strait of Hormuz has sent shockwaves through European economies, China has demonstrated remarkable resilience. Beijing anticipated this vulnerability during Donald Trump’s first term, launching a systematic energy autarky strategy. Meanwhile, in another geopolitical shift, North Korea has maintained a calculated neutrality, refraining from military shipments to Iran and avoiding official condolences for the late Supreme Leader, seeking to keep diplomatic channels open ahead of a scheduled summit with Trump.

## Energy Shield: China Undisturbed by Hormuz Closure

The blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and the loss of oil exports from Venezuela and Iran have not disrupted China's economy:
*   **European Panic**: European states are experiencing severe inflation and energy shortages as maritime lanes close.
*   **Chinese Resilience**: China's domestic markets, fuel distribution, and manufacturing sectors have remained entirely stable.
*   **Strategic Preparation**: The stability is the result of years of structural planning designed to withstand a total maritime embargo.

---

## Trump's First Term: The Catalyst for Beijing's Autarky

Beijing's shift toward self-reliance was triggered by the geopolitical lessons of Trump's first presidency:
*   **The Trade Confrontation**: The technological battles and tariff campaigns of the late 2010s alerted China's leadership to the vulnerability of global supply chains.
*   **Decoupling Strategy**: Under شي جين بينغ, the Communist Party launched a program to decouple critical manufacturing from Western technologies and energy markets.
*   **Autarky Focus**: Domestic production of energy and raw materials was prioritized as a matter of national survival.

---

## Strategic Reserves: China's Massive Crude Oil Stockpiles

A key element of China's preparation involved the accumulation of physical energy buffers:
*   **NYT Reports**: According to the New York Times, Beijing utilized the low-price periods of the early 2020s to stockpile massive quantities of crude oil.
*   **Subsurface Storage**: These reserves, held in underground salt caverns and surface depots, provide a multi-month buffer against supply disruptions.
*   **Controlled Consumption**: China has managed its release of these reserves to keep domestic fuel prices stable.

---

## The German WWII Template: Converting Coal to Petrochemicals

China has resolved its dependence on imported oil for industrial manufacturing through domestic coal:
*   **The Technology**: Beijing revived and scaled coal-to-chemical technologies originally developed in Germany during World War II.
*   **Petrochemical Autarky**: The country converted its plastics, rubber, and synthetic fiber plants to use domestic coal as feedstocks instead of imported petroleum.
*   **Scale of Consumption**: China's chemical sector now consumes more coal annually than the entire domestic consumption of the United States.

---

## EV Transition: Ending Oil Dependence in Transport

The rapid adoption of electric vehicles has insulated China's transport sector from global oil spikes:
*   **Market Dominance**: China is the largest market for electric vehicles (EVs) globally, representing over half of global sales.
*   **Fuel Demand Decline**: The rapid adoption of EVs has led to a steady decline in domestic gasoline and diesel consumption.
*   **Industrial Resilience**: Local fertilizer prices remain low due to coal-based ammonia production, allowing China to export energy aid to Vietnam and the Philippines.

---

## North Korea's Silence: Distancing from Tehran's War

Despite historic military links, North Korea is maintaining a calculated distance from the current conflict:
*   **No Weapons Shipments**: South Korean intelligence reports confirm that Pyongyang has sent no arms or military hardware to Iran since the conflict began on February 28.
*   **No Condolences**: Kim Jong Un did not send official condolences following the death of Iranian Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei in U.S. strikes.
*   **No Congratulatory Messages**: Pyongyang did not congratulate Mojtaba Khamenei on his succession to the leadership post.

---

## Raw Material Advancements: Kim's Carbon Fiber Solid-Fuel Missiles

While maintaining diplomatic silence, North Korea continues to develop advanced missile systems:
*   **Carbon Fiber Materials**: In late March, Kim Jong Un supervised a ground test of a solid-fuel rocket engine constructed from lightweight carbon fiber.
*   **Increased Range**: The carbon fiber body reduces weight, allowing missiles to carry heavier, multi-warhead payloads capable of reaching the U.S. mainland.
*   **Strategic Stance**: The tests demonstrate that North Korea is focused on expanding its own nuclear deterrent rather than supporting regional proxies.

---

## Geopolitical Isolation: Iran Fights Alone in the Gulf

The caution of China, Russia, and North Korea has left Iran in a solitary geopolitical position:
*   **Hormuz Friction**: While Beijing and Moscow accept Iran's sovereignty, they reject its total blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, which disrupts international shipping.
*   **Diplomatic Distance**: Both powers have chosen to treat the Gulf conflict as a purely bilateral war between Tehran and Washington.
*   **No Active Support**: Tehran is fighting without direct military coordination from its East Asian partners, leaving it isolated in its maritime campaign.

---

## Conclusion: The Reshaped Eurasian Security Alignment

The Strait of Hormuz crisis has revealed that the "Eurasian Axis" is defined by national self-interest rather than joint defense.

China's energy autarky—built on massive oil stockpiles, coal-to-chemical conversion, and rapid EV adoption—has protected it from the fuel crisis affecting Europe. At the same time, North Korea's strategic silence and refusal to supply weapons to Iran show a desire to maintain diplomatic options ahead of the May summit with Donald Trump. Left without military support from Beijing or Pyongyang, Iran faces the full weight of the U.S. and Israeli air campaign alone in the Gulf.
