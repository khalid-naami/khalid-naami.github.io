# Haifa Port Blinded: Greek Radar Hit by Drones

The Iranian military has executed a targeted drone strike on the primary maritime surveillance and electronic warfare node at Haifa Port. According to military statement No. 46, a swarm of suicide drones successfully bypassed air defenses, striking a newly deployed backup radar system provided by the Hellenic (Greek) Armed Forces. The destruction of this radar has disabled the port's early warning capabilities, halting commercial shipping and forcing authorities to redirect incoming container vessels to alternate Mediterranean hubs.

## The Haifa Strike: Statement 46 and the Radar Target

The strike on Haifa Port represents a shift in targeting strategy. In statement No. 46, the Iranian command confirmed the deployment of a high-density swarm of suicide drones targeting the port's industrial sector.

The primary target was not the cargo cranes or warehouses, but the localized air-defense and maritime surveillance radar network. The drones, utilizing low-altitude flight paths, struck the radar assembly, disabling the early warning feed and leaving the northern maritime border without real-time tracking coverage.

---

## The Greek Defense Node: Hitting the Backup EW Radar

The disabled system was not a standard Israeli defense asset, but a specialized electronic warfare and radar unit supplied by the Hellenic (Greek) Armed Forces.

The Greek-operated radar was deployed to Haifa as a temporary backup to replace native Israeli systems that had been damaged in previous strikes. By targeting this specific system, Iran has neutralized the international assistance provided to bolster Israel's air defense network, confirming that foreign-operated assets are treated as legitimate targets within the combat zone.

---

## The Shipping Collapse: Satellite Imagery Reveals Deficits

The destruction of the radar has had an immediate impact on Haifa's commercial operations. Satellite imagery has confirmed a dramatic decline in port traffic:
*   **Vessels scheduled**: The port's daily registry scheduled the arrival of 19 container ships.
*   **Vessels received**: Following the strike, only four vessels were able to dock and discharge their cargo.
*   **Redirections**: The remaining ships were ordered to redirect to alternative ports in Cyprus and Greece due to the loss of radar guidance.
*   **Registry Encryption**: Port authorities have encrypted the maritime tracking feed to prevent public disclosure of the shipping gridlock.

---

## The Symmetrical Response: Caspian Sea vs. Haifa Port

The strike on Haifa's port infrastructure was designed as a symmetrical response to Israeli operations. Previously, Israeli forces had targeted port facilities in Iran's Caspian Sea sector, attempting to disrupt trade routes with Russia.

By disabling Haifa's main maritime radar, Tehran has established a direct equivalent, demonstrating that any strike on its northern ports will result in the immediate closure of Israel's primary Mediterranean terminal.

## The 10% Leak: Why Fixed Radars Cannot Escape Strikes

The success of the Haifa strike highlights the limits of Israel's air defense shield. According to official IDF admissions, the average interception rate for drone and missile salvos is approximately 90%.

While a 90% interception rate is effective for protecting large residential areas, it leaves a **10% operational leak**. When a swarm of 15 to 20 drones is deployed against a static, high-value target like a fixed radar antenna, the 10% leak is sufficient to ensure a direct hit, as a single impact is enough to disable the radar's transceiver arrays.

## ELTA Systems: The Architecture of Israeli Air Sensors

The tracking of low-flying drones relies on technology designed by ELTA Systems, a subsidiary of Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI). Since 1967, ELTA has been the primary provider of radar and electronic intelligence systems for the IDF.

ELTA's radars utilize Active Electronically Scanned Array (AESA) technology, allowing them to track multiple low-signature threats simultaneously. However, the high sensitivity of these arrays makes them vulnerable to physical damage; even minor shrapnel impacts from a drone detonation can disable the electronic phase shifters, rendering the radar blind.

---

## EL/M-2084 MMR: Hitting the Heart of Iron Dome

The defense of northern Israel is coordinated through the **EL/M-2084 Multi-Mission Radar (MMR)**:
*   **The System**: The primary sensor for both the Iron Dome and the David's Sling air defense networks.
*   **The Mission**: Tracking incoming short-range rockets, cruise missiles, and low-flying UAVs.
*   **The Target**: Iranian drone swarms have focused on targeting these fixed radar nodes to blind the air defense batteries before launching ballistic missile salvos.

---

## The Naval Decoupled Defense: Sa'ar Corvettes and AESA

To mitigate the loss of land-based radars, the Israeli Navy relies on mobile sensors mounted on its Sa'ar class corvettes:
*   **The EL/M-2248 MF-STAR**: A 3D AESA radar mounted on Sa'ar 5 and Sa'ar 6 corvettes, providing 360-degree air defense coverage.
*   **Decoupled tracking**: The naval radars can transmit tracking data to land-based Iron Dome batteries, partially replacing the disabled port radar.
*   **The Constraint**: Corvettes are vulnerable to anti-ship cruise missiles and must operate at a distance, reducing the accuracy of the tracking loop for low-altitude drones in urban port environments.

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Fixed Radar Infrastructure

The drone strike on the Greek-operated radar at Haifa Port and the subsequent collapse of shipping traffic confirm the vulnerability of Israel's fixed defense infrastructure.

By exploiting the 10% interception leak, Iranian drone swarms have successfully disabled critical early warning systems. The redirection of commercial shipping and the encryption of the port's registry prove that the economic impact of the war is expanding. If the IDF cannot protect its primary radar nodes from low-cost drone strikes, it will struggle to coordinate its air defense batteries, leaving the industrial zones of Haifa and Tel Aviv exposed to future missile campaigns.
