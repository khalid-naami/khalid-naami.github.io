# Black Day: 6 U.S. Aircraft Lost at Hormuz

In the most devastating day for the U.S. Air Force since the campaign began, six American aircraft were hit or downed near the Strait of Hormuz and southern Iran. Two A-10 Warthogs and an F-15 Eagle crashed, a Black Hawk helicopter was shot down during a rescue mission, and an F-35 and F-16 sustained damage requiring emergency landings. The New York Times confirmed the simultaneous loss of the F-15 and A-10, while an Israeli official—not the Pentagon—was the first to confirm the rescue of an American pilot. Columnist Galb wrote that "the ambitious regime change plan has turned into a sad search for lost pilots in Iran's mountains."

## The F-15 and A-10: Simultaneous Losses

The New York Times, citing Pentagon sources, confirmed the simultaneous downing of two frontline combat aircraft:
*   **The F-15**: A U.S. Air Force F-15 Eagle was shot down over or near the Strait of Hormuz. The crew's fate remained unknown for hours.
*   **The A-10**: An A-10 Thunderbolt II (Warthog) ground-attack aircraft crashed near the Strait of Hormuz at approximately the same time.
*   **The Second A-10**: A second A-10 was struck in the Strait of Hormuz area—one sank while the other sustained severe damage.
*   **30 A-10s Deployed**: The USAF had deployed 30 A-10s for ground attack and reconnaissance missions across southern Iran.

---

## Black Hawk Down: Rescue Mission Destroyed

The attempt to recover the downed F-15 crew triggered additional losses:
*   **NewsMax Report**: Iran shot down a UH-60 Black Hawk helicopter that was participating in the search and rescue mission for the F-15 crew.
*   **Fox News Confirmation**: A parallel report confirmed the helicopter loss.
*   **The Cascade**: The downing of the rescue helicopter left the missing crew members stranded, complicating recovery operations in hostile territory.

---

## Additional Damage: F-35 and F-16 Hit

Beyond the confirmed crashes, two additional high-value aircraft sustained combat damage:
*   **The F-35**: An F-35 Lightning II sustained damage and was forced to make an emergency landing at a Saudi Arabian airfield.
*   **The F-16**: An F-16 Fighting Falcon was hit and required emergency recovery.
*   **Total Count**: Six aircraft were hit in a single operational day—2 A-10s (1 sank), 1 F-15 (downed), 1 Black Hawk (shot down), 1 F-35 (damaged), 1 F-16 (damaged).

---

## Israel Confirms: U.S. Pilot Rescued

In an extraordinary development, it was Israel—not the United States—that first confirmed the rescue of an American crew member:
*   **The Announcement**: At 1:36 PM, an Israeli official confirmed that one American crew member had been rescued from a downed aircraft in Iran and was alive.
*   **The Search**: The search continued for a second crew member.
*   **The Question**: Why did the confirmation come from Israel rather than the Pentagon? The implication is that Israeli special forces, not American assets, conducted the rescue after the Black Hawk was shot down.

---

## CENTCOM's Credibility: Denial Then Admission

The Pentagon's handling of the losses has severely damaged its credibility:
*   **Initial Denial**: CENTCOM initially denied that any fighter aircraft had been downed.
*   **23-Hour Reversal**: Twenty-three hours later, CENTCOM acknowledged the F-15 loss—after it became impossible to maintain the denial.
*   **The Pattern**: While CENTCOM searched for the pilot "elsewhere," the wreckage near Qeshm Island told a different story. The delayed acknowledgment suggests a systematic effort to manage the narrative of losses.

---

## Hegseth's Contradiction: Air Defenses "Neutralized"

The aircraft losses have exposed the fatal contradiction at the heart of the campaign's command:
*   **The Claim**: Defense Secretary Hegseth had repeatedly stated that all Iranian air defenses had been neutralized.
*   **The Reality**: If air defenses were neutralized, how were six aircraft hit or downed in a single day?
*   **The Firing**: Hegseth had fired the Joint Chiefs chairman who held a different operational assessment—the losses vindicate the dismissed commander's warnings.

---

## B-52 Vulnerability: If the F-15 Can't Escape

The losses have immediate implications for the broader air campaign:
*   **The Logic**: If an F-15—one of the fastest combat aircraft in the USAF inventory—could not escape Iranian air defenses, then the massive B-52 strategic bombers flying over Iran are at extreme risk.
*   **The Doctrine**: The losses challenge the assumption that air power alone can sustain a campaign against a state with integrated air defense systems.
*   **The Shift**: Israel cancelled its entire day's bombing agenda to focus resources on the pilot rescue, allowing Iran to launch intense strikes supporting Hezbollah against northern Israel.

## Galb's Verdict: From Regime Change to Pilot Search

The political commentary surrounding the losses has been devastating:
*   **The Column**: Galb wrote: "The ambitious plan for regime change has now turned into a sad search for lost pilots in Iran's mountains."
*   **The Shock**: The image of the world's most powerful military searching for downed pilots—rescued by Israeli, not American, forces—has shattered the narrative of overwhelming U.S. air superiority.
*   **The KC-135 Narrative**: The U.S. released imagery of KC-135 tankers refueling E-3 Sentry AWACS aircraft to demonstrate continued operational capability, but the gesture rings hollow against the backdrop of six lost aircraft.

## Conclusion: The Air Superiority Myth Collapses

The loss of six aircraft in a single day near the Strait of Hormuz marks the end of the assumption that the U.S. can wage an air campaign against Iran without significant losses.

The simultaneous downing of an F-15 and two A-10s, the shooting down of a Black Hawk rescue helicopter, and the combat damage to an F-35 and F-16 demonstrate that Iranian air defenses remain lethal despite claims of their neutralization. The fact that an Israeli official—not the Pentagon—confirmed the rescue of an American pilot reveals the dysfunction at the heart of the command structure. Hegseth's firing of the Joint Chiefs chairman who warned against this scenario has left the military leadership discredited, and Galb's verdict—"regime change has become a pilot search"—encapsulates the strategic collapse of the entire campaign's premise.
