Mitchell Report: U.S. Air Force Unprepared for China

July 30, 2026 · 5 min read
Khalid Naami
Founder, Owner, and CEO at Dashboard Options

A strategic study published by the Mitchell Institute for Aerospace Studies in April 2026 warns that the U.S. Air Force (USAF) has lost the capacity to win a peer-level conflict against China. The report, titled "Rebuilding America's Military Aviation: Balancing Air Force Capabilities to Fight a Peer Adversary," details how decades of underinvestment, combined with rapid munition depletion in Ukraine and Iran, have compromised U.S. air superiority. Without urgent structural changes, the report concludes that the U.S. homeland and key Indo-Pacific bases remain vulnerable.

The Mitchell Institute Report: Defeating the Superiority Illusion​

The published analysis has challenged long-standing assumptions about Western air dominance:

  • The Finding: The USAF lacks the combat capacity, operational range, and survival capability required to defeat a peer adversary.
  • Peer Adversary: The report specifically designates China's PLA and the Russian Air Force as peer adversaries capable of contesting U.S. systems.
  • The Consensus: Defense planners acknowledge that the conventional fleet cannot secure uncontested control over the Taiwan Strait.

Years of Underinvestment: Structural Causes of the Deficit​

The current limitations of the air fleet are the result of long-term budgetary decisions:

  • Modernization Delays: Key procurement programs for next-generation platforms have faced continuous administrative delays.
  • Legacy Reliance: The U.S. has relied on upgraded fourth-generation airframes, which are ineffective against modern integrated air defenses.
  • Fiscal Neglect: Budget allocations prioritized low-intensity asymmetrical conflicts over preparing for high-intensity peer wars.

June 2025 War Games: Failing to Defend Taiwan​

The report's conclusions are supported by simulation data from joint war games conducted in mid-2025:

  • The Simulation: Planners tested defense scenarios for Taiwan up to the year 2035.
  • Partial Modernization: In the first scenario, which assumed limited upgrades, U.S. forces failed to prevent the PLA from securing permanent control of the island.
  • Sortie Deficits: The failure was caused by a severe shortage of combat sorties due to the destruction of forward staging airfields.

The Attrition Crisis: Limits of Long-Term Sustainment​

Even under favorable conditions, the U.S. air fleet cannot sustain a prolonged campaign:

  • Full Modernization: The second scenario assumed stable, long-term funding for advanced acquisitions.
  • Sustainment Failure: Despite achieving initial intercepts, U.S. forces faced critical maintenance bottlenecks and high attrition rates.
  • Industrial Bottlenecks: U.S. defense manufacturing cannot produce replacement airframes or engines at the speed required during active combat.

The Strike Gap: Uncoordinated Sorties as a Threat​

A key tactical vulnerability identified in the study is the presence of an operational strike gap:

  • The Strike Gap: The term refers to periods where U.S. forces cannot execute coordinated, heavy strikes due to refueling and logistics delays.
  • PLA Advantage: These gaps give the Chinese military time to repair runways, rebuild air defense batteries, and reposition mobile assets.
  • Coordinated Need: The study emphasizes that sporadic, uncoordinated sorties are easily neutralized by modern layered defenses.

Exhausted Munitions: Depletion in Ukraine and Iran​

The primary constraint on U.S. operational planning is the depletion of precision-guided weapons:

  • Depleted Reserves: Long-range precision munitions have been heavily depleted to support operations in Ukraine and the Gulf campaign.
  • Supply Chains: U.S. factories require years to replenish advanced anti-ship and air-to-air missile stockpiles.
  • Defensive Gap: The shortage leaves the U.S. homeland and major logistics hubs in the Pacific without sufficient defensive buffers.

Twelve Recommendations: Mitchell's Urgent Push to Congress​

The report outlines twelve urgent strategic recommendations to prevent the collapse of air superiority:

  • Congressional Funding: Demanding that Congress immediately fund the procurement of new-generation strike platforms.
  • Production Acceleration: Prioritizing the mass assembly of stealth bombers and advanced multi-role fighters.
  • Munitions Focus: Establishing dedicated, high-output production lines for long-range precision-guided missiles.

The Boeing F-47 and B-21 Raider: Legacy Fleet Obsolete​

The study calls for a complete transition away from legacy platforms like the F-15, F-16, and F-35:

  • F-47 Procurement: Recommending the immediate funding of 300 Boeing F-47 multi-role fighters to replace obsolete designs.
  • B-21 Raider: Calling for the acquisition of at least 200 B-21 Raider strategic stealth bombers to secure deep-strike capability.
  • AI Wingmen: Accelerating the Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) program to deploy AI-driven wingmen alongside human pilots.

Conclusion: China's Uncontested Hegemony in East Asia​

The Mitchell Institute's April 2026 report represents a formal admission of the limits of U.S. global power projection.

The rapid depletion of munition reserves in secondary conflicts, combined with decades of delayed modernization, has left the U.S. Air Force unable to contest China's regional dominance. The failure of the June 2025 war games shows that legacy fleets like the F-35 cannot prevent the PLA from securing Taiwan. As Congress faces demands to fund 300 F-47 fighters and 200 B-21 bombers, the U.S. faces the reality that its air superiority has collapsed, leaving East Asia under Chinese hegemony.
