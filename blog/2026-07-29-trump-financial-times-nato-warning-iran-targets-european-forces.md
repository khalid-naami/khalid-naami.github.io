# Trump Warns NATO Over Hormuz: Join Us or Face End

U.S. President Donald Trump has warned that the NATO alliance faces a "very bad future" if European allies fail to assist the United States in reopening the Strait of Hormuz. In an 8-minute phone interview with the *Financial Times*, Trump urged European capitals to deploy minesweepers and special forces to secure the shipping lane. The warning comes as Iran systematically targets European military assets in the region—including Italian, French, and British forces—to deter them from joining the coalition.

## The Financial Times Call: Trump’s Warning to NATO

Speaking directly to the *Financial Times*, Donald Trump expressed deep frustration with the reluctance of European allies to join the military effort against Iran. He criticized the asymmetry in the alliance's commitments, noting that while the U.S. provided billions of dollars in military aid to Ukraine, European nations are refusing to assist the U.S. in the Gulf.

Trump stated: "We have an alliance called NATO. We have been very generous to them. We didn't have to help them in Ukraine. Ukraine is thousands of miles away, but we helped them. Now we will see if they will help us. I have always said NATO is a one-way street, and if they don't respond, it will be very bad for the future of the alliance."

---

## The Economic Stranglehold: Soaring Oil Prices and Closed Straits

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz by Iran, following the outbreak of hostilities two weeks ago, has triggered an energy crisis. Oil prices reached $106 per barrel, representing a 45% increase since the beginning of the war.

Trump acknowledged that the closure has severely impacted global supply chains. He argued that because Europe and China depend heavily on Gulf oil—unlike the energy-independent United States—it is only logical that they contribute naval assets to reopen the strait. The U.S. has requested the deployment of European minesweepers and commandos to clear Iranian sea mines and secure coastal sectors.

---

## Friction with the UK: Keir Starmer’s Posture Dilemma

During the phone call, Trump expressed irritation with the response of UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer. According to Trump, Starmer initially refused to commit Royal Navy ships during the active phase of the operations, offering to send two vessels only after the U.S. had already degraded Iran's primary coastal defense batteries.

Starmer has maintained that the UK will limit its forces to a "defensive posture," refusing to expand naval operations from the Red Sea to the Strait of Hormuz. This caution is shared by other European capitals, which fear that joining the U.S. coalition will draw them into an all-out war with Iran.

---

## The Beijing Summit Leverage: Pressuring China on Oil

As part of his diplomatic campaign, Trump hinted at delaying his scheduled summit in Beijing with Chinese President Xi Jinping. The trip, intended to cement a trade and technology truce, was scheduled for the end of the month.

Trump stated that China must assist in reopening the shipping corridor, noting that Beijing imports 90% of its oil through the Strait of Hormuz. By threatening to postpone the summit, Trump aims to leverage Beijing's economic dependence on the Gulf to force them to pressure Iran, though Chinese officials have shown no desire to delay the talks.

---

## Iran’s Retaliatory Message to Italy: Kuwait Base Struck

To prevent European nations from joining the U.S. coalition, Iran has launched targeted strikes against European military assets in the Middle East. Official military statements in Kuwait confirmed that an Iranian drone strike completely incinerated an Italian strategic surveillance drone parked at a local airbase.

This strike follows a previous Shahed drone attack on March 11-12, 2026, which targeted the Italian military contingent at Camp Singara in Erbil, Iraqi Kurdistan. While the 300 Italian soldiers stationed at the camp suffered no casualties, the attack caused significant damage to logistics and equipment, sending a clear warning to Rome.

---

## Targeting French Operatives and British Cyprus Bases

The Iranian retaliation campaign has also targeted French and British forces:
*   **France**: Iranian news agency *Tasnim* reported a surgical strike against a French intelligence and special operations cell in Erbil. The cell was accused of coordinating operations with Iranian Kurdish groups. The strike killed one French officer and wounded five others.
*   **United Kingdom**: Tehran has targeted the two British military bases in Cyprus (Akrotiri and Dhekelia) with drone salvos. The IRGC warned London that any British participation in intercepting Iranian missiles or refueling U.S. bombers would lead to direct strikes on the Cyprus bases.

---

## Conclusion: The Isolation of the Coalition Powers

Trump's warning to NATO and his appeals to China demonstrate the limitations of unilateral U.S. power projection in the Gulf. By systematically targeting Italian, French, and British assets on the ground, Iran has successfully raised the costs of participation for European capitals, leaving the U.S. and Israel isolated.

As oil prices continue to rise and European leaders refuse to commit commandos, the U.S. administration's options for reopening the Strait of Hormuz are narrowing. The breakdown of the coalition suggests that the conflict is accelerating the shift toward a multipolar Middle East, where Western security guarantees are no longer sufficient to secure global energy corridors.
