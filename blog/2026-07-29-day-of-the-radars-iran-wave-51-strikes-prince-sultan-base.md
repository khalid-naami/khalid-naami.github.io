# Day of the Radars: Iran Strikedown of US Air Defenses

Iran and its regional partners have launched a high-intensity, systematic campaign specifically designed to disable U.S. air defense radar arrays across the Middle East. Dubbed the "Day of the Radars" (يوم الرادارات), this strategic offensive has targeted key installations from Iraq and Jordan to Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and the UAE. The strikes aim to strip away the regional defense blanket, leaving airspace open to unintercepted precision ballistic missile attacks.

## Day of the Radars: Systematic Air Defense Suppression

The "Day of the Radars" campaign represents a shift in Iranian targeting doctrine. Rather than focusing on logistics hubs or troop centers, the IRGC has prioritized the destruction of U.S. AN/TPY-2 and Patriot radar arrays.

These radar units serve as the eyes of the regional coalition, tracking incoming launches and directing interceptors. By systematically neutralizing these sensors, the IRGC aims to blind the U.S. Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD) and Patriot networks, creating blind spots in the defensive perimeter.

---

## Wave 51: Strikes Target Prince Sultan Air Base

The most significant action of the campaign occurred during Wave 51 of the missile offensive. The strike package was directed at Prince Sultan Air Base in Al-Kharj, Saudi Arabia, which has served as a central operational node for U.S. aviation since the evacuation of Al Udeid and Al Dhafra.

According to military sources, Wave 51 succeeded in destroying two Patriot radar systems at the base. The destruction of these radar units occurred alongside earlier losses, including five refueling tankers disabled on the tarmac, severely degrading the base's operational capabilities.

---

## Ali Al Salem and U.S. Embassy Radars Struck

The radar suppression campaign extended to multiple regional installations:
*   **Kuwait**: Precision drone and missile strikes hit the military sector of Ali Al Salem Airbase, disabling the runway and neutralizing local Patriot tracking radars.
*   **Iraq**: A precision strike destroyed the air defense radar protecting the U.S. Embassy in Baghdad. When U.S. teams deployed a replacement radar unit, a second strike package was launched to destroy the new unit.
*   **Jordan**: Strikes targeted radar components at the Azraq base, which U.S. forces had utilized to coordinate interception networks after the degradation of systems in Qatar.

---

## Exhaustion of U.S. Patriot Stocks and Strategic Relocation

The high rate of radar destruction has created a logistical crisis for the Pentagon. Because advanced radar arrays are highly complex and slow to manufacture, the U.S. has exhausted its regional reserve stocks.

To sustain operations, the U.S. military has depleted its forward-deployed air defense networks in Japan and South Korea. Furthermore, planners have begun drawing directly from domestic strategic stockpiles within the U.S. homeland, shipping Patriot systems to the Gulf. However, the speed of replacement cannot keep pace with the rate of destruction, threatening to leave regional airspace undefended.

---

## The Ukrainian Drone Precedent in the GCC

The current radar suppression strategy resembles the tactics utilized in Eastern Europe, prompting direct collaboration between the coalition partners. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu requested assistance from Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky to manage the threat.

Since the fifth day of the war, 10,000 Ukrainian interceptor drones have been deployed to the GCC. These drones are used to intercept low-altitude Iranian suicide drones, trying to preserve expensive Patriot and THAAD interceptors. However, while these drones can target smaller platforms, they are bypassed by heavy ballistic and cruise missiles.

---

## Hezbollah’s Cluster Missiles: The Hard Nucleus Engages

On the northern front, Hezbollah has coordinated its operations with the IRGC’s radar suppression campaign. Over the course of 50 separate operations, the group has deployed its "hard nucleus" (القوة الصلبة) strategic weapons.

For the second time this week, Hezbollah launched heavy **cluster/submunition ballistic missiles** (صواريخ انشطارية أو عنقودية) targeting U.S. and Israeli assets. Heavy rocket salvos targeted artillery positions at Metula, Dishon, Odaisseh, and Khiam, alongside strikes hitting the 7200 logistics base south of Haifa. The deployment of cluster warheads is designed to cause widespread damage to military equipment and personnel, cementing a balance of terror.

---

## Conclusion: Stripping the Middle East Air Defense Blankets

The "Day of the Radars" campaign highlights the vulnerability of high-technology air defense networks to persistent saturation and targeting. By systematically striking radar arrays from Baghdad to Al-Kharj, the IRGC has degraded the coalition’s early warning capabilities.

As U.S. forces deplete domestic stockpiles to replace destroyed units and Hezbollah deploys cluster munitions, the conflict is transitioning into a phase of open skies. The inability of the U.S. to protect its radar assets suggests that the regional defensive shield is close to collapse, leaving the coalition's bases exposed to direct ballistic threats.
