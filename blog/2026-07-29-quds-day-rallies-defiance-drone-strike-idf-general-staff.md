# Quds Day Defiance: Leaders March, IDF HQ Targeted

Iran's annual Quds Day (يوم القدس) rallies have served as a platform for political and military defiance against the U.S.-led coalition. While U.S. and Israeli officials claimed the Iranian leadership had fled to underground bunkers following heavy bomber strikes, Tehran’s top officials walked openly in the streets among massive crowds. Simultaneously, a drone strike directly targeted the IDF General Staff Headquarters in Tel Aviv, highlighting the escalation of the conflict.

## The Quds Day Rallies: Open Defiance in Tehran

The Quds Day rallies in Tehran saw millions of Iranians take to the streets, linking their national sovereignty directly to the Palestinian cause. The massive public participation was framed by local media as a demonstration of domestic support for the government during a period of high military tension.

The key detail of the rallies was the open participation of the state's top leadership. President Masoud Pezeshkian, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, defense policy chief Ali Larijani, and the head of the Atomic Energy Organization of Iran (AEOI) walked among the crowds, demonstrating that the government's control remains intact despite strategic bombardment.

---

## Debunking the U.S. Bunkers Narrative

The open appearance of the Iranian leadership directly challenged U.S. and Israeli statements. In a joint press conference, the U.S. Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth claimed that Iranian leaders were "hiding in deep underground bunkers," asserting that coalition air strikes had dismantled the regime's command structures.

By walking openly in the streets of Tehran without extensive defensive cordons, the leaders showed that these statements were inaccurate. Hebrew media outlets acknowledged the significance of this appearance, noting that it contradicted coalition assessments regarding the impact of the air campaign.

---

## The Disconnect of the Exiled Opposition

The scale of the rallies also highlighted the political disconnect facing the exiled opposition. Reza Pahlavi, the son of the former Shah, issued public appeals urging the Iranian public to refrain from participating in the marches, claiming the government was on the verge of collapse.

However, the large crowds that responded to the state's call demonstrated that the exiled opposition's appeals had limited impact. This disconnect shows that domestic public opinion remains focused on national defense, prioritizing resistance against external military pressure over calls for internal change.

---

## Hakirya Struck: Drone Strike on the IDF General Staff

While the rallies were underway in Tehran, a direct military message was delivered in Tel Aviv. A coordinated drone strike targeted the IDF General Staff Headquarters (Hakirya) in the heart of the city.

The strike occurred while public demonstrations were taking place in Tel Aviv, creating significant disruption. The drones bypassed local short-range defenses, impacting the command complex. This strike demonstrated that the command structure of the Israeli military remains vulnerable to precision strikes, even during periods of high alert.

---

## Wave 45: Deploying the Kheibar Shekan Ballistic Missile

Following the Quds Day rallies, the IRGC launched Wave 45 of its counter-offensive. The strike relied on the **Kheibar Shekan (خيبر شكن)** ballistic missile, which has proven highly effective in penetrating air defense networks.

First deployed during the 12-day war in 2025 and utilized throughout the 2026 campaign, the Kheibar Shekan is recognized by Israeli analysts as a primary threat. Its high terminal velocity and maneuverability allow it to bypass the Arrow interceptor network, delivering high-explosive payloads onto strategic command hubs and airbase facilities.

---

## Ground Push to the Litani: IDF 98th Division Mobilized

In Lebanon, the IDF has deployed a third division—the 98th Division—to push several kilometers deeper into Lebanese territory. The objective of the ground operation is to establish physical control over the area leading to the Litani River.

Israeli commanders have stated they do not trust the Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) to enforce any disarmament agreements. By pushing toward the Litani, Israel aims to create a permanent buffer zone under its own authority, effectively bypassing the parameters of the November 2024 ceasefire agreement.

---

## Conclusion: The Reshaping of the Levantine Map

The events of Quds Day demonstrate that the conflict is reshaping the geopolitical map of the Middle East. By ignoring UN resolutions and French mediation, the U.S. and Israel seek to establish a new security architecture in the Levant, marginalizing European influence in Lebanon and Syria.

However, the IDF's ground push has met stiff resistance, turning the border campaign into a war of attrition. As long as Iran sustains its missile salvos and its leadership maintains domestic support, the coalition's attempt to establish unilateral control over the region will face significant obstacles, extending the duration of the confrontation.
