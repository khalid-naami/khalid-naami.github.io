# Patriot Diversion: Pentagon Strips Ukraine for Gulf

A security crisis is unfolding in Eastern Europe as the Pentagon redirects strategic air defense assets to the Middle East. According to a report by Ukrainian defense outlet *Defense Express*, the U.S. is diverting Patriot and APKWS missile stockpiles originally allocated for Ukraine to the Persian Gulf. This diversion, driven by the high consumption of air defense assets in the war with Iran, has left Ukraine's airspace exposed to Russian strikes. It highlights a strategic priority shift, where Gulf security has sidelined the defense of Ukraine.

## The Strategic Pivot: Stripping Ukraine's Air Shield

The redirection of air defense munitions represents a shift in U.S. military priorities. Faced with a high-intensity war of attrition against Iranian missile and drone swarms in the Persian Gulf, the Pentagon has prioritized protecting its staging bases and naval assets.

As a result, ammunition shipments intended for the Ukrainian Armed Forces (AFU) have been redirected. This shift has occurred despite previous commitments by the U.S. and its NATO allies, confirming that when faced with a multi-front conflict, Washington has prioritized its Middle Eastern assets over its commitments in Eastern Europe.

---

## The Defense Express Report: Patriot Diverted to the Gulf

*Defense Express* detailed the extent of the missile diversion, confirming that new production lots of PAC-2 and PAC-3 interceptors are being redirected:
*   **The Shipments**: Missiles manufactured by Lockheed Martin, originally scheduled for delivery to Kyiv, are being shipped directly to regional storage sites in the Gulf.
*   **Production limits**: Lockheed Martin's maximum production capacity is limited to 620 Patriot missiles annually.
*   **The Deficit**: The redirection of these missiles leaves the Ukrainian military without the interceptors required to replace those expended in active defense operations.

---

## The Financial Mechanism Crisis: The Borrel/US Route

The diversion has affected the special procurement channels established to fund Ukraine's defense:
*   **The Mechanism**: Under this arrangement, European allies utilize their own funds to purchase U.S. military hardware for Ukraine.
*   **The Volume**: Since the summer of 2025, approximately 75% of all Patriot missiles delivered to Ukraine were secured through this mechanism.
*   **The Re-routing**: As reported by the *Washington Post*, the Pentagon is considering suspending this program, redirecting the newly manufactured missiles to meet its own needs in the Gulf.

This leaves European allies with the financial burden but without the capability to secure the weapons for Kyiv.

---

## The Scale of Attrition: 1,600 Interceptors in Two Weeks

The requirement for the diversion is driven by the high rate of interceptor consumption in the Middle East. An evaluation by the Royal United Services Institute (RUSI) confirms the scale of the attrition:
*   **Expenditure**: Middle Eastern allies and U.S. commands have launched over 1,600 Patriot interceptors in a two-week period.
*   **The Target**: These high-cost missiles are deployed to counter low-cost Iranian drone swarms and ballistic missile salvos.
*   **The Disparity**: The high consumption rate has depleted regional stockpiles, forcing the Pentagon to divert European-destined shipments to prevent a complete collapse of the air defense shield in the Gulf.

---

## Beyond Patriot: Redirecting the Secondary Missile Inventory

The missile diversion extends beyond the Patriot system, affecting secondary air defense assets:
*   **APKWS Rockets**: The Advanced Precision Kill Weapon System, which is used in Ukraine on "Vampire" ground systems and F-16s to intercept Shahed drones, has had its entire supply chain redirected to the Middle East.
*   **AIM-120 and AIM-9**: Air-to-air missiles, including older AIM-9M variants, are being sent to the Gulf for use on U.S. A-10 aircraft tasked with drone interception.
*   **NASAMS**: Interceptor stocks for these medium-range systems are also being diverted, reducing the air defense coverage of Ukrainian cities.

---

## The FrankenSAM Threat: Sea Sparrow and Hawk Depletion

The diversion has also affected the munitions used in Ukraine's hybrid defense systems, known as "FrankenSAM":
*   **RIM-7 Sea Sparrow**: Critical for the modified Buk-M1 launchers used by the AFU, these missiles are being diverted to naval units in the Red Sea.
*   **MIM-23 Hawk**: Although retired from active U.S. service, Hawk batteries are used by Ukraine; their remaining stockpiles are now being redirected to the Middle East as low-cost interceptors.

The depletion of these secondary munitions leaves Ukraine without the capability to sustain its low-altitude air defense network.

---

## Europe's Defense Role: The Charles de Gaulle Deployment

While European states have avoided participating in the offensive campaign against Iran to protect their own security, they are involved in the defensive coalition:
*   **Naval Assets**: Germany, the UK, and France have deployed frigates to the Red Sea to protect shipping.
*   **The French Carrier**: The aircraft carrier *Charles de Gaulle* has been deployed to the region, utilizing its aircraft to intercept drones.

This involvement has depleted European domestic air defense stockpiles, reducing the availability of weapons that could have been transferred to Ukraine.

---

## Trump's Putin Guarantee vs. European Security Fears

President Trump has attempted to reassure European allies regarding the security risk in the East, offering a personal guarantee:
*   **The Guarantee**: Trump has urged European leaders to support the Middle East campaign, claiming that he has secured an agreement with Vladimir Putin that Russia will not launch new offensives in Ukraine.
*   **European Skepticism**: Leaders in Germany, France, and the UK do not share this trust, viewing the diversion of air defenses as a move that leaves Eastern Europe vulnerable.
*   **Russia's Neutrality**: Russia has maintained a calculated neutrality, withholding advanced systems like the S-400 from Iran to protect its economic relations with the UAE and Saudi Arabia, while benefiting from the depletion of U.S. military stockpiles.

---

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Eastern Europe

The diversion of Patriot, APKWS, and secondary interceptors from Ukraine to the Persian Gulf confirms the limits of U.S. global defense capacity.

By prioritizing the defense of its Gulf assets and allies over its commitments in Europe, the U.S. has left Ukraine's air defense network vulnerable. With Lockheed Martin's production capacity unable to meet the high consumption rate of the Gulf war, Eastern Europe faces a long-term security deficit. Trump's personal guarantees regarding Russian intentions have failed to reassure European capitals, who recognize that the depletion of their air defense shield has left their eastern border exposed.
