# Gulf Target List: Iran Threatens US Oil Assets

As the military confrontation in the Middle East intensifies, the economic consequences of the conflict are reshaping political dynamics in Washington and Tel Aviv. Iran has clarified its energy retaliation doctrine, issuing a precise target list of all oil, gas, and petrochemical facilities in the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) where U.S. corporations hold significant capital. This targeted threat has triggered a fierce lobby war in Washington, forcing a major shift in Israel's political leadership.

## The Targeted Retaliation Doctrine: Sparing Domestic Grids

Iranian defense officials have issued a clarification regarding previous statements about regional energy disruptions. While defense policy chief Ali Larijani warned of a regional blackout, Tehran clarified that it will not target the domestic electrical grids of neighboring Gulf states.

Instead, Iran's retaliation strategy will focus exclusively on energy infrastructure where U.S. multi-nationals own equity. By sparing local civil infrastructure while targeting U.S. corporate assets, Tehran aims to pressure Washington directly through its primary economic lobby, avoiding a direct confrontation with local Gulf populations.

## Mapping the GCC Target List: U.S. Energy Investments

The target list published by Iranian military planners maps out key joint-venture energy facilities in Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar. The selected targets represent billions of dollars of U.S. capital investment, operated by giants such as Chevron, ExxonMobil, and Occidental Petroleum:
*   **Saudi Arabia**: Joint-venture refineries and petrochemical complexes in Yanbu and Jubail.
*   **United Arab Emirates**: The Al Hosn gas field and refining units at Ruwais/Ras Laffan.
*   **Qatar**: Petrochemical and gas processing facilities in Mesaieed and Q-Chem.

## Saudi Arabia: Yanbu Refinery and Jubail Chevron Phillips

In Saudi Arabia, the primary targets identified by the IRGC are facilities with heavy U.S. corporate equity:
*   **Yanbu Refinery (Samref)**: A joint venture between Saudi Aramco and **ExxonMobil**, with a capacity of 400,000 barrels per day. The facility produces gasoline, jet fuel, diesel, and marine white oil.
*   **Jubail Chevron Phillips (JCP)**: Owned by **Chevron Phillips** in partnership with the Saudi Industrial Investment Group (SIIG), with Chevron holding a 35% stake, producing benzene and ethylene.
*   **Sadara Chemical Complex**: A major chemical production hub in Jubail featuring significant U.S. technology and capital participation.

## UAE and Qatar: Al Hosn Gas Field and Q-Chem Projects

In the UAE and Qatar, the target list focuses on crucial gas and petrochemical hubs:
*   **Al Hosn Gas Field (UAE)**: A joint venture where U.S. corporation **Occidental Petroleum (Oxy)** holds a 40% stake, with ADNOC owning the remaining 60%.
*   **Ruwais/Ras Laffan Refinery Phases 1 & 2**: Featuring significant refining and processing assets backed by U.S. capital.
*   **Q-Chem and Mesaieed Holding (Qatar)**: Major petrochemical complexes where U.S. energy giant **Chevron** holds substantial equity shares.

## The Washington Lobby War: Energy Giants vs. AIPAC

The targeting of U.S. assets in the Gulf has triggered a major political clash in Washington. U.S. energy corporations are furious that Trump's "America First" policy has prioritized Israeli strategic objectives over vital U.S. energy infrastructure in the Middle East.

Historically, U.S. foreign policy balanced the interests of the oil lobby and the pro-Israel lobby (AIPAC). However, Trump's current campaign has marginalized the oil giants—partly because Chevron and others refused to invest $100 billion in Venezuela's heavy crude sector, choosing instead to invest in Eastern Mediterranean gas fields linking Israel, Gaza, and Egypt. Now, facing catastrophic losses in the Gulf, the oil lobby is utilizing its financial influence to pressure the Republican leadership.

## Netanyahu’s Exit Strategy: Herzog’s Resignation Amnesty Deal

The pressure from U.S. corporate lobbies has forced a significant political shift in Tel Aviv. The threat of U.S. energy giants cutting off campaign funding for the Republican party has forced Donald Trump to demand a rapid conclusion to Israeli military operations.

To facilitate an exit, Israeli President Isaac Herzog (هرتسوغ) has brokered a compromise with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Under the terms of the deal, Netanyahu will agree to resign from office immediately following the conclusion of the military campaign against Iran. In exchange, he will receive a full presidential amnesty and pardon, shielding him from ongoing corruption trials.
