# Khorramshahr-4 Strike Vaporizes Rafael Strategic Depots

A heavy Khorramshahr-4 ballistic missile strike has destroyed one of Israel's primary strategic weapons reserves in the north. Developed and managed by state-owned Rafael Advanced Defense Systems, the targeted storage complexes in Shlomi, Haifa Bay, and the Krayot area housed major reserves of anti-tank guided missiles and air defense interceptors. The strike, occurring immediately after the completion of a U.S. military munitions airlift, has forced the IDF to delay its planned ground offensive in southern Lebanon.

## The Khorramshahr-4 Strike: Heavy Ballistic Shock in the North

During the latest round of ballistic missile strikes, the IRGC deployed the heavy Khorramshahr-4 ballistic missile against targets in northern Israel. The Khorramshahr-4 is designed to carry a warhead weighing over two tonnes (2,000 kilograms), specializing in deep penetration of hardened underground structures.

Previously, Hezbollah had targeted Rafael's northern production lines using tactical Fadi-1 and Fadi-2 rocket salvos. However, the deployment of the Khorramshahr-4 represents a shift from localized harassment to the destruction of Israel's long-term strategic reserves.

---

## Targeting Rafael’s Strategic Reserves in Haifa Bay and Shlomi

The target of the heavy ballistic missile was the strategic storage network of Rafael Advanced Defense Systems. These depots are located in Shlomi, the Krayot district, and the industrial zone of Haifa Bay.

Unlike tactical forward depots that hold active field ammunition, these complexes store Israel's strategic reserves. These reserves are intended to sustain the IDF during a prolonged multi-front conflict, and their destruction limits the defense sector's ability to supply frontline units.

---

## Destroying the Spike Anti-Tank Munitions Reserve

A significant loss from the strike was the destruction of a major reserve of Spike anti-tank guided missiles (ATGMs). The Spike family of missiles is manufactured by Rafael and serves as a core component of the IDF's tactical land combat systems.

These missiles were prepared to support the infantry and armored units assigned to the ground offensive in southern Lebanon. The destruction of this stockpile directly affects the IDF's land combat capability, reducing the anti-armor support available to units operating against fortified positions.

---

## Compromising Assembly Lines for Arrow-4 Development

The Khorramshahr-4 strike also caused damage to Rafael's manufacturing and assembly complexes. The blast affected the assembly areas dedicated to the development of the next-generation Arrow-4 (Hetz-4) air defense system.

Following the lessons of the earlier 12-day war, Rafael had prioritized the development of the Arrow-4 to intercept high-velocity, maneuvering hypersonic targets. The destruction of these assembly halls disrupts the development timeline of this interceptor, leaving the air defense network dependent on older Arrow-3 models.

---

## The U.S. Munitions Airlift: Intercepting the Delivery

Intelligence reports suggest that the timing of the strike was coordinated with U.S. logistics. A U.S. military airlift had completed the transfer of precision-guided munitions and interceptor components to the northern depots.

Iranian intelligence tracked the transit of these U.S. supplies. By waiting until the airlifted munitions were stored in the Rafael depots before launching the Khorramshahr-4, the IRGC ensured that the fresh shipments were destroyed alongside existing strategic reserves.

---

## Ground Offensive Delays: Defensive Chaos in Southern Lebanon

The destruction of the anti-tank and air defense stocks has forced the IDF's Northern Command to delay its planned ground operations in Lebanon. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz announced that the ground offensive would target the Khiam sector.

However, the loss of tactical anti-tank munitions has led to repeated postponements. The first delay of 100 hours has been extended, showing the impact of the loss of Rafael's strategic reserves on the IDF's offensive planning.

---

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Key Strategic Depots

The Khorramshahr-4 strike on Rafael's strategic depots highlights the difficulty of protecting high-value industrial hubs from heavy ballistic missiles. By targeting the complexes in Haifa Bay and Shlomi immediately after the U.S. airlift, the IRGC has degraded both Israel's domestic production and its supply chains.

As the IDF manages the logistical shortages and evaluates the damage to the Arrow-4 assembly lines, the limits of the strategic reserves are clear. The vulnerability of these concentrated storage depots suggests that future operations will face supply challenges, limiting the ability to execute high-intensity ground offensives without continuous resupply.
