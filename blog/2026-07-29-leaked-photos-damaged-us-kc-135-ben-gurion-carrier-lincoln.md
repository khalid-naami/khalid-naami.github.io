# Leaked: Damaged US Tanker Lands at Ben Gurion

A series of leaks from Israeli media outlets has exposed the scale of U.S. and Israeli air losses during recent operations in western Iraq. High-resolution photographs showing a damaged U.S. KC-135 Stratotanker at Ben Gurion Airport have contradicted official Pentagon accounts of the engagement. This development, combined with satellite tracking showing evasive maneuvers by the carrier USS *Abraham Lincoln*, has forced the coalition to plan a massive, preemptive air campaign to suppress regional launch sites.

## The Leaked Images: Confirming the Second KC-135 Hit

Although the U.S. Defense Department and CENTCOM officially acknowledged the loss of only one KC-135 Stratotanker—claiming it crashed due to a mechanical issue in a non-hostile zone—leaked images from Ben Gurion Airport tell a different story.

The leaked photographs show the second KC-135, which managed to land in Israel, displaying extensive shrapnel damage. The tail section of the tanker was severely compromised, confirming that the aircraft was hit by fragments from a close-proximity missile explosion. The visual reports confirm that both refueling aircraft were targeted and hit during the engagement over western Iraq.

## Tail Damage and Casualty Figures at Ben Gurion

The damage to the tail section of the second KC-135 has permanently disabled the aircraft, removing it from the active inventory. The severity of the shrapnel damage also explains why the pilots activated the international emergency code 7700 during their return flight.

The engagement has resulted in significant U.S. casualties. In addition to the four crew members confirmed dead on the first aircraft (with two others missing and presumed dead), several crew members on the second tanker suffered serious injuries from shrapnel and decompression. At least one Israeli liaison officer was on board the damaged aircraft, coordinating the emergency transit.

## USS Abraham Lincoln: Course Deviation and Coordinates Spoofing

The U.S. Navy has faced additional challenges with the carrier USS *Abraham Lincoln* (CVN-72). While the Pentagon claimed the Lincoln was operating safely at distance, civilian satellite tracking systems detected a major course correction.

The carrier executed a sharp 256-degree turn, indicating it was executing emergency evasive maneuvers following a threat detection. To mask the carrier's actual position, military planners spoofed coordinates, showing the vessel in Bangladesh. In reality, the carrier is operating at a reduced speed of 20.3 knots, attempting to approach within 250 kilometers of the Gulf under heavy defensive cover.

To regain the operational initiative and secure the Strait of Hormuz, the U.S. and Israel have prepared a massive night strike campaign. The goal of the operation is to \"freeze\" regional launch sites before they can execute counter-salvos.

The U.S. has mobilized 15 strategic heavy bombers—including B-52, B-1B, and B-2 Spirits—from RAF Fairford in the United Kingdom. These bombers will coordinate with over 180 combat aircraft launched from Israeli bases and U.S. naval assets, including carrier support from the USS *Abraham Lincoln* moving via Crete, to deliver a high volume of ordnance.

## Suppressing the Launch Sites: The Air Campaign in Lebanon

The preemptive air campaign is designed to suppress launch networks in both Iran and Lebanon. To prevent Hezbollah from launching retaliatory strikes while Iran is being targeted, the coalition has intensified its air strikes in Lebanon, covering waves 40 through 44.

The coalition's ground push into southern Lebanon seeks to force Hezbollah's launch teams back from the border, while the massive air strikes aim to suppress their ability to launch long-range missiles into central Israel. This coordinated pressure is designed to create a temporary fire window for the coalition.

The current air campaign in Lebanon is operated under a unified U.S.-Israeli joint command center. U.S. planners are directly involved in targeting and flight coordination, integrating U.S. surveillance assets with Israeli strike wings.

## Conclusion: The Race to Prevent Regional Counter-Salvos

The leaks showing the damaged KC-135 at Ben Gurion Airport and the course correction of the USS *Abraham Lincoln* demonstrate the vulnerabilities of coalition assets. By targeting refueling tankers and carriers, regional forces have degraded the logistics of the air campaign.

As the coalition prepares its 15-bomber strike fleet, the success of the campaign will depend on whether it can neutralize the launch sites before counter-salvos are initiated. The failure of previous strikes to prevent launches suggests that the preemptive campaign carries a high risk of retaliation, shaping the final phases of the confrontation.
