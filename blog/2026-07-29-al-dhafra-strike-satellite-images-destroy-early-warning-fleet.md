# Al Dhafra Strike: Satellite Shows Fleet Destroyed

Satellite imagery obtained after the recent Iranian strike packages has confirmed devastating damage to Al Dhafra Airbase in the United Arab Emirates. Analyzed by the Ukrainian defense portal *Defense Express*, the images show the destruction of seven highly fortified aircraft hangars. These hangars housed the U.S. and Emirati aerial surveillance, reconnaissance, and early warning fleets, representing one of the costliest material losses of the air campaign.

## The Origin of the Strike: Retaliation for the HIMARS Attacks

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi confirmed that the strike on Al Dhafra was a direct response to hostile operations launched from Emirati soil. According to satellite tracking, U.S. forces utilized HIMARS (High Mobility Artillery Rocket System) batteries positioned in Ras Al Khaimah, near Dubai, to strike Iranian oil infrastructure on Kharg Island (جزيرة خارك).

In response, the IRGC deployed its Wave 50 strike package, launching precision cruise and ballistic missiles against Al Dhafra Airbase, which hosts the reconnaissance assets that coordinate regional missile targeting.

---

## Satellite Evidence: Analyzing the Hangar Fires at Al Dhafra

Unlike previous engagements where missiles impacted empty runways or open ground, the satellite images analyzed by *Defense Express* show clear patterns of internal combustion within seven aircraft hangars.

The structures display characteristic roof collapses and internal burn marks, confirming that the assets parked inside were destroyed by the impacts. Western intelligence assessments estimate that between 30% and 70% of the active reconnaissance fleet was parked in these hangars during the strike, a detail confirmed by automated warning systems transmitting alerts back to the aircraft manufacturers.

---

## Saab GlobalEye: Destructive Blow to Emirati Early Warning

The most significant loss identified in the satellite images is the destruction of three hangars housing Saab GlobalEye Airborne Early Warning and Control (AEW&C) aircraft. These multi-mission platforms are operated by the UAE Air Force to coordinate regional defense networks.

The UAE purchased five GlobalEye units under a series of contracts totaling $2.3 billion, valuing each aircraft at approximately $460 million. The destruction of three of these platforms represents a loss of **$1.38 billion** for the UAE Air Force, neutralizing its early warning capability and exposing regional airspace to further penetration.

---

## Neutralizing U.S. Navy Triton and Air Force Reaper Assets

The remaining four destroyed hangars housed U.S. reconnaissance and combat drones that operate persistently over Iranian territory:
*   **MQ-4C Triton**: Two hangars housing these high-altitude, long-endurance maritime surveillance drones were destroyed. At a contract cost of $133 million per unit, the loss is valued at **$266 million**.
*   **MQ-9 Reaper**: Two hangars housing these strike and reconnaissance platforms were hit. At $40 million per unit, the loss of these two assets is valued at **$80 million**.

These unmanned platforms were actively used to coordinate strikes against ballistic launch pads in Iran, and their destruction limits the coalition's tactical intelligence gathering.

---

## Tactical Context: Disproving Official Air Defense Claims

The level of destruction on the ground at Al Dhafra contradicts the official narrative promoted by UAE defense officials. UAE media reports claimed that out of 1,475 drones launched by Iran, only 90 reached their targets, and only two out of 262 ballistic missiles penetrated local airspace.

However, the destruction of seven highly fortified hangar complexes proves that the air defense networks—including U.S. Patriot batteries—failed to protect the base's most valuable assets. Ukrainian defense commentators have used the incident to emphasize the need for specialized interceptor drones, of which 10,000 have been sold to the Gulf, though these systems remain ineffective against ballistic warheads.

---

## The Financial and Operational Toll: Billions in Losses

When calculating the value of the destroyed aircraft alone—without accounting for ground support equipment, advanced spare parts, weapons storage, and the hangars themselves—the financial loss exceeds **$1.726 billion**.

If the costs of infrastructure repair and the replacement of specialized diagnostic electronics are included, the total bill for the Al Dhafra strike rises into the billions of dollars. This represents a significant economic burden for the coalition, outstripping the financial cost of the Iranian missiles and drones used to execute the strike.

---

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Gulf Reconnaissance Fleets

The Al Dhafra strike demonstrates that regional airbases can no longer guarantee the safety of high-value reconnaissance assets. By targeting the hangars that house Saab GlobalEye, Triton, and Reaper units, the IRGC has degraded the early warning and intelligence networks that sustain U.S. operations.

As the U.S. and UAE evaluate the satellite imagery, the operational capabilities of the coalition in the Gulf have been restricted. The systematic destruction of these platforms suggests that the coalition's ability to monitor launch sites and coordinate interceptors has been compromised, changing the dynamics of the air campaign.
