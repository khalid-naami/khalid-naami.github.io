# Blown Away: IAF Chief Admits War Has Shifted to Israel

The military confrontation in the Middle East has reached a critical juncture, characterized by a fundamental shift in the operational theater. In a rare public acknowledgment, the Commander of the Israeli Air Force (IAF) admitted that the focus of active combat has moved directly into Israel itself. This development represents a significant strategic success for Iran, challenging the core assumptions of the U.S.-led coalition's campaign.

## Major General Tomer Bar's Admission: The Shift of Fire

In a public address to military personnel, IAF Commander Major General Tomer Bar stated: "I want the war to be fought over there, not here. I want there to be fire over there, and for this country to continue working."

This statement marks the first time a senior Israeli commander has admitted that the IDF has failed to keep the conflict outside the country's borders. Bar acknowledged that the primary challenge for the IAF is to deprive Iran of its long-range missile launch capabilities, admitting that the current level of incoming fire presents an existential challenge to Israel's domestic security.

---

## The "95% Destruction" Dilemma and Trump’s Deadline

The Israeli military command recently briefed the political leadership, claiming they have already neutralized 80% of Iran's missile launch infrastructure. They asserted that with an additional three to four days of operations, they could reach 95% destruction. This presentation occurred as U.S. President Donald Trump demanded a clear deadline to conclude operations.

However, independent defense analysts point out a major discrepancy. If only 20% of Iran's capabilities remain, it should not have been possible for the IRGC to launch the "Khorramshahr-4 Night" offensive, which sustained heavy missile barrages for three hours. Furthermore, analysts note that even if Israel achieves 95% destruction, the remaining 5% of Iran's arsenal is sufficient to maintain active launch networks.

---

## Tonnage Comparison: Enduring a 300% Increase in Munitions

The scale of the bombardment endured by Iran has exceeded all historical benchmarks. Over the past 11 days of conflict, U.S. and Israeli forces have dropped over 1,000 heavy, bunker-busting bombs per day on Iranian territory.

The total tonnage of munitions dropped during this campaign is equivalent to 300% (three times) of the total ordnance used during the entire 12-day war in 2025. Despite this high volume of fire, Iran's missile launch rate on day 11 exceeded its day 1 rate. This demonstrates the survival of its underground "missile cities," which are designed to withstand heavy strategic bombardment.

---

## The 20-Country Fire Zone: Breaking Coalition Logistics

The core factor that disrupted the U.S.-Israeli operational plan was Iran's decision to expand the combat theater to a 20-country fire zone. The original coalition plan assumed that logistics nodes, staging airfields, and refueling zones in the Gulf and neighboring states would remain safe.

By launching targeted missile and drone strikes against coalition assets across 20 countries, Iran shattered this assumption. U.S. and Israeli aircraft can no longer operate with the expectation of safe return to regional bases, forcing the coalition to alter its flight paths and logistics chains.

---

## The New York Times Report: 17 U.S. Bases Damaged

The expansion of the fire zone has resulted in significant losses for U.S. forces stationed in the region. A report published by the *New York Times* confirmed that 17 U.S. military bases and logistics facilities in the Middle East have suffered severe damage.

The Hebrew press has begun translating and republishing these reports for the Israeli public. By showing the losses suffered by their primary ally, the media aims to contextualize the damage to Israel's own infrastructure, preparing the public for the reality of a protracted conflict.

---

## The Failures of the Special Infiltration Operations

The strategic pressure on the Israeli command has been intensified by the failure of several high-risk ground operations. The most significant of these was the foiled commando mission in Nabi Chit, which aimed to extract enriched uranium.

The failure of the 15-helicopter landing operation, combined with the loss of one aircraft, has limited the IDF's options for deep operations. Without the ability to execute surprise command raids, the military has had to rely on continued air strikes, which have failed to neutralize the launch networks.

---

## Conclusion: The Unprecedented Resilience of Iran's Missile Network

The admission by Major General Tomer Bar that the war has shifted into Israel highlights the limits of U.S. and Israeli air superiority. Despite dropping three times the tonnage of the previous war, the coalition has been unable to silence the launch sites.

As the U.S. administration pushes for a timeline to end the conflict, the reality of the 20-country fire zone and the destruction of U.S. regional bases will shape the diplomatic negotiations. The survival of Iran's precision launch networks suggests that any future settlement must address the new balance of power in the region.
