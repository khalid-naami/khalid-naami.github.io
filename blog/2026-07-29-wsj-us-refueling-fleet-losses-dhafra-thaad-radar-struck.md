# WSJ: US Refueling Fleet Struck, THAAD Targeted

Reports published by the *Wall Street Journal* have outlined significant aerial losses and rising casualties among U.S. forces in the Persian Gulf. In addition to losing refueling tankers over Iraq, a precision strike on Prince Sultan Air Base has disabled several support aircraft. Concurrently, the IRGC has targeted newly deployed U.S. THAAD air defense batteries, including a unit recently transferred from South Korea to Al Dhafra Airbase in the United Arab Emirates.

## Refueling Fleet Losses: Prince Sultan Air Base Struck

The *Wall Street Journal* confirmed that the U.S. aerial refueling fleet has suffered major losses during recent operations. In addition to the two KC-135 Stratotankers struck over western Iraq, a coordinated Iranian missile strike targeted Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia.

The strike hit parked support aircraft on the tarmac, destroying five refueling tankers. This brings the total loss to seven refueling aircraft disabled or destroyed within a 48-hour window. The reduction in available tankers directly limits the operational range and frequency of U.S. and coalition bombing runs.

---

## U.S. Casualty Toll: WSJ Reports Rising Numbers

The *Wall Street Journal* also disclosed the latest casualty figures among U.S. personnel deployed in the region. According to military sources, 13 U.S. soldiers have been killed and 200 wounded during the recent missile salvos.

Of the wounded, 10 remain in critical condition, and at least 20 have suffered permanent, life-altering disabilities that prevent them from returning to active duty. This brings the total number of soldiers permanently removed from service (killed or disabled) to 43. While the Pentagon has attempted to manage public reaction, medical evacuations have been documented, including 19 wounded soldiers flown from Saudi Arabia to Landstuhl Regional Medical Center in Germany.

## Targeting THAAD: Strikedown of the Al Dhafra Battery

A primary objective of Iran's missile campaign is the systematic neutralization of the U.S. Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD) network. Following the destruction of THAAD installations in the Negev and Jordan, the U.S. military transferred a THAAD battery from South Korea to Al Dhafra Airbase in the UAE.

This South Korean battery, intended to replace previously degraded assets, was targeted by the IRGC hours after becoming operational. Precision missiles struck the radar and launch components at Al Dhafra, neutralizing the system. The destruction of this unit means the U.S. has exhausted its regional reserve units, forcing planners to consider drawing from domestic stockpiles.

---

## Tactics of THAAD Neutralization: Bypassing Radar Arrays

To overcome the advanced interception capabilities of the THAAD system, Iranian planners have utilized coordinated strike packages. The tactic involves launching waves of low-cost cruise missiles and decoy drones first.

These initial salvos force the THAAD batteries to exhaust their interceptor missiles. Once the battery's magazine is empty and the radar system is saturated, the IRGC deploys precision ballistic missiles to strike the radar arrays directly. By targeting the AN/TPY-2 radar, the IRGC disables the entire battery's tracking capacity.

---

## Ukrainian Interceptor Drones in the GCC

To defend Gulf airspace against the high volume of Iranian drones, the U.S. and GCC states have deployed 10,000 Ukrainian interceptor drones. These unmanned systems, developed during the conflict in Eastern Europe, are optimized to intercept low-flying suicide drones.

While these interceptor drones have achieved some success against smaller, slower targets, they have proven ineffective against heavy ballistic and cruise missiles. The integration of these drones has added a layer of tactical complexity to the regional defense network, but has failed to prevent precision strikes on key bases.

## The Israel-Lebanon Front: Evacuation of Ramat David

On the northern front, military operations have intensified. Due to continuous rocket and missile fire from Hezbollah and Iran, the Israeli Air Force has evacuated key assets and logistics from Ramat David Airbase (قاعدة رمات ديفيد الجوية) in northern Israel.

Israel is preparing to apply its "Gaza military doctrine" to southern Lebanon, anticipating a ground campaign up to the Litani River. However, this push faces a structured defense. Hebrew military analyst Zamir (زامير) confirmed that Hezbollah's launch of 150 rockets successfully targeted and destroyed two high-value strategic objectives within Israel's core military infrastructure bank.

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Regional Air Defense Architectures

The loss of seven refueling tankers and the destruction of the THAAD battery at Al Dhafra highlight the limitations of current air defense models. The coalition's reliance on high-cost interceptors makes it vulnerable to saturation tactics, allowing precision missiles to penetrate key installations.

As U.S. forces transfer assets from East Asia and face rising casualties, the strategic balance in the Gulf is shifting. The ability of regional forces to systematically disable air defense networks suggests that securing commercial and military infrastructure in the Gulf will require a reassessment of deployment strategies, with significant implications for global logistics.
