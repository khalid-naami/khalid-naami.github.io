# Blinded: Intel Failure and Israel Subway Cyberattack

A coordinated military and cyber offensive has exposed critical vulnerabilities within Israel's defense and civil infrastructure. Israeli military intelligence (Aman) failed to anticipate a massive joint salvo consisting of 150 missiles and 30 drones launched from Lebanon. Simultaneously, a sophisticated cyberattack hijacked the digital infrastructure of Israel Railways, broadcasting emergency evacuation orders across the Tel Aviv subway network and causing widespread domestic panic.

## The Intelligence Failure: Blind to the 200-Salvo Strike

The launch of approximately 200 missiles and drones from southern Lebanon occurred without any early warning from Israeli intelligence agencies. The failure of Aman and other tracking networks to detect the coordination between the IRGC and Hezbollah has raised serious concerns within the Israeli defense establishment.

The lack of tactical warning prevented the deployment of preemptive air strikes, allowing Hezbollah's launch teams to operate without disruption. As a result, heavy salvos reached targets across northern and central Israel, forcing over two million civilians into bomb shelters for more than three hours.

---

## Shlomi Binder and the Aman Source Protection Dispute

To explain the failure, a media conflict has broken out within the IDF. Reports published by Avi Ashkenazi in *Maariv* suggest that Aman Director Major General Shlomi Binder was aware of the impending attack but blocked preemptive strikes and public warnings.

According to this narrative, Binder sought to protect a high-value intelligence asset inside Hezbollah's command structure. Critics argue that prioritizing source protection over the safety of the civilian population was a critical error, while defenders of the intelligence chief claim that the leaks are part of an internal campaign to shift blame for the systemic tracking failure.

---

## Internal Blame: The Northern Command vs. Military Intelligence

The fallout from the strike has triggered public arguments between different branches of the IDF. Officers from the Northern Command and the Home Front Command have criticized Aman's decision to restrict the flow of tactical warnings.

In an attempt to manage public reaction, the military dispatched Brigadier General Efie Defrin, the IDF spokesperson, to address the media. However, critics accused the spokesperson of delivering "half-truths" that did not address why warnings were withheld. This has further eroded public confidence in the military's communication protocols during active escalations.

---

## The Subway Cyberattack: Hijacking Israel's Transit Signage

Coinciding with the missile strikes, a cyber offensive targeted Israel's transportation infrastructure. Hijackers breached the control networks of Israel Railways (سكك حديد إسرائيل), gaining access to the digital signage and passenger information boards in multiple subway and train stations.

The hijacked screens began displaying warning messages in Hebrew: "Iranian missile alert activated in the subway. You must evacuate the subway stations carefully and proceed to bomb shelters immediately. The subway is not safe at this time."

---

## The Widespread Panic: \"The Subway is Not Safe\"

The combination of sirens at the surface and evacuation warnings on the underground screens created significant panic. Commuters scrambled to exit the stations, believing that the subway systems were active targets for incoming ballistic missiles.

Although Israel Railways issued statements clarifying that the breach only affected the external passenger information displays and did not compromise the core signaling or safety systems of the trains, the incident demonstrated the psychological impact of coordinated cyber operations. By targeting transit hubs, the hackers caused widespread disruption without physical destruction.

---

## The U.S. Response: Trump’s Sleeper Cell Claims

The failure of both U.S. and Israeli intelligence to detect the coordinated strike has caused concern in Washington. In an effort to reassure the public and restore the image of intelligence dominance, U.S. President Donald Trump made a public address.

Trump claimed that U.S. intelligence agencies have complete visibility inside Iran, stating they know "the locations of all sleeper cells and command nodes." However, analysts indicate that these statements were primarily intended to cover up the failure to detect the joint Lebanese-Iranian launch.

---

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Connected Defense Systems

The events of the latest escalation demonstrate the challenges of defending against coordinated physical and cyber operations. The failure to detect the missile launches, combined with the hacking of civilian transit displays, shows the difficulty of securing connected networks.

As the IDF command continues to debate the decisions of its intelligence directors, the coordination between cyber units and rocket teams represents a new challenge for regional air defenses. Without reliable early warning and secure civilian infrastructure, the impact of future salvos will remain difficult to manage, altering the tactical requirements of the conflict.
