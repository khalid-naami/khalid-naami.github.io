# Double Strategic Surprise: Jordan & Oman hit

Iran's military has executed a double strategic surprise, launching deep precision strikes on Jordan's Prince Hassan Air Base (H-5) and Oman's Port of Duqm. The strikes destroyed hangars housing U.S. MQ-9 Reaper drones in northern Jordan and targeted the fuel docks supporting U.S. carrier strike groups in the Arabian Sea. The operations utilized five advanced Iranian missile systems, including the Qader, Emad, and Kheibar Shekan.

## ## Strategic Surprise: Dual Assault Targets Prince Hassan Base and Duqm Port

The regular military and the IRGC coordinated a deep strike targeting U.S. logistic corridors:
*   **Dual Targets**: Strikes hit Prince Hassan Air Base in Jordan and the Port of Duqm in Oman.
*   **Strategic Scope**: The targets represent the northern and southern pillars of the U.S. regional corridor.
*   **Precision Hits**: Both strikes achieved direct hits on logistics and command targets.

---

## Prince Hassan Air Base: Destroying MQ-9 Reaper Hangars at H-5

The strike on northern Jordan targeted the main drone surveillance hub in the Levant:
*   **Prince Hassan (H-5)**: The base is located in Safawi, near the Iraqi and Syrian borders.
*   **MQ-9 Reapers**: The strike destroyed hangars housing U.S. MQ-9 Reaper reconnaissance drones.
*   **Aircraft Destroyed**: At least four MQ-9 Reaper drones were destroyed on the tarmac.

---

## Duqm Port Strike: Cutting the Fuel Supply of U.S. Carriers

The second strike targeted the primary logistical node for U.S. naval assets in the Arabian Sea:
*   **Duqm Port**: The Omani port hosts deep-water berths for giant naval vessels.
*   **Carrier Refueling**: The strike hit the fuel depot used by the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier group.
*   **Operational Hold**: Disabling the depot restricts refueling schedules for carrier-based wings.

## Missile Deployment: The Five Systems Used in the Deep Strikes

Iranian forces utilized five distinct missile models to penetrate defensive networks:
*   **Combined Salvo**: The strikes integrated solid and liquid-fuel systems to saturate radars.
*   **System Diversification**: Planners selected specific models for range and payload requirements.
*   **Tactical Sync**: Launches from multiple sites were timed to reach their targets simultaneously.

---

## Heavy Ballistics: The Qader Missile Targets Jordan's Safawi Base

The strike on Jordan's H-5 base utilized Iran's heavy long-range ballistic missile:
*   **Qader Missile**: A heavy ballistic missile with a range approaching 1,950 km.
*   **Heavy Payload**: The missile weighs 15 to 17.5 tons, carrying a high-explosive warhead.
*   **Target Hit**: The Qader successfully hit the central command block of the base.

---

## Precision Systems: The Emad Saturation launches from Underground Cities

Liquid-fueled precision missiles were launched to saturate regional air defense batteries:
*   **Emad Missile**: A precision-guided intermediate-range missile with a 1,700 km range.
*   **Underground Cities**: The missiles were launched from underground launch cities in western Iran.
*   **Decoy Shield**: The Emad warhead uses active guidance to adjust target coordinates during descent.

---

## Maneuverable Reentry: The Kheibar Shekan Evades Air Defense Arrays

The high-speed Kheibar Shekan missile was deployed to bypass advanced interceptor shields:
*   **Kheibar Shekan**: A solid-fuel missile with a range of 1,450 km.
*   **MaRV Warhead**: The maneuverable reentry vehicle flies at 5,000 km/h (Mach 4+) during the final stage.
*   **Evasion Success**: The warhead executed terminal maneuvers to evade Patriot interceptors.

---

## Port Targeting: The Fateh-110 and Zolfaghar Strikes on Duqm Docks

Short-range precision systems were deployed against the Omani port facilities:
*   **Fateh-110**: A solid-fuel precision missile used to target the fuel loading docks.
*   **Zolfaghar**: A road-mobile solid-fuel missile with a 700 km range.
*   **Dual Launchers**: The missiles were launched from mobile dual-launcher trailers.

## Conclusion: The Vulnerability of Alternative Transport Corridors

The double strategic surprise strikes on Prince Hassan Air Base in Jordan and the Port of Duqm in Oman show the vulnerability of alternative transport corridors.

By destroying MQ-9 Reaper hangars at H-5 and targeting the fuel depot at Duqm with Qader, Emad, and Kheibar Shekan missiles, Iran has demonstrated its ability to disrupt U.S. logistics. The destruction of Carrier refueling networks and the bypass of defensive radars confirm that alternative corridors face high retaliatory risks. As U.S. carrier groups adjust their refueling schedules, these developments show that deep staging bases are no longer immune to precision strikes.
