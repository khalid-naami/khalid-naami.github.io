# Stealth Compromised: Chinese AI Tracks B-2 Bombers

A report published by a technology enterprise affiliated with the Chinese military has revealed critical vulnerabilities in the U.S. B-2 Spirit stealth bomber fleet. According to the PLA-linked Jinjiang Technology, an AI-driven signals monitoring network successfully detected, tracked, and reconstructed the flight profiles of four B-2 bombers during a strike mission over Iran. The disclosure suggests that U.S. physical stealth advantages are being compromised by advanced electronic tracking and algorithmic analysis.

## The Trigger: Bypassing the Chinese HQ-9B Air Defense Network

The air campaign against Iran, named "Operation Epic Wrath," was launched after U.S. and Israeli planners verified they could bypass Iran's primary air defense system. Iran had deployed the Chinese-manufactured HQ-9B (H-Q-9B) surface-to-air missile system, an advanced design derived from Russia's S-300 and S-400 series.

The ability to jam and bypass the HQ-9B was the primary technical trigger that allowed the U.S. to approve B-2 stealth bomber strikes. However, while the U.S. bypassed the physical radar net on the ground, China responded by exposing the vulnerability of the bombers through indirect digital tracking.

---

## Jinjiang Technology’s Bombshell: Tracking B-2 Radio Signatures

The disclosure was made by Jinjiang Technology, a state-owned defense contractor based in Hangzhou that operates under the direction of the People's Liberation Army (PLA). The company revealed that its signals intelligence network has bypassed the B-2's radar-absorbent design.

While the B-2's physical shape minimizes direct radar cross-section reflection, the aircraft still generates radio emissions, telemetry data, and satellite communication links. Jinjiang Technology successfully intercepted these minor radio frequency (RF) emissions, utilizing them as a digital signature to track the bombers.

---

## The Jingqi AI System: Reconstructing the March 1 Mission Profile

The tracking was executed by a proprietary, PLA-funded artificial intelligence platform named "Jingqi" (جينغ تشي). During a strike mission on March 1, 2026, the Jingqi AI intercepted the communications of a formation of four B-2 Spirit bombers operating under the callsigns PETRO 41, PETRO 42, PETRO 43, and PETRO 44.

By processing the raw RF emissions and combining them with open-source commercial flight logs and civil weather satellite data, the AI reconstructed the entire mission profile. The system mapped the bombers' flight path from their takeoff staging grounds, their entry into Iranian airspace, and their return routes to base.

---

## Pre-Flight Detection: Spotting the Attack Weeks in Advance

Crucially, the Jinjiang Technology report reveals that the Jingqi AI detected preparations for the strike weeks before B-2 wheels left the tarmac. The system identified anomalies in U.S. communication channels in January and February 2026.

These early indicators were generated by pre-flight logistics coordination and ground-based radio checks. By tracing the unique data patterns while the bombers were still on the ground, the AI predicted the timing and scale of the March 1 strike, eliminating the element of operational surprise.

---

## Algorithmic Warfare: The Shift from Physical Stealth to AI Codes

The success of the Jingqi AI highlights a transition in modern warfare. The battlefield is shifting from physical stealth (shape, paint, and radar evasion) to algorithmic and AI-driven confrontation (صراع الخوارزميات والذكاء الاصطناعي).

While B-2 bombers remain difficult to target immediately with terminal fire-control radar in real-time, the ability of Chinese AI to extract high-quality intelligence out of data noise (ضوضاء البيانات) eliminates the element of surprise. It allows adversaries to study U.S. combat doctrines, map operational patterns, and prepare defensive grids.

---

## Geopolitical Fallout: Undermining U.S. Deterrence Over Taiwan

The exposure of the B-2's operational data has strategic implications for U.S. deterrence in the Indo-Pacific. The B-2 Spirit, B-1B Lancer, and B-52 fleets are key assets in U.S. plans to defend Taiwan from a Chinese amphibious invasion.

By demonstrating that its AI systems can track B-2 formations and predict their missions before takeoff, Beijing has signaled that U.S. stealth assets are no longer invisible. Having previously countered U.S. carriers via hypersonic anti-ship missiles, China has now degraded U.S. heavy bomber deterrence, changing the strategic calculations for the Western Pacific.

---

## Conclusion: The Era of Algorithmic Transparency

The Jinjiang Technology report on the tracking of B-2 bombers shows the limits of physical stealth in an era of AI-driven signals intelligence. The ability to reconstruct the mission profiles of the callsigns PETRO 41 to 44 proves that RF emissions can serve as a tracking vector.

As the U.S. evaluates the vulnerability of its stealth fleets and adapts its communication protocols, the parameters of air power are changing. The transition to algorithmic warfare suggests that future conflicts will be defined by the ability to hide digital signatures, as physical concealment is no longer sufficient to guarantee operational surprise.
