# Bazan Hit Again: Fire Rages at Haifa Refinery

For the second time in ten days, a precision Iranian ballistic missile has successfully targeted the Bazan Oil Refinery in Haifa. The strike hit a primary distillation unit and ruptured a fuel storage silo containing 3,000 cubic meters of gasoline, triggering a massive, uncontrolled fire. The incident has intensified the domestic debate in Israel regarding the delay in evacuating the petrochemical plants, while President Donald Trump's immediate rhetorical intervention highlights a strategic effort to protect U.S. corporate assets in the Gulf.

## The Second Strike: Bazan Refinery Targeted

The strike on the Bazan (بازان) refinery was executed last night, marking the second time the facility has been hit within a two-week window.

The missile bypassed regional air defense arrays, striking the roof of an active distillation column. Unlike previous strikes where damage was superficial, the impact ruptured adjacent fuel pipelines, causing flammable liquids to ignite and spreading the fire across the central processing pad.

---

## The Gasoline Fire: 3,000 Cubic Meters Ablaze

The primary source of the fire is a storage silo containing 3,000 cubic meters of active gasoline:
*   **The Detonation**: Shrapnel from the missile's cluster warhead punctured the silo's steel hull, igniting the pressurized fuel.
*   **The Fire**: The resulting blaze sent black smoke over the Haifa bay area, visible from surrounding residential sectors.
*   **The Hazard**: Firefighters have been unable to extinguish the core of the fire, focusing their efforts on cooling adjacent fuel tanks to prevent a chain-reaction explosion.

---

## Pinpoint Targeting: Sparing the Volatile Tanks

Military analysts note that the strike demonstrated a high degree of guidance precision. The missile struck a specific fuel tank that was surrounded by:
*   **Eight active storage tanks** housing volatile petrochemical compounds.
*   **Critical distillation towers** handling pressurized gases.

Had the missile struck any of the adjacent units, the resulting explosion would have destroyed the entire industrial zone. The deliberate selection of the gasoline silo indicates that Tehran is demonstrating its capability to execute surgical strikes on economic targets without triggering a catastrophic industrial disaster.

---

## Green Course Condemns Hochman's "Reckless Delay"

The strike has triggered political backlash within Israel. Elad Hochman, Director General of the environmental organization *Green Course* (منظمة الاتجاه الأخضر), issued a statement condemning the government:
*   **The Criticism**: Hochman targeted Prime Minister Netanyahu and Energy Minister Eli Cohen for delaying the evacuation of the petrochemical plants from the Haifa bay area.
*   **The Postponement**: The government recently pushed back the final evacuation date to 2031, a move Hochman labeled as "reckless and negligent."
*   **The Demand**: Hochman demanded an immediate halt to all refining operations in Haifa, calling for the distribution of fuel reserves to decentralized, secure underground facilities.

---

## Ne'ot Hovav Update: Adama Strike Damage Exposed

The strike in Haifa occurred alongside new disclosures regarding the previous missile attack on the Adama chemical plant in Ne'ot Hovav:
*   **Residential damage**: Reports by *Calcalist* confirmed that the blast wave damaged over 100 nearby housing units in the Beer Sheva sector.
*   **Hazardous leak**: The strike caused a verified leak of toxic chemical agents, forcing the closure of Route 40 and surrounding transit links.

These details confirm that the strikes on Israel's chemical and refining infrastructure are having a direct impact on surrounding civilian sectors.

---

## Trump's Retaliation Threat in the New York Post

Immediately following the Haifa strike, President Trump took ownership of the rhetorical response:
*   **The Statement**: Speaking to the *New York Post*, Trump declared: \"The response is coming, and you will see it very soon.\"
*   **Sidelining the Cabinet**: Trump's statement bypassed the Israeli cabinet, with Netanyahu and his ministers remaining silent regarding the details of the response.
*   ** Retaliation Target**: U.S. advisors confirm that the Pentagon is drafting plans to target Iran's Kharg Island oil terminal in response to the Haifa blast.

---

## The Retaliation Reframing: U.S. Retaliation vs. Gulf Shield

Trump's decision to lead the response is a calculated move to reframe the conflict. By portraying any future strike on Iran as a U.S.-led operation, the administration is attempting to:
*   **Deter Gulf strikes**: Signal to Tehran that U.S. forces will respond directly to any strikes on allied infrastructure.
*   **Control the escalation**: Keep the exchange within a managed envelope, preventing unilateral Israeli actions that could draw the entire region into a war.

---

## Protecting U.S. Corporate Assets in the Gulf

The primary driver of Trump's intervention is the protection of U.S. commercial and corporate interests in the Gulf.

U.S. enterprises have invested billions of dollars in the infrastructure of the UAE, Kuwait, and Bahrain. By taking control of the retaliation narrative, Trump is attempting to signal to Iran that U.S. assets in the Gulf must remain outside the targeting envelope. The strategy seeks to confine the war to U.S.-Israeli strikes on the Iranian mainland, shielding U.S. corporate interests from direct cost.

---

## Conclusion: The Cost of the Reframing Strategy

The second strike on the Bazan Refinery and Trump's intervention highlight the strategic compromise at the heart of the U.S. campaign.

While Trump attempts to protect U.S. corporate interests in the Gulf by taking ownership of the retaliation, the strategy effectively leaves Israel to bear the physical cost of the war. By delaying the evacuation of Haifa's petrochemical plants, the Israeli cabinet has left its own citizens exposed to precision strikes. If the U.S. executes its threat to strike Kharg Island, it must accept that the response may not be confined to Israel, but will target the Gulf assets Trump is trying to protect, ending the security shield that has protected the region's energy resources.
