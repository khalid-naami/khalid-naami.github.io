# Somaliland Proposes US Military Move to Berbera

The breakaway East African republic of Somaliland has issued a public proposal to the United States, offering to host U.S. air and naval assets currently stationed in the Persian Gulf. Brokered by Israel, the proposal suggests transferring major assets, including the Al Udeid Air Base and the U.S. Navy Fifth Fleet, to the port city of Berbera. The development highlights a shifting geopolitical alignment that seeks to bypass host nation constraints in the Gulf and counter Chinese influence in the Horn of Africa.

## The Proposal: Somaliland Offers Berbera to the U.S.

The Republic of Somaliland, which declared independence from Somalia in 1991 but remains unrecognized by most of the international community, has officially invited Washington to establish a military presence in its territory.

The proposal offers the port of Berbera as a new home for U.S. regional forces. Specifically, Somaliland suggests relocating the Al Udeid Air Base, currently in Qatar, and the U.S. Navy Fifth Fleet, currently in Bahrain. In exchange, Somaliland seeks official U.S. and international diplomatic recognition, alongside a formal strategic security partnership.

---

## Access to Critical Minerals and Off-Shore Energy Blocks

To sweeten the deal, Somaliland has offered the U.S. exclusive economic concessions. According to documents attached to the official proposal, Washington would receive exclusive mining rights to Somaliland's untapped natural resources, including lithium, gold, copper, tin, and gypsum.

Further, the proposal grants exclusive exploration and drilling rights in 24 offshore and onshore blocks. These blocks hold estimated oil and gas reserves of between 1 and 5 billion barrels. Somaliland has indicated that these assets are ready for development in partnership with Western energy companies, including Genel Energy and RAK Gas.

---

## Israel’s Diplomatic Mediation and Geopolitical Ambitions

The diplomatic initiative is heavily backed by Israel, which remains the only state to have formally recognized Somaliland's independence. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has encouraged the U.S. administration to accept the offer, seeking to establish a non-Arab ally near the Red Sea.

Israeli planners view the relocation of U.S. bases as a method to achieve two goals: removing U.S. forces from vulnerable positions in the Gulf, and drawing the U.S. into a direct confrontation with Iran. By promoting Somaliland as a secure alternative, Israel aims to facilitate a broader strategic realignment in the region.

---

## The Relocation Calculus: Dismantling Gulf Base Dependency

The proposal aligns with discussions in Washington regarding the future of U.S. bases in the GCC. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and National Security Advisor Michael Waltz have reportedly noted that U.S. forces in the Gulf face high vulnerabilities due to local political pressure and proximity to Iranian strike networks.

Since the U.S. does not pay direct lease fees for several of its Gulf installations, planners are less constrained by real estate investments. Relocating to Berbera would allow the U.S. military to operate with greater independence, free from the host nation restrictions that limit operations launched from Qatar or Bahrain.

---

## The Horn of Africa: Confronting China’s Presence in Djibouti

A primary strategic advantage of the Berbera proposal is its proximity to neighboring Djibouti. Djibouti hosts China's first overseas military base, establishing a key node for Beijing's maritime security network in the Indian Ocean.

By establishing a major air and naval hub in Berbera, the U.S. military would place itself directly adjacent to the Chinese facility. This footprint would allow the U.S. to integrate operations across Central Command (CENTCOM) and Africa Command (AFRICOM), using partners like Egypt to counter Chinese economic and military influence in East Africa.

---

## Regional Backlash: The Fallout in Saudi Arabia and the UAE

The proposed Israel-Somaliland axis has caused significant concern in Riyadh and Abu Dhabi. Saudi Arabia views the presence of an Israeli-backed state on the Horn of Africa as a threat to its southern security, prompting it to alter its strategy in Yemen.

Riyadh actively worked to disrupt the UAE-brokered federal projects in Yemen, which sought to establish control over Bab el-Mandeb. The collapse of these projects, combined with the strategic retreat of the UAE model (which sought to act as the \"Switzerland of the Arabs\" while intervening in regional conflicts), has left local states exposed to retaliatory pressure from both Iran and Western lobbies.

---

## Conclusion: A New Security Architecture Beyond the Arab World

The Somaliland proposal highlights the changing priorities of U.S. and Israeli strategic planning. By offering Berbera as an alternative to Gulf bases, Somaliland has provided the U.S. with a method to bypass local political constraints while maintaining surveillance over Bab el-Mandeb and the Red Sea.

As the U.S. administration evaluates the economic and mineral concessions, the future of the Gulf bases remains uncertain. If the relocation proceeds, it will mark the end of the post-Gulf War security architecture, shifting the focus of U.S. power projection to the Horn of Africa and creating a direct line of confrontation with both regional forces and Chinese global influence.
