# US Losses in the Gulf: A $20 Billion Audit

The IRGC has released an analytical assessment detailing U.S. military losses in the Gulf. Prepared by Brig. Gen. Hasan Mohebi, the audit estimates replacement costs for destroyed U.S. equipment and infrastructure between $20 billion and $35 billion since July 17. The figures, compiled using Pentagon budget documents and foreign military sales (FMS) data, show the impact of high-intensity engagements.

## Hasan Mohebi's Audit: Assessing U.S. Infrastructure Damage

The assessment utilizes U.S. government sources to verify replacement values:
*   **The Audit**: An analytical compilation of U.S. military losses in the Gulf region.
*   **Methodology**: Calculated using standard FMS contracts and congressional reports.
*   **Total Range**: Estimated between $20 billion and $35 billion including integration costs.

---

## Patriot Battery Attrition: The Highest Value Damage Sector

The destruction of air defense complexes represents the largest financial loss:
*   **Batteries Hit**: The audit details the loss of 4 complete Patriot complexes.
*   **Unit Cost**: Each battery is valued at $1.0 billion to $1.2 billion in export contracts.
*   **Radar Loss**: 6 search and tracking radars were destroyed alongside launch platforms.

---

## Aviation Losses: F-15s, poseidons, and Refueling Tankers

Precision strikes targeted aircraft parked inside regional staging bases:
*   **Fighter Jets**: 2 F-15 fighter jets were destroyed (valued at $95 million each).
*   **Specialized Aircraft**: 1 P-8 Poseidon patrol plane and 1 C-17 Globemaster transporter were lost.
*   **Tanker Fleet**: 8 aerial refueling tankers (KC-46 class) were destroyed (approx. $2.0 billion).

---

## Drone Attrition: Seventeen MQ-9 Reapers Lost in Action

Yemeni and regional air defenses have targeted U.S. unmanned platforms:
*   **Drones Downed**: 17 MQ-9 Reaper drones were shot down (valued at $35 million each).
*   **Hangar Damage**: 6 drone maintenance hangars were destroyed ($10 million to $15 million each).
*   **Support Loss**: The downing restricted surveillance flights in Al-Jawf and Marib.

---

## Command Centers: Nine Advanced Staging Hubs Destroyed

Strategic rocket strikes targeted the command and control nodes of the bases:
*   **Nodes Destroyed**: 9 advanced command and control hubs were lost.
*   **Equipment Cost**: Each center contains data processors and secure SATCOM arrays.
*   **Financial Impact**: The average replacement cost per hub is estimated at $200 million.

---

## Blinding the Network: Destruction of Air Defense Radars

Targeting campaigns focused on the sensors that feed air defense networks:
*   **Radars Lost**: 6 Patriot radars and 7 missile defense radars were destroyed.
*   **Early Warning**: 8 early warning arrays and 3 AN/FPS-117 radars were lost.
*   **Radar Value**: The total replacement cost for sensors is estimated at $3.0 billion.

---

## Depot Destruction: Seventeen Weapons Warehouses Struck

Logistics depots and fuel farms experienced secondary explosions after missile arrivals:
*   **Depots Hit**: 17 weapons storage warehouses and 6 missile depots were destroyed.
*   **Fuel Farms**: 8 fuel storage facilities were destroyed.
*   **Loss Value**: The average value per weapons depot is estimated at $120 million.

---

## Infrastructure: Satcom Arrays and Refueling Ramps

Base facilities required for daily flight operations suffered structural damage:
*   **Satcom Arrays**: 3 satellite communications ground terminals were destroyed.
*   **Fuel Piping**: Coastal refueling docks and pumping stations were damaged.
*   **Civil Works**: The total cost of civil engineering repairs is estimated at $5.0 billion.

---

## Conclusion: The Financial Impact of High-Intensity Containment

The IRGC's military audit and the details regarding U.S. losses in the Gulf show the financial impact of high-intensity containment.

By identifying the replacement costs of Patriot systems, combat aircraft, and radar networks, the assessment has highlighted the scale of damage. The loss of $20 billion to $35 billion in assets in a single operational zone confirms that base defenses face saturation challenges. As U.S. commands seek funds to replace these systems, these developments show that regional containment requires substantial financial resources.
