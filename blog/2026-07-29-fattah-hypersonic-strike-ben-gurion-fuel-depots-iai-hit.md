# Fattah Strike: Ben Gurion Fuel Depots and IAI Hit

Iran's Wave 55 missile campaign has struck critical targets in central Israel, utilizing advanced hypersonic systems to bypass air defense networks. According to reports from the IRGC and Israeli media, heavy precision-guided **Fattah (فتاح)** hypersonic missiles hit the aviation fuel storage facility at Ben Gurion Airport and the core production plant of state-owned Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI). The strikes represent a coordinated effort to disrupt Israel's long-range flight operations and aerospace manufacturing.

## Wave 55: Deploying Heavy Fattah Hypersonic Missiles

The IRGC's Wave 55 campaign utilized a heavy, precision-guided variant of the Fattah hypersonic missile. Unlike lighter baseline models, this heavy variant is designed to carry a larger high-explosive payload while executing complex, low-altitude maneuvering profiles.

The heavy Fattah missile enters the terminal phase of flight at velocities exceeding Mach 15. Its ability to perform lateral maneuvers during atmospheric reentry makes it difficult for automated tracking systems to compute an interception path, ensuring the warhead reaches its coordinates.

---

## Refueling Infrastructure Struck at Ben Gurion Airport

The primary target of the hypersonic strike was the aviation fuel depot at Ben Gurion Airport. This facility is the central storage and pumping hub for the specialized fuel required by Israel's strategic aerial refueling tankers.

By targeting the fuel infrastructure rather than the runways, the IRGC aimed to disable the support assets that enable long-range operations. The strike succeeded in damaging the main pumping terminals and storage tanks, disrupting fuel supply lines within the military sector of the airport.

---

## Strategic Impact: Degrading Israel’s Aerial Tanker Fleet

The targeting of fuel depots is a calculated effort to ground Israel's aerial refueling fleet. Without persistent aerial refueling capabilities, Israeli fighter jets cannot sustain long-range sorties over distant targets like Iran.

This logistic disruption limits Israel's offensive range, forcing its air force to operate within regional parameters unless refueling can be secured from U.S. carrier fleets. The strike highlights the vulnerability of the support infrastructure that sustains modern air power.

---

## The Leak: Channel 12 Reports Fuel Contamination Fears

Despite strict military censorship, Israeli Channel 12 reported growing anxiety over a major fuel leak in the vicinity of Ben Gurion Airport. The damage to the storage tanks resulted in thousands of liters of aviation fuel escaping into surrounding soil and civil infrastructure.

Emergency teams were deployed to contain the leak and prevent a secondary fire. The public admission of a fuel leak by local media confirms that the strike bypassed the airport's terminal defenses, causing real damage to vital infrastructure.

---

## Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI) Plant Hit

The second hypersonic missile targeted the main production facility of state-owned Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI) in central Israel. IAI is the crown jewel of Israel's defense sector, responsible for manufacturing the Arrow missile defense systems and advanced satellite networks.

Israeli media acknowledged that a missile struck "near a highly sensitive security facility in central Israel." While official reports tried to downplay the impact by claiming it landed at the entrance, Iranian news agency *Tasnim* and IRGC sources confirmed the warhead penetrated the core assembly hangars, causing infrastructure damage to the assembly lines.

---

## Defensive Saturation: Bypassing Arrow-3 with Emad Decoys

To ensure the Fattah missiles penetrated Israel's multi-layered air defense network, the IRGC utilized saturation tactics. The first waves of the strike package consisted of older liquid-fueled **Emad (عماد)** and **Ghadr (قدر)** missiles.

These older missiles acted as decoys, forcing the Arrow-3 (Hetz-3) interceptor batteries to engage and exhaust their ready-to-fire missiles. Once the defensive shield was saturated and reloading delays were triggered, the high-velocity Fattah missiles were launched to strike their targets without facing interceptors.

---

## Conclusion: Vulnerabilities in Central Israel’s Staging Facilities

The strikes on the Ben Gurion fuel depots and the IAI aerospace facility demonstrate the vulnerability of central Israel's key defense installations. The use of heavy Fattah hypersonic missiles, supported by decoy salvos, has shown that static defensive shields cannot guarantee absolute protection.

As Israel manages the fuel leak at its primary airport and assesses the damage to its aerospace manufacturing, the strategic balance continues to shift. The ability of regional forces to systematically target the logistics of the air campaign suggests that future operations will face significant supply and equipment constraints, altering the duration and scope of the conflict.
