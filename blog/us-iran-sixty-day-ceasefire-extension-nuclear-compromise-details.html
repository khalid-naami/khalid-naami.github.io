Leaked US-Iran 60-Day Ceasefire Proposal

July 31, 2026 · 4 min read
Khalid Naami
Founder, Owner, and CEO at Dashboard Options

Leaked details of a proposed 60-day ceasefire extension between the U.S. and Iran have emerged following mediation by Pakistan. The draft agreement outlines compromises regarding Iran's enriched uranium stockpile and the management of the Strait of Hormuz. While Donald Trump has indicated the parties are close to an agreement, Israeli officials have opposed the interim terms, warning they do not eliminate Iran's nuclear capabilities.

Ceasefire Extension: The Proposed Sixty-Day Stabilization Window​

The draft proposal focuses on establishing a temporary pause in active operations:

  • The Truce: A 60-day extension of the active ceasefire has been proposed to facilitate talks.
  • Purpose: The window is intended to allow technical teams to draft detailed protocols.
  • Hajj Period: The pause would cover the upcoming Hajj season, preventing escalations during regional events.

High-Level Consultations: Trump's Regional Phone Diplomacy​

The U.S. President has coordinated with regional leaders to discuss the draft terms:

  • Phone Diplomacy: Donald Trump held scheduled calls with leaders of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, and Turkey.
  • Consensus Building: The calls were intended to secure regional support for the ceasefire terms.
  • Gulf Role: Gulf states have supported the extension to avoid disruptions to commercial shipping.

The White House Meeting: Reviewing the Draft in Washington​

The administration has convened its advisory team to evaluate the final details of the text:

  • The Meeting: Trump met with advisors Jared Kushner, Steve Witkoff, and Vice President JD Vance.
  • Axios Interview: Trump confirmed to Axios that he had read the draft, stating the parties are close to an agreement.
  • Vance's Stance: The Vice President favored reviewing the draft but warned against long-term compromises without nuclear concessions.

Split Stockpile Compromise: Dilution and Staggered Exports​

The draft address the enriched uranium inventory through a split-management protocol:

  • Uranium Splitting: Iran will retain its 20% enriched uranium but compromise on the 60% stockpile.
  • Domestic Dilution: A portion of the 60% stockpile will be diluted to lower levels under IAEA supervision.
  • Staggered Exports: The remaining portion will be exported in small, verified batches to third countries.

The Ten-Year Cap: Enrichment Restrictions to Three Percent​

The agreement imposes limits on Iran's future enrichment activities:

  • Enrichment Cap: Iran will suspend uranium enrichment above 3.67% for a period of ten years.
  • Verification: The IAEA will maintain continuous monitoring at the Fordow and Natanz facilities.
  • Right to Enrich: The treaty recognizes Iran's right to maintain low-level enrichment for peaceful use.

Sanctions Relief Schedule: Phased Lifting of Secondary Restrictions​

Financial concessions are linked to Iranian compliance with the nuclear terms:

  • Sanctions Relief: U.S. secondary sanctions targeting banking and oil exports will be lifted in phases.
  • Asset Release: Iran's frozen assets, estimated at $133 billion, will be released through banking channels.
  • Intermediate Funds: Initial funds will be routed through third countries to ensure compliance.

Reopening Hormuz: Suspending Shipping Fees During the Truce​

Commercial transit terms are defined for the duration of the 60-day pause:

  • Hormuz Transit: Iran will temporarily suspend transit fees on merchant vessels in the Strait.
  • Reopening Corridor: Maritime routes will be reopened to allow shipping volumes to return to normal levels.
  • Sovereignty Rights: Tehran retains its right to manage security under the 1982 Law of the Sea.

Israeli Opposition: Tel Aviv Rejects the Interim Compromise​

Israel's security cabinet has opposed the terms of the draft:

  • Opposition: Israeli officials warned that a temporary cap allows Iran to retain its enrichment infrastructure.
  • Truce Backlash: Tel Aviv argues the 60-day pause will be used by adversary groups to rebuild assets.
  • Target Demands: The government continues to advocate for direct operations targeting underground facilities.

Conclusion: The Operational Limitations of the Sixty-Day Truce​

The leaked details of the U.S.-Iran 60-day ceasefire proposal show the compromises required to establish a temporary truce.

By proposing a split stockpile compromise and a ten-year enrichment cap, the draft seeks to balance security concerns with sovereignty demands. Donald Trump's consultations with regional leaders show the diplomatic efforts to secure the extension. As Israel opposes the compromise, the draft confirms that the upcoming 60-day window will be a key period for Middle East diplomacy.
